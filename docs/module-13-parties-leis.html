01 · The word that survives every product

Buyer, Seller, Counterparty, Broker, Dealer — the Roles, Precisely

Module 1 §3 already established Dealer (Principal) vs Broker (Agent). This section restates the full role set in one place.

RoleMeaning
BuyerWhichever party is acquiring the instrument/exposure on a specific trade — direction-specific
SellerThe party disposing of the instrument/exposure — the mirror of Buyer
CounterpartyThe generic term for "the other side of the trade," regardless of direction
Broker (Agent)Executes on behalf of a client without taking the position onto its own book — Module 1 §3b
Dealer (Principal)Takes the position onto its own book, becoming the client's actual Counterparty — Module 1 §3a
Diagram · Why "Counterparty" Survives Every ContextBuyer/Seller breaks down for swaps
Coca-Cola trade (Module 4 §4a):
  Maple Leaf Pension Fund is the BUYER.
  Goldman Sachs is the SELLER.
  -> From Maple Leaf Pension Fund's perspective, Goldman Sachs
     is simply "the Counterparty" -- the label that doesn't
     depend on which direction the trade ran.

5Y CAD IRS (Module 8 §4a):
  Bank Of Toronto pays fixed, Goldman Sachs pays floating --
  there's no "buyer" or "seller" of a swap in the equity sense
  at all, yet BOTH parties are unambiguously each other's
  COUNTERPARTY. This is exactly why Module 11's entire risk
  framework (Current Exposure, Net Exposure, CVA) is built
  around "Counterparty," never "Buyer" or "Seller" -- those
  labels don't even apply to every product in this roadmap.
Takeaway. Buyer/Seller is a Cash-instrument-shaped pair of labels that breaks down for products like swaps that have no natural buy/sell direction; Counterparty is the one term general enough to describe every relationship in every module of this roadmap, which is exactly why Module 11's risk vocabulary standardized on it.

02 · One ledger, several service layers

Market Infrastructure Roles — CCP, Custodian, CSD & Settlement Agent

Four roles this roadmap has used constantly, often without pausing to distinguish them precisely. They frequently sit at the same institution in practice, but are logically distinct functions.

RoleWhat it actually doesAlready seen in
CCPBecomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer post-trade, absorbing counterparty riskNSCC, LCH SwapClear, ICE Clear Credit, CME Clearing, OCC
CSDThe single central ledger recording legal ownership of a security — DvP happens hereDTC, CDS/CDSX (Module 1 §7)
CustodianHolds securities and cash on behalf of an institutional owner, and may hold posted collateralModules 4-12
Settlement AgentExecutes the actual DvP instruction — moving securities and cash on settlement dateModule 1 §7's Fedwire example
Tri-Party AgentSpecialized custodian-like role for automated collateral selection, valuation, substitutionEuroclear, Clearstream (Module 12 §4)
Diagram · Why These Roles Blur, But Aren't the SameOne institution, several relationships
A single large custodian bank might simultaneously:
  - hold Maple Leaf Pension Fund's Coca-Cola shares as CUSTODIAN
  - act as SETTLEMENT AGENT, instructing DTC (the CSD) to move
    those shares on T+1
  - separately act as TRI-PARTY AGENT for a totally different
    client's derivatives collateral

-> One institution, three DIFFERENT ROLES on three different
   relationships. A reporting system that has a single
   "Custodian" field covering all three blurs together data that
   actually serves three separate operational purposes.
Takeaway. The CSD is the one true central ledger in this list — everything else is a service layer sitting on top of, or feeding instructions into, that single ledger. This is why a real operational trade record still needs separate Custodian and Settlement Agent fields to reflect who's actually doing the work.

03 · Not everyone can access a CCP directly

Clearing Member — Direct Access vs Client Clearing

A Clearing Member is a firm that IS a direct member of a CCP; everyone else accesses clearing indirectly, through a Clearing Member, in a relationship called client clearing.

Diagram · Direct vs Client ClearingBank Of Toronto as Maple Leaf's Clearing Member
Goldman Sachs: large enough to be a DIRECT Clearing Member of
  LCH SwapClear -- posts its own Initial Margin (Module 12 §2)
  directly to the CCP, faces the CCP's default waterfall
  directly (Module 11 §5)

Maple Leaf Pension Fund: NOT a direct Clearing Member -- too
  small, doesn't meet LCH SwapClear's membership/capital
  requirements. To get a centrally-cleared IRS anyway, it uses
  Bank Of Toronto as its CLEARING MEMBER (a "client clearing"
  relationship):

  Maple Leaf Pension Fund  --trades with-->  Bank Of Toronto
                                                   |
                                      (submits trade to CCP,
                                       GUARANTEES performance)
                                                   v
                                          LCH SwapClear (CCP)

-> Bank Of Toronto stands between Maple Leaf Pension Fund and
   the CCP -- if Maple Leaf Pension Fund fails, Bank Of Toronto
   (the Clearing Member) is on the hook to the CCP, not the
   other way around. This is a DIFFERENT counterparty risk
   relationship than the bilateral CVA math of Module 11 §4.
IT/reporting angle: a client-cleared trade record needs a field distinguishing "Clearing Member" from "Counterparty" — Bank Of Toronto is Maple Leaf Pension Fund's Clearing Member, but economically Maple Leaf Pension Fund's real counterparty risk, post-novation, runs to the CCP, with Bank Of Toronto as an intermediary guarantor, not the ultimate risk-bearer the way Module 11's bilateral CVA framework assumed.
Takeaway. Client clearing is the reason a firm can access all of Module 11 §5's CCP risk-mutualization benefits without itself being large enough to be a direct member — but it introduces a new, genuinely separate counterparty relationship sitting alongside the CCP relationship, not replacing it.

04 · "Who" turns out to have seven sub-questions

LEI Deep Dive — One Trade, Many Roles

Module 4 §6 first introduced LEI as identifying "who," not "what." This roadmap has since shown LEI playing genuinely different roles across different modules.

LEI RoleWhat It IdentifiesFirst Seen
Buyer / Seller LEIThe two economic counterparties to the tradeModule 4 §6
Counterparty LEIGeneric label for either side, used when Buyer/Seller doesn't applyModule 9 §6
Reference Entity LEIThe company whose default risk a CDS references — NOT a trading partyModule 9 §6
Clearing Member LEIThe firm submitting a client's trade to a CCP (Section 03)New to this module
CCP LEIThe CCP itself — CCPs have their own LEIs, since they're legal entities tooNew to this module
Reporting Entity LEIWhichever party is legally responsible for submitting the report (Section 06)New to this module
Custodian LEIThe institution holding securities/collateral on a party's behalfModule 12 §4
Takeaway. LEI answering "who" turns out to have at least seven distinct sub-questions once every role this roadmap has introduced is laid out side by side — "who's trading," "who's the subject," "who's clearing," "who's reporting," and "who's custodying" are five genuinely different questions that all happen to be answered with the same 20-character identifier format.

05 · Seven entities, seven roles, one trade

Worked Example — Full Party Map on One Trade

The concrete payoff of every "who" question this roadmap has asked since Module 4 §6.

5Y CAD IRS · Client-ClearedFull party map
Maple Leaf Pension Fund wants a 5Y CAD IRS, pays fixed 4.00%,
Notional CAD 50,000,000 -- economically identical structure to
Module 8 §4a's swap, different notional and buyer

EXECUTION: Maple Leaf Pension Fund trades bilaterally against
  Goldman Sachs (dealer, Module 1 §3a)

CLEARING: submitted to LCH SwapClear for novation (Module 3 §6)
  - Goldman Sachs: DIRECT Clearing Member, submits its own side
  - Maple Leaf Pension Fund: uses Bank Of Toronto as its
    CLEARING MEMBER (Section 03), which submits Maple Leaf's
    side and guarantees its performance to the CCP

COLLATERAL: Maple Leaf Pension Fund's Initial Margin (Module 12
  §2) is held via a TRI-PARTY AGENT (Euroclear, Module 12 §4)

ONE TRADE RECORD, SEVEN LEI FIELDS:
  Buyer LEI              : Maple Leaf Pension Fund
  Seller LEI              : Goldman Sachs
  Clearing Member LEI     : Bank Of Toronto (for Maple Leaf's side)
  CCP LEI                 : LCH SwapClear
  Reporting Entity LEI    : Bank Of Toronto (delegated reporting
                             on Maple Leaf's behalf -- Section 06)
  Custodian/Tri-Party LEI : Euroclear
  (Counterparty LEI field would duplicate Buyer/Seller here,
   since this product DOES have a clear buy/sell direction on
   the fixed-vs-floating economics -- Section 01's nuance)

-> Seven distinct legal entities, seven distinct LEI roles, ONE
   trade. None of these seven entities appears twice in the
   same role, and none of these roles is optional to model
   correctly if this trade needs to be reconstructed later from
   its LEI fields alone.
Takeaway. This single worked example is the concrete payoff of every "who" question this roadmap has asked since Module 4 §6 — a production trade record genuinely needs this many distinct party-role fields, and collapsing any two of them into one generic "Counterparty" field loses real, legally-meaningful information about who actually did what.

06 · Not a fixed role — assigned per regime

Reporting Entity vs Counterparty — Who Actually Files the Report

Module 2 §7's OTC derivative reporting obligation doesn't automatically mean both trading parties independently submit identical reports.

Diagram · Dual-Sided vs Single-Sided ReportingEMIR vs Dodd-Frank
EMIR (EU/UK) — DUAL-SIDED: BOTH counterparties must report their
  side of the trade to a Trade Repository, though a smaller
  counterparty can DELEGATE its reporting obligation to the
  larger one (exactly Section 05's Bank Of Toronto reporting on
  Maple Leaf Pension Fund's behalf)

Dodd-Frank (US) — SINGLE-SIDED: only ONE party (determined by a
  defined hierarchy -- generally the more "regulated" or
  "swap dealer" party) reports the trade; the other party has no
  independent reporting obligation for that same trade at all

-> The SAME bilateral trade between a US dealer and an EU buy-
   side client can be REPORTED ONCE under Dodd-Frank's rules but
   TWICE (or once, if delegated) under EMIR's rules for the
   EU-domiciled leg -- "Reporting Entity" isn't a fixed role,
   it's assigned differently by every regulatory regime a trade
   happens to touch.
Takeaway. A reporting field showing "Reporting Entity: Bank Of Toronto" on a trade where Maple Leaf Pension Fund is the actual Buyer isn't an error — it's Section 05's delegated-reporting arrangement working exactly as EMIR permits, and Module 14 picks this distinction up directly when it covers what regulators actually receive.

07 · A stale copy is a genuine risk

IT/Reporting Angle — Reference Data, Golden Source & LEI Lifecycle

Every party/LEI field discussed so far is reference data, not trade data — it describes an entity that exists independently of any one trade.

Diagram · Reference Data vs Trade DataGolden Source
TRADE DATA (specific to THIS trade, changes every time):
  Notional, Price, Trade Date, Maturity Date

REFERENCE DATA (describes an ENTITY, reused across every trade
that entity is party to):
  Maple Leaf Pension Fund's LEI, registered address, credit
  rating -- the SAME values whether this is trade #1 or #10,000
  Bank Of Toronto references this year

-> A "Golden Source" is the single authoritative system a firm
   designates to own each reference data entity -- every other
   system (trade capture, risk, collateral, reporting) is
   supposed to LOOK UP that entity's LEI from the Golden Source,
   never store its own independently-typed copy that can drift
   out of sync.
LEI Lifecycle — a genuinely operational reality: an LEI must be renewed annually with the issuing Local Operating Unit (LOU), and a lapsed (unrenewed) LEI is flagged "LAPSED" in the GLEIF global database rather than being invalid outright. A counterparty with a lapsed LEI can still legally exist and even still trade in some jurisdictions, but MiFID II's "No LEI, No Trade" principle (Section 08) means an EU-regulated firm generally cannot execute a NEW reportable trade against a counterparty whose LEI has lapsed until it's renewed — making LEI lifecycle monitoring a genuine, ongoing operational control, not a one-time onboarding checkbox.
Takeaway. Every party role this module has named — Buyer, Clearing Member, Custodian, Reference Entity — ultimately resolves to a Golden-Source-managed LEI record whose own lifecycle (issued, renewed, lapsed) can independently affect whether a NEW trade against that entity is even legally executable, entirely separate from anything about the trade's own economics.

08 · A data-quality concept that's also a legal pre-condition

Regulatory Perspective — GLEIF/LOUs, "No LEI, No Trade" & CPMI-IOSCO

The one area in this roadmap where identifier hygiene is also a hard legal pre-condition for trading at all.

Regulation / BodyHow it touches Parties, LEIs & Market Infrastructure specifically
GLEIFOversees the global LEI system, accrediting the Local Operating Units (LOUs) — the entities that issue LEIs to firms in each country, and maintain the LAPSED/ACTIVE status Section 07 describes
MiFID II "No LEI, No Trade"The EU rule that an investment firm generally cannot execute a reportable trade for/against a counterparty lacking a valid, active LEI — turning LEI from a reporting nicety into a genuine trading pre-condition
CPMI-IOSCOThe joint global standard-setter for CCP and CSD risk-management standards (the "Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures") — sets the baseline every CCP in Section 02's table is expected to meet, regardless of which national regulator directly supervises it
EMIR / Dodd-Frank (Reporting Entity rules)Each regime independently defines its own Reporting Entity hierarchy — this is where the "dual-sided vs single-sided" distinction in Section 06 is actually codified into law, not just market convention
National CCP/CSD RegulatorsEach jurisdiction's markets regulator (FCA for LCH, the Bank of Canada/CIRO for CDCC, the Fed/CFTC/SEC jointly for US CCPs) directly licenses and supervises its own domestic CCPs/CSDs under the CPMI-IOSCO baseline
Takeaway. LEI regulation is the one area in this entire roadmap where a data-quality concept is ALSO a hard legal pre-condition for trading at all — "No LEI, No Trade" means Section 07's Golden Source and lifecycle-monitoring concerns aren't just good reference-data hygiene, they're the difference between a trade being executable and not.

Self-check · Solutions

Recap Quiz — Answers & Explanations

Check your work against these before moving on.

Q1 — The "Clearing Member LEI" field would disappear, replaced by Maple Leaf Pension Fund submitting directly to LCH SwapClear

Section 03's diagram shows exactly this alternative: Goldman Sachs, being large enough, skips the intermediary entirely and faces the CCP directly — becoming a direct Clearing Member is precisely the structural change that would eliminate the need for Bank Of Toronto's guarantor role in Section 05's party map.

Q2 — No, "Buyer" doesn't meaningfully apply to a plain IRS; Module 11 uses "Counterparty" because it's general enough for relationships with no buy/sell asymmetry

Section 01's takeaway states this directly: Buyer/Seller is Cash-instrument-shaped and breaks down for swaps; Counterparty is the one term general enough to describe every relationship in every module of this roadmap.

Q3 — Generally NO — MiFID II's "No LEI, No Trade" principle blocks new reportable trading activity against a lapsed LEI
Section 07-08 together establish this: a lapsed LEI doesn't invalidate the entity itself, but it does block new MiFID II-reportable trading activity specifically — existing positions aren't unwound, but new trade execution is where the rule bites.
Q4 — Yes, both regimes can independently require a report for the same trade, and they are NOT automatically the same report

Section 06's takeaway states this precisely: "Reporting Entity" isn't a fixed role, it's assigned differently by every regulatory regime a trade happens to touch — a cross-border trade doesn't get ONE report, it potentially gets as many reports as there are regimes with jurisdiction over either party.

Q5 — A large custodian bank can simultaneously be Custodian, Settlement Agent, AND Tri-Party Agent for different relationships

Section 02's diagram states this directly: "One institution, three DIFFERENT ROLES on three different relationships" — this doesn't make the roles the same function because each serves a logically distinct purpose, regardless of whether they happen to share a company name.

Q6 — Client clearing introduces a guarantor relationship (Bank Of Toronto to the CCP) rather than a pure bilateral CVA relationship

Section 03 states this explicitly: client clearing "introduces a new, genuinely separate counterparty relationship... sitting alongside the CCP relationship, not replacing it" — if Maple Leaf Pension Fund defaults, Bank Of Toronto is on the hook to the CCP for Maple Leaf's obligations, a fundamentally different risk than Module 11 §4's bilateral-uncleared-trade CVA scenario.

Q7 — Reference data can change independently of any trade, and a stale local copy has no mechanism to learn about that change

Section 07's takeaway makes this the central argument for Golden Source architecture: correctness on entry day doesn't guarantee correctness later, and LEI lifecycle events (renewal, lapse) are exactly the kind of change a stale, locally-copied reference data field will silently miss.
