01 · Two identifiers, two layers

What Are UPI and UTI? — Why Derivatives Needed New Identifiers

Cash securities have had a single global identifier for decades — the ISIN. OTC derivatives never had an equivalent, because a derivative has no existence independent of the two parties' contract, so there's no natural "one instrument, many owners" object to attach a single code to.

The 2008 financial crisis made that gap a serious regulatory problem — the G20's post-crisis OTC derivatives reforms required trade reporting, but reports from different firms used different internal product codes, making it effectively impossible for a regulator to aggregate "how much risk exists in 5Y CAD interest rate swaps across the whole market." Two identifiers were built to close that gap, at two different layers:

IdentifierLayerAnswers
UPI (Unique Product Identifier)Product"What KIND of derivative is this?" — one code per product template, reused across thousands of unrelated trades
UTI (Unique Transaction Identifier)Trade"WHICH specific transaction is this?" — one code per trade, generated fresh every time
Diagram · Where UPI and UTI SitRestated from Module 2 §12
ASSET CLASS -> PRODUCT -> [no instrument-level ID for OTC derivatives] -> TRADE
                    |                                                          |
                    v                                                          v
            UPI identifies                                        UTI identifies
            the PRODUCT TEMPLATE                                  ONE TRANSACTION
            (reused across trades)                                 (new every time)

Both are GLOBAL standards -- not firm-specific, not jurisdiction-
specific -- built specifically so a US regulator, an EU regulator,
and a UK regulator looking at the SAME cross-border trade all see
the SAME UPI and the SAME UTI on their respective reports.
Takeaway. UPI and UTI aren't two flavors of the same idea — they solve two genuinely different aggregation problems. UPI lets a regulator ask "how much of THIS PRODUCT exists market-wide" without reading every firm's contract terms; UTI lets a regulator (or a Trade Repository) ask "did both counterparties report the SAME TRADE consistently" — exactly the pairing/reconciliation mechanism Module 14 §3 already introduced for EMIR.

02 · Structure & who generates it

UTI Deep Dive — Structure, Generation Waterfall & Worked Examples

A UTI is a single alphanumeric code, up to 52 characters, structured as a 20-character LEI-based prefix (the generating entity) plus up to 32 additional characters the generator assigns as a unique suffix — from CPMI-IOSCO's 2017 Technical Guidance, the global standard every major jurisdiction converged on.

            this UTI)                    WHICH trade)

   the suffix length is up to the generator, not fixed at 32.

Who generates the UTI — the waterfall

Because a UTI must be identical on both counterparties' independently-submitted reports for a Trade Repository to pair them (Module 14 §3), regulators needed a rule for deciding WHICH party generates it and shares it with the other before either party's reporting deadline — the UTI generation waterfall.

PriorityRule (illustrative, simplified)Example
1If the trade is executed on a trading platform/venue that generates UTIs, the venue generates itAn exchange-like OTC platform assigns the UTI at execution
2If the trade is (or becomes) centrally cleared, the CCP generates the UTI for the cleared leg(s)Module 8's 5Y CAD IRS, novated to LCH SwapClear — LCH SwapClear generates the UTI once cleared
3Otherwise, a hierarchy among the counterparties applies (e.g. clearing member > swap dealer/major participant > financial counterparty > non-financial counterparty)Two dealers of the same seniority tier trading bilaterally
4If still tied, a simple tie-break (e.g. alphabetical LEI) decidesSee the worked example below
Callout — this is a simplified illustration, not the literal current rule text. The exact waterfall differs in small but legally material ways across EMIR, UK EMIR, Dodd-Frank/CFTC, and other regimes, and gets revised periodically. Confirm the current text against Section 8's official links before encoding it into a rules engine — the same hedge SFTR §7 and UK DMO §2 already apply to their own regulatory specifics.

Rule 2 (CCP-generated)?      NO  -- confirmed bilateral, no CCP involved

                                     standing in this illustration
Rule 4 (alphabetical LEI tie-break):
  Bank Of Toronto's LEI:  5493001KJTIIGC8Y1R12
  Goldman Sachs' LEI:     784F5XWPLTWKTBV3E584   (illustrative)

  "5493..." sorts BEFORE "784F..." alphanumerically
  -> BANK OF TORONTO generates the UTI, and must share it with
     Goldman Sachs before either party's own reporting deadline
     (Module 14 §3's dual-sided pairing depends on this)
Takeaway. The waterfall exists to remove ambiguity, not preference — neither counterparty "wants" to generate the UTI, but exactly one of them MUST, deterministically, so the other can simply receive and reuse the same value. Module 14 §3's UTI-mismatch pairing-break scenario is precisely what happens when this hand-off fails.

03 · One template per asset class

UPI Deep Dive — Structure, Reference Data & Worked Examples Across Asset Classes

A UPI is a 12-character alphanumeric code (formatted similarly to an ISIN, including a check digit) under ISO 4914. Like an ISIN, the code is just a lookup key — the substance lives in the UPI Reference Data record behind it, maintained by the DSB (Section 4).

Critical rule, restated from Module 2 §12: two trades generate the SAME UPI if and only if every product-defining field matches. Trade-specific details — notional amount, price/rate, counterparty, trade date — are deliberately excluded from what defines a UPI.

What's an "Underlier"? Every worked example below has an Underlier field — Module 1 §8 defines this general concept in full (it's the reference asset/rate/index/entity a derivative's cash flows are driven by, distinct from Notional, which is just the size), including what CORRA (the Interest Rate template's Underlier below) actually is and how it's calculated. Module 8 §1 has the full CDOR-to-CORRA transition story.

One UPI reference-data template per asset class

Different asset classes describe "the product" using different fields. Reusing trades already built elsewhere in this curriculum:

  Asset Class: Interest Rate | Instrument Type: Swap, Fixed-Float
  Underlier: CORRA | Notional Currency: CAD | Tenor: 5 Years

  Reference Entity: Northstar Resources Inc. | Seniority: Senior
  Unsecured | Notional Currency: USD | Tenor: 5 Years
  -> UPI: RT4M8LN2WD93 (illustrative)

FOREIGN EXCHANGE  (Module 6's 3M USD/CAD Forward)
  Asset Class: Foreign Exchange | Instrument Type: Forward, Deliverable
  Currency Pair: USD/CAD | Settlement Type: Physical
  -> UPI: FX7K1PXQ0H26 (illustrative)

EQUITY  (Module 2 §11's Total Return Swap on Apple Inc. shares)
  Asset Class: Equity | Instrument Type: Swap, Price Return
  Underlier: Apple Inc. common stock | Return Type: Price + Dividend
  -> UPI: EQ3T9YBV5C48 (illustrative)

COMMODITY  (Module 7's 1Y Brent Crude Oil Swap)
  Asset Class: Commodity | Instrument Type: Swap
  Underlier: ICE Brent | Sub-Asset: Energy, Oil | Notional Currency: USD
  -> UPI: CM6X2FGL8N71 (illustrative)
Takeaway. Notice the Credit template includes "Reference Entity" as a product-defining field — a CDS on Northstar Resources Inc. and an otherwise-identical CDS on a different company get DIFFERENT UPIs, even though both are "5Y Single-Name CDS." This is the opposite of the Interest Rate template, where the underlier is a shared rate benchmark (CORRA) — a reminder that "what counts as the same product" is asset-class-specific, defined field-by-field in each template the DSB maintains.

Requesting a UPI

A firm needing a UPI for a new product shape submits the reference-data fields to the DSB via its platform or API. The DSB checks whether an identical reference-data combination already has a UPI on file — if so, it returns the existing code (why thousands of unrelated trades correctly share one UPI, Section 1); if not, it issues a new one — the same lookup-or-create pattern an ISIN-issuing National Numbering Agency follows for a genuinely new security, just against a derivatives-specific schema.

04 · Infrastructure, not a regulator

ANNA-DSB — Who Runs This, Governance & How It Actually Works

ANNA (the Association of National Numbering Agencies) has coordinated ISIN issuance globally since the 1990s, working through each country's own National Numbering Agency. In 2017, ANNA established a dedicated subsidiary, the DSB (Derivatives Service Bureau Limited), specifically because OTC derivatives' near-infinite combination of underliers and terms didn't fit the National-Numbering-Agency-per-country model that works for cash securities.

Diagram · One Organization, Two Identifier OutputsANNA → DSB
ANNA (Association of National Numbering Agencies)
  -> coordinates ISIN issuance globally, via national agencies,
     for CASH securities (Module 2 §2)
     |
     v
DSB (Derivatives Service Bureau Limited) -- ANNA's subsidiary,
est. 2017, purpose-built for OTC DERIVATIVES:

  OUTPUT 1 -- ISIN-for-derivatives (since 2017/2018)
    Issued specifically for EU MiFID II/MiFIR pre- and post-trade
    TRANSPARENCY requirements (Section 5) -- an OTC derivative
    gets an ISIN-shaped code from the DSB, distinct from a cash
    security's ISIN, but structurally an ISIN

  OUTPUT 2 -- UPI (since 2022)
    Issued for GLOBAL TRADE REPORTING under the FSB's 2020
    mandate (implementing CPMI-IOSCO's Technical Guidance) --
    EMIR, UK EMIR, Dodd-Frank/CFTC, and other G20 jurisdictions'
    reporting regimes all consume this SAME UPI (Section 6)

Governance: the Financial Stability Board (FSB) designated the DSB as the sole global UPI Service Provider, following technical guidance jointly developed by CPMI (Committee on Payments and Market Infrastructures) and IOSCO — the same standard-setters Module 13 §8 already introduced for their broader role overseeing global market-infrastructure standards. The DSB operates on a not-for-profit, cost-recovery basis: UPI reference data lookups are free and publicly available (a deliberate transparency design choice), while firms submitting NEW product definitions may be subject to a contributory fee structure funding the DSB's operations.

Takeaway. The DSB isn't a regulator — it's market infrastructure, operating under a mandate from standard-setters (FSB/CPMI-IOSCO), the same way Module 1 and Module 13 §8 already distinguish infrastructure providers (CSDs, Trade Repositories) from the regulators that oversee them. Confusing "the DSB requires X" with "the FCA/CSA/SEC requires X" is a common category error.

05 · The most confusing fact about the DSB

UPI vs. the DSB's Other Output — ISINs for MiFIR

The exact same underlying reference-data record can produce two different identifier outputs, for two different regimes, serving two different purposes — an ISIN-shaped code for EU MiFIR transparency, and a UPI for global trade reporting.

Diagram · One Product, Two Regulatory OutputsMiFIR × EMIR
Bank Of Toronto's 5Y CAD IRS with Goldman Sachs (Module 8 §4a),
if EITHER counterparty is EU/UK MiFID-scope:

-> MiFIR pre/post-trade TRANSPARENCY (Module 14 §4's purpose --
   "did anyone behave abusively," restated for derivatives):
   the DSB issues an OTC-DERIVATIVE ISIN for this product, used
   on MiFIR transaction reports and public transparency data

-> EMIR TRADE REPORTING (Module 14 §3's purpose -- "how much
   systemic derivatives risk exists"):
   the SAME trade's product is ALSO assigned a UPI, used on the
   EMIR report submitted to the Trade Repository

-> TWO DIFFERENT DSB-ISSUED CODES, on TWO DIFFERENT REPORTS, for
   the IDENTICAL underlying product -- because MiFIR and EMIR are
   genuinely separate regimes (Module 14 §1's "one trade, five
   regimes" theme, restated at the identifier level)
Takeaway. A reporting engineer who assumes "the DSB gives one code per product" will build a system that silently drops one of two legitimately required identifiers — MiFIR-scope firms need BOTH the OTC-derivative ISIN and the UPI on the same underlying trade, from the same source organization, because Module 14 §1 already established that regulatory purposes don't collapse into one just because the source data does.

06 · Not every regime uses both

Where UPI/UTI Are Actually Used — Regime-by-Regime Map

Not every reporting regime this curriculum has covered uses UPI and UTI the same way — some use both, some use only UTI, and some use neither.

RegimeUses UTI?Uses UPI?Notes
EMIR / UK EMIRYes — pairing keyYes — mandatory since EMIR REFIT/UPI phase-inThe regime this module's Section 2/3 examples are built around
Dodd-Frank / CFTCYesYes — replacing CFTC's older internal product taxonomyUS swap data reporting to an SDR
SFTR / UK SFTRYes — same UTI concept, same pairing purposeNo — SFTs use SFTR's own "Type of SFT" codes (REPO, SBSC, SLEB, MGLD) insteadThe clean illustration that UTI and UPI are genuinely independent
MiFID II / MiFIRNot the primary key — keys off transaction reference number + OTC-derivative ISINUses the DSB's OTHER output — an OTC-derivative ISIN, not a UPI (Section 5)The regime most likely to be confused with UPI usage
UK DMO reportingNoNoNot an OTC derivative regime — gilts are cash securities (ISIN), DMO reporting is commercial/contractual
Takeaway. "Does this trade need a UPI?" and "does this trade need a UTI?" are two separate yes/no questions, exactly like Module 14 §1's "is this trade reportable?" was five separate yes/no questions against five regimes — SFTR proves a regime can need dual-sided pairing (UTI) without needing product-template aggregation (UPI).

Worked Example — One Trade's Full Identifier Stack, End to End

Pulling every identifier this module and Module 2 §12 have covered onto one trade record.

Full Identifier Stack5Y CAD IRS · Bank Of Toronto ↔ Goldman Sachs
(Module 8 §4a's trade, novated to LCH SwapClear per Module 8 §5)

Trade ID         : BOT-2026-000514        (Bank Of Toronto's own
                                            internal reference only)

UTI              : generated by LCH SwapClear once cleared (Section 2's
                   waterfall Rule 2 -- CCP-generated for cleared trades),
                   e.g. 549300LCHXXXXXXXXX2026081700099 (illustrative)

UPI              : QZ9F4K2XR7T1 (illustrative -- Section 3's Interest
                   Rate template: 5Y CAD Fixed-Float Swap vs CORRA)

Counterparty LEIs: Bank Of Toronto  5493001KJTIIGC8Y1R12
                   Goldman Sachs    784F5XWPLTWKTBV3E584 (illustrative)

OTC-derivative ISIN (Section 5, ONLY if MiFIR-scope):
                   a separate DSB-issued ISIN-shaped code, distinct
                   from the UPI, used solely on MiFIR reports

-> FIVE different identifiers on one trade, each answering a
   different question: Trade ID (internal), UTI (which specific
   transaction, for EMIR pairing), UPI (which product template,
   for EMIR aggregation), LEIs (who is trading), OTC-derivative
   ISIN (which product, for MiFIR transparency) -- restating
   Module 4 §6's "four completely different identifiers, four
   completely different questions" pattern, now with the
   derivative-specific layer fully populated
Takeaway. No single identifier on this record is redundant with another, even though several superficially "identify the product" — UPI and the OTC-derivative ISIN both describe the SAME product shape but exist for two regimes that don't share infrastructure (Section 5), while UTI and Trade ID both identify the SAME single transaction but at two different scopes (global/regulatory vs. internal/firm-specific).

Self-check · Solutions

Recap Quiz — Answers & Explanations

Check your work against these.

Q1 — A derivative has no existence independent of the two parties' contract, so there's no single persistent "instrument" object for an ISIN-style code to attach to

Section 1 traces this back to the same structural point Module 2 §12 made about why UPI has to sit at the product layer instead — one rung higher than where an ISIN sits for cash instruments.

Q2 — 5493001KJTIIGC8Y1R12 (first 20 chars) is the generating entity's LEI (Bank Of Toronto); 2026081700001 is the generator's own unique suffix identifying this one trade

Section 2's "UTI Anatomy" diagram states this exact split — the LEI-based prefix answers "who generated this UTI," the suffix answers "which trade."

Q3 — The trade is bilateral, so the waterfall falls through to Rule 4's alphabetical LEI tie-break, and Bank Of Toronto's LEI (5493...) sorts before Goldman Sachs' (784F...). If centrally cleared, Rule 2 would apply and the CCP itself would generate the UTI
Section 2's worked waterfall example walks through exactly this elimination, rule by rule, and notes the CCP-generation case already happens for the cleared IRS in Section 7.
Q4 — No, they would NOT share a UPI — the Credit template includes "Reference Entity" as a product-defining field

Section 3's takeaway states this explicitly: a CDS on a different company fails the "every product-defining field matches" test from the start, contrasted with the Interest Rate template where the underlier is a shared rate benchmark, not a specific company.

Q5 — No, the DSB is market infrastructure, not a regulator — the FSB designated it under CPMI-IOSCO guidance; individual jurisdictions' regulators (ESMA, CFTC, FCA) mandate its use

Section 4's takeaway draws the same infrastructure-vs-regulator distinction Module 1 and Module 13 §8 already established for CSDs and Trade Repositories.

Q6 — Two: a UPI (for EMIR trade reporting) and a separate OTC-derivative ISIN (for MiFIR transparency reporting), both from the same reference-data record
Section 5's diagram states this precisely — a reporting engineer who assumes one DSB code per product will silently under-report one of the two legitimately required identifiers.
Q7 — SFTR requires a UTI but NOT a UPI — SFTs use SFTR's own "Type of SFT" codes instead

Section 6's table flags SFTR as "the clean illustration that UTI and UPI are genuinely independent — a regime can need one without the other," cross-referencing SFTR §2's own SFT-type-code table.

Q8 — Because they exist for two structurally separate regimes (EMIR wants a UPI, MiFIR wants an OTC-derivative ISIN) that don't share reporting infrastructure

Section 7's takeaway restates Section 5's point in context: no identifier on the worked trade record is truly redundant, even the two that superficially describe "the same product," because each answers to a different regime's own reporting pipeline.
