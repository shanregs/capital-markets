01 · One phrase, five regimes

What Is Regulatory Reporting? — Five Obligations, Not One

"Regulatory reporting" gets used as one catch-all phrase, but this roadmap has actually touched five distinct reporting obligations, each answering a different regulatory question.

ObligationWhat Gets ReportedAnswersFirst Seen
Trade Reporting (generic term)Loosely used for any of the below—Throughout
Transaction Reporting (MiFIR)EVERY execution in scope — equities, bonds, listed AND OTC derivatives"Did market abuse happen?"Module 4 §8
Position ReportingSTANDING positions, periodically"Is any participant over-concentrated?"Module 7 §8
Derivatives Reporting (EMIR/Dodd-Frank)OTC derivative trade economics, to a Trade Repository"How much systemic OTC risk exists?"Module 2 §7
Securities Financing Reporting (SFTR)Repo, securities lending, financing transactions"How much collateral re-use/leverage exists?"Module 5 §8
Diagram · One Trade, Four Regimes, Four Different Answers5Y CAD IRS
Bank Of Toronto's 5Y CAD IRS (Module 8 §4a) with Goldman Sachs:

-> DERIVATIVES REPORTING (EMIR/Dodd-Frank): YES, reportable --
   it's an OTC derivative (Module 2 §07)

-> TRANSACTION REPORTING (MiFIR): potentially YES if either
   party is EU/UK-regulated and the swap is deemed
   MiFID-in-scope (Module 6 §08's boundary discussion, applied
   to Rates instead of FX)

-> POSITION REPORTING: only if Bank Of Toronto's cumulative
   position in a POSITION-LIMIT-SUBJECT product (Module 7 §08's
   commodity derivative limits) crosses a threshold -- NOT
   applicable to a plain CAD IRS

-> SECURITIES FINANCING REPORTING (SFTR): NO -- an IRS is a
   derivative, not a financing transaction (Module 5 §08's
   distinction, restated)

-> Same trade, evaluated against FOUR different reporting
   regimes, with genuinely different YES/NO/MAYBE answers for
   each -- exactly the kind of decision matrix a reporting
   system's rules engine has to encode correctly.
Takeaway. "Is this trade reportable?" is never a single yes/no question — it's five separate yes/no questions against five separate regimes, and a trade can legitimately be reportable under some and exempt under others simultaneously. Module 13 §6 already showed this same multiplicity for WHO reports; this section shows it for WHAT gets reported at all.
Deep dive available. Position Reporting and Derivatives/SFT Reporting run on genuinely different clocks — one periodic and concentration-focused, the others a daily valuation-update obligation for as long as a position stays open. For the full breakdown with worked EMIR and SFTR daily-valuation examples, see Module 26 — Position Deep Dive §11.

03 · Pairing and reconciliation at the regulator

EMIR Deep Dive — What an OTC Derivative Report Actually Contains

Module 2 §7 introduced EMIR's Trade Repository obligation; Module 13 §6 introduced dual-sided reporting. Here's what actually gets submitted, and a genuinely new concept: pairing and reconciliation at the Trade Repository itself.

Diagram · EMIR Report Field GroupsBank Of Toronto's 5Y CAD IRS
Counterparty data: Bank Of Toronto's LEI, Goldman Sachs' LEI
                    (Module 4 §6, Module 13 §4)
Common data:        UPI (product template), UTI (this specific
                    trade), Notional, Fixed Rate, Maturity
                    (Module 2 §12)
Valuation data:      MTM, valuation timestamp, Fair Value Level
                    (Module 10 §6)
Collateral data:     Collateral posted, CSA reference (Module 12)

PAIRING & RECONCILIATION (new): because EMIR is dual-sided
(Module 13 §6), BOTH Bank Of Toronto's and Goldman Sachs'
independently-submitted reports for this SAME trade land at the
Trade Repository. The Trade Repository automatically attempts to
PAIR them (matching on UTI) and RECONCILE the economic fields --
if Bank Of Toronto reports Notional CAD 100,000,000 and Goldman
Sachs reports CAD 100,500,000 for the same UTI, that's a
reconciliation BREAK, flagged back to both firms for correction.
Takeaway. EMIR's dual-sided design isn't just about redundancy — the Trade Repository's pairing/reconciliation process is itself a data-quality control, catching exactly the kind of Module 12 §7-style dispute at the regulatory level, not just the bilateral collateral-call level.

04 · Same infrastructure, different mission

MiFID II Transaction Reporting Deep Dive — A Different Regime, A Different Purpose

MiFID II Transaction Reporting (RTS 22) is frequently confused with EMIR, but the two regimes differ on almost every dimension that matters.

DimensionEMIRMiFID II Transaction Reporting
ScopeOTC derivatives onlyALL in-scope instruments — equities, bonds, listed AND OTC derivatives
DestinationTrade RepositoryNational Competent Authority directly
PurposeSystemic risk monitoringMarket abuse detection
Sides ReportingDual-sidedGenerally single-sided
Field Count (illustrative)~85 fields under EMIR REFIT~65 fields under RTS 22
DeadlineT+1T+1
Diagram · Same Equity Trade, Two Regimes, Zero Purpose OverlapCoca-Cola stock
Maple Leaf Pension Fund buys Coca-Cola stock via Bank Of Toronto
(Module 4 §4a):

EMIR: NOT APPLICABLE -- Coca-Cola stock is a cash security, not
      an OTC derivative (Module 4 §08's exact scoping rule)

MiFID II Transaction Reporting: APPLICABLE -- Bank Of Toronto
      reports the execution to its National Competent Authority,
      specifically so regulators can screen for suspicious
      trading patterns (e.g., trading ahead of undisclosed news)

-> A cash equity trade that NEVER touches EMIR still generates
   a full MiFID II transaction report -- proof that "regulatory
   reporting" genuinely means different things depending on
   which regime is asking.
Takeaway. EMIR asks "how much systemic derivatives risk exists in the market"; MiFID II Transaction Reporting asks "did anyone behave abusively on this specific execution" — two regimes that happen to share some infrastructure concepts but serve completely different regulatory missions.

05 · The same shape, a different question

SFTR Deep Dive — Completing the Repo Reporting Picture

Module 5 §8 first introduced SFTR; systems-landscape §12 walked through the repo worked example. Here's how that connects to this module's broader reporting framework.

SFTR ReportBank Of Toronto's overnight repo (systems-landscape §12)
Loan data:      EUR 50,000,000 cash lent, overnight, 3.15% rate
Collateral data: Germany 3% Bund 2033, Market Value, Haircut
                 (Module 12 §3's exact haircut mechanic, applied
                 to THIS specific collateral)
Counterparty:    Bank Of Toronto's LEI, Goldman Sachs' LEI

-> Notice SFTR's report shape genuinely mirrors EMIR's (Section
   03) -- counterparty data, economic terms, collateral data --
   but answers a DIFFERENT systemic question (Section 01's
   "how much collateral re-use and financing leverage exists,"
   not "how much derivatives risk exists").
Takeaway. SFTR is this roadmap's proof that the reporting-field PATTERN (who, what, valuation, collateral) is more universal than any single regime — the same shape of data serves EMIR's derivatives-risk question and SFTR's financing-leverage question equally well.

Full AnnotationEvery field, traced to its origin
Trade ID: IRS12345
  -> the human-readable trade identifier; the actual regulatory
     identifier reported under EMIR/Dodd-Frank is the UTI (Module
     2 §12), generated at execution, globally unique

Asset Class: Derivative / Product: Interest Rate Swap
  -> Module 1 §2's Asset Class -> Product hierarchy, refined by
     the ISDA Taxonomy (Module 2 §7) into the UPI that actually
     gets reported

Notional: CAD 100M
  -> Module 1 §8's foundational point: a REFERENCE amount for
     calculating cash flows, matching Module 8 §4a's exact IRS --
     this notional itself never settles

Party A: Bank Of Toronto / Party B: Goldman Sachs
  -> Module 13 §1's Counterparty terminology (not Buyer/Seller --
     a swap has no natural buy/sell direction), each identified
     by their own LEI (Module 4 §6, Module 13 §4) on the actual
     report, not just a name

Maturity: 5Y
  -> static economic term, Module 1 §8

MTM: +CAD 2.4M
  -> Module 10 §1's Mark-to-Market concept -- and specifically,
     this figure matches Module 11 §3's GROSS POSITIVE MTM across
     Bank Of Toronto's full netting set with Goldman Sachs (the
     IRS's own +1,500,000 plus the FX Forward's +900,000). This
     is a genuinely important real-world ambiguity: is "MTM" on
     this record describing Trade ID IRS12345 ALONE, or the whole
     NETTING SET? Module 11 §3's IT/reporting angle flagged
     exactly this -- a record needs an explicit Netting Set ID
     field to disambiguate, because in practice systems often DO
     show netting-set-level Exposure/Collateral fields attached to
     one representative Trade ID, exactly as this capstone record does

Exposure: CAD 2.0M
  -> Module 11 §3's NET Exposure after ISDA close-out netting --
     LOWER than MTM specifically because Trade 3 (the CDS, -CAD
     400,000) nets against the other two

Collateral: CAD 1.8M
  -> Module 12 §5's Required Collateral, driven by a CAD 200,000
     CSA Threshold -- NOT collateral posted "at random," a
     specific negotiated number

Custodian: XYZ
  -> Module 13 §2's Custodian role -- specifically Bank Of
     Toronto's OWN custodian, holding Bank Of Toronto's side of
     posted/received collateral (this record is Bank Of Toronto's
     own trade capture, so every field on it is Bank Of Toronto's
     view). Goldman Sachs would separately have its own custodian
     on its own books, not shown here. Logically distinct from the
     CCP (if this were centrally cleared) or the CSD (Module 13
     §2's full role table)

Settlement: ...
  -> Module 1 §7's DvP mechanics for the quarterly net cash flows
     (Module 8 §4a), or Module 3's full lifecycle if referring to
     the trade's own confirmation/clearing process

Reporting: ...
  -> Section 03 of THIS module -- an EMIR (or Dodd-Frank) report,
     dual-sided (Module 13 §6), reconciled against Goldman Sachs'
     own independently-submitted report at the Trade Repository
Takeaway. Every field on this record — the exact record the curriculum set out to make understandable from the very first page — now has a specific module, section, and worked-number origin behind it. Nothing on this record is arbitrary, and nothing on it needs to be memorized as an isolated fact; each field is the concrete output of a concept this roadmap built with real numbers, from Module 1 all the way through Module 15.

08 · Submit-and-forget is not an option

Regulatory Perspective — Reporting Accuracy, ISO 20022 & Enforcement

Regulatory reporting is an ongoing control discipline, not a one-time submission.

Regulation / BodyHow it touches Regulatory Reporting specifically
EMIR REFIT (2024 update)A significant overhaul standardizing EMIR's reporting fields onto the ISO 20022 XML message format — a genuinely major IT migration event, similar in scale to Module 8 §1's LIBOR-to-RFR transition
National Competent Authorities (enforcement)Regulators actively fine firms for reporting FAILURES, not just missing data — late reports, unreconciled breaks (Section 03), and incorrect field population have all drawn real, material regulatory fines
Firms' own control functionsMost large banks run a dedicated Regulatory Reporting Control function (adjacent to, but distinct from, Product Control/IPV from Module 10 §7) verifying reportable trades were actually reported, correctly, on time
IOSCO / CPMIContinue setting the global principles every regime in Section 06 ultimately implements locally — the same standard-setters from Module 10-13 close the loop here as well
Takeaway. Regulatory reporting isn't a "submit and forget" process — it's an ongoing control discipline with its own dedicated function, its own data-format migrations, and its own enforcement risk, exactly mirroring the Independent Price Verification control Module 10 §7 introduced for valuation.

Self-check · Solutions

Recap Quiz — Answers & Explanations

Check your work against these.

Q1 — SFTR: NO. EMIR: NO. MiFID II Transaction Reporting: YES

Section 01's decision-matrix framing applies directly: each regime's applicability has to be checked independently, and a trade can be simultaneously out-of-scope for two regimes while being squarely in-scope for a third — exactly what Section 04's worked example demonstrates explicitly for this same Coca-Cola trade.

Q2 — A pairing/reconciliation BREAK — an even more severe flag than an economic-field mismatch

Section 03 states the pairing mechanism matches "on UTI" specifically — a UTI mismatch means the fundamental linking key itself is wrong, likely because one firm generated its own UTI instead of using the one the UTI-generating counterparty actually produced (Module 2 §12), a more foundational data problem than a simple field discrepancy.

Q3 — The two regimes have entirely different SCOPES by design
Section 04's worked example states this precisely — EMIR's product scope (derivatives only) and MiFID II's much broader instrument scope are independently defined, not derived from a shared underlying principle that would apply them identically.
Q4 — Both share the same underlying field PATTERN — counterparty, economic terms, collateral

Section 05's takeaway states this directly: SFTR and EMIR cover completely different products but converge on nearly identical report SHAPES because the underlying regulatory concern (systemic exposure, backed by what collateral) is structurally the same question either way.

Q5 — A "Netting Set ID" field, distinct from "Trade ID," would resolve the ambiguity

Section 07 states this explicitly, citing Module 11 §3's IT/reporting angle callout directly: exposure/collateral fields commonly get attached to netting-set-level aggregates in real systems even when displayed alongside a single representative Trade ID.

Q6 — Module 12 (Collateral & Margin), specifically Section 05's Threshold-driven Required Collateral calculation

Section 07's annotation points back to "Module 12 §5's Required Collateral, driven by a CAD 200,000 CSA Threshold" — the CAD 1.8M figure is fully reconstructable from Net Exposure and the Threshold, both legally documented CSA terms.

Q7 — LATE reporting or a reconciliation break flagged and fixed after the fact can both draw fines, independent of eventual accuracy

Section 08 states this directly: regulators "fine firms for reporting FAILURES, not just missing data — late reports, unreconciled breaks, and incorrect field population have all drawn real, material regulatory fines" — timeliness and first-time accuracy are independently enforceable obligations.
