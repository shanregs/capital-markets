01 · Purpose & institutional basis

What Is DMO Reporting? — Purpose & Institutional Basis

The UK Debt Management Office (DMO) is an executive agency of HM Treasury, established in 1998, responsible for issuing and managing UK government debt — gilts and Treasury bills — and the government's day-to-day cash management. "DMO reporting" is the operational and commercial reporting a small panel of appointed banks, the GEMMs (Gilt-Edged Market Makers), must submit to the DMO as a condition of their GEMM status.

Important distinction up front. Unlike SFTR or EMIR (Module 14), DMO reporting is not a statutory regulatory reporting regime. There's no Trade Repository, no regulation number, no fine from a conduct regulator for missing a deadline. It's reporting owed under a commercial agreement (the GEMM agreement) — closer to a supplier scorecard than a regulatory filing, even though losing GEMM status is commercially serious. Section 9 draws this distinction out fully.

Why it exists: two purposes — (1) reliable, well-subscribed auctions, since the DMO needs a panel of dealers committed in advance to bidding meaningfully every time; and (2) monitoring genuine market-making, since GEMM status carries real privileges (Section 2) the DMO needs ongoing evidence are being earned, not just held.

Takeaway. DMO reporting exists to keep a two-sided bargain honest — the DMO grants a small panel of banks privileged, exclusive access to UK government debt issuance; in exchange, those banks owe the DMO continuous proof, at four different time horizons (Sections 4–7), that they're holding up their end.

02 · The reporting panel

Who Reports — GEMMs & the GEMM Framework

GEMMs (Gilt-Edged Market Makers) are a panel of banks and broker-dealers formally recognized by the DMO, under a bilateral GEMM agreement, as primary dealers in the gilt market. Bank Of Toronto's London entity — GEMM code BOT-GEMM-07 (illustrative) — is one such firm throughout this module.

GEMM ObligationGEMM Privilege (in return)
Continuous two-way price-making in gilts (quoting both a bid and an offer throughout the trading day)Direct, exclusive access to the DMO's gilt auctions as a bidding counterparty
Minimum participation in every gilt auction (competitive bidding)Non-Competitive Allocation (NCA) access at auctions (Section 4)
Minimum ongoing secondary-market turnover / market share, measured on a rolling basis (Section 7)Post Auction Option Facility (PAOF) access (Section 4)
Regular reporting of bids, allocations & turnover to the DMO (Sections 4–7)Access to the DMO's secondary-market dealing facilities (e.g., a gilt-lending facility to cover short positions)
—Inclusion on the published GEMM panel, and standing in the DMO's periodic market-share rankings
Callout — verify current obligations before building against them. Exact minimum bidding percentages, NCA/PAOF caps, and market-share thresholds are set out in the DMO's own published GEMM rules and operational notices, which get revised over time — treat every percentage in this module as an illustrative teaching number, and confirm live figures against Section 11's official links.
Takeaway. GEMM status is a privilege conditional on continuous proof of performance — it isn't granted once and held indefinitely. That conditionality is exactly why four separate report types exist at four separate time horizons (Section 3): the DMO wants evidence at the auction level, the daily level, the weekly level, and the rolled-up periodic level, not just a single annual certification.

03 · Same activity, four time horizons

The Four Report Types — Overview

A GEMM's reporting to the DMO isn't four independent obligations — it's the same underlying trading activity, rolled up at four different time horizons, each one built from the one below it.

Takeaway. This is a different kind of multiplicity than Module 14 §1's "one trade, five regimes" — there, the same trade was checked against five independent regimes with different scopes. Here, the same trading activity is rolled up through four dependent time horizons. Get the daily figure wrong, and the weekly and periodic figures are wrong too — there's no independent recomputation at each level the way EMIR/SFTR's dual-sided reporting provides a built-in check (Section 9 draws this comparison out further).

04 · Competitive · NCA · PAOF

Auction Reporting — Worked Example

A UK gilt auction runs as a single-price (uniform-price) auction: every successful competitive bidder pays the same clearing price (the lowest accepted price). Separately, GEMMs can apply for a Non-Competitive Allocation (NCA) — a fixed nominal amount with no price specified, allotted at the average accepted price — and shortly after results, for further stock via the Post Auction Option Facility (PAOF), also at the average price.

Gilt Auction · 4¼% Treasury Gilt 2036GBP 3,000,000,000 On Offer
Auction date: Wed 19-Aug-2026     Competitive bidding closes: 10:30am
Settlement (T+1): Thu 20-Aug-2026 (gilts settle T+1 via CREST --
                   systems-landscape §03's UK gilt settlement cycle)

BANK OF TORONTO'S COMPETITIVE BIDS:
  Bid 1: GBP 400,000,000 @ price 98.75 (yield ~4.42%)
  Bid 2: GBP 300,000,000 @ price 98.70 (yield ~4.43%)
  Bid 3: GBP 200,000,000 @ price 98.60 (yield ~4.45%)
  Total competitive bid submitted: GBP 900,000,000

BANK OF TORONTO'S NCA APPLICATION:
  GBP 15,000,000 (illustrative 0.5% of the GBP 3,000,000,000
  on offer -- the typical per-GEMM NCA cap)

AUCTION-WIDE RESULT (published by the DMO same day):
  Total bids received (all GEMMs): GBP 8,100,000,000
  Bid-to-cover ratio = 8,100,000,000 / 3,000,000,000 = 2.70
  Lowest accepted (clearing) price: 98.62 (yield 4.44%)
  Average accepted price:            98.71 (yield 4.43%)
  Tail = average price - clearing price = 98.71 - 98.62 = 0.09
         (~1 basis point of yield -- a "tight" auction)

BANK OF TORONTO'S ALLOCATION:
  Bid 1 (98.75 >= clearing 98.62): FILLED  -- 400,000,000
  Bid 2 (98.70 >= clearing 98.62): FILLED  -- 300,000,000
  Bid 3 (98.60 <  clearing 98.62): REJECTED -- 0
  Competitive allocation = 700,000,000 nominal @ 98.62
    = GBP 690,340,000 consideration

  NCA allocation = 15,000,000 nominal @ average price 98.71
    = GBP 14,806,500 consideration

PAOF (illustrative cap: 10% of own competitive allocation):
  Entitlement = 10% x 700,000,000 = GBP 70,000,000 nominal
  Bank Of Toronto exercises PAOF in full @ average price 98.71
    = GBP 69,097,000 consideration

TOTAL FROM THIS AUCTION: 715,000,000 + 70,000,000
  = GBP 785,000,000 nominal
  = GBP 690,340,000 + 14,806,500 + 69,097,000
  = GBP 774,243,500 total consideration

Auction Report fields captured for this one auction: GEMM ID, auction date, gilt identifier, every competitive bid (price, yield, nominal), the NCA application, the PAOF exercise, the clearing price, the average price, and the final allocation/consideration at each layer — see Section 8's full field reference.

Takeaway. A single auction generates three economically distinct allocations at three different prices (clearing price for competitive, average price for both NCA and PAOF) — a reporting system that only captures "GEMM won GBP 785,000,000 of the gilt" without preserving which layer each slice came from would be unable to reconcile the GBP 774,243,500 total consideration back to its components, or prove Bid 3 was correctly rejected.

05 · Every trading day

Daily Trade Reporting — Worked Example

Every trading day, a GEMM reports its secondary-market gilt turnover — every buy and every sell, across every gilt, regardless of counterparty — to the DMO. This is the raw activity feed everything in Sections 6–7 gets built from.

Daily Trade ReportBank Of Toronto · Thu 20-Aug-2026
(the day after Section 4's auction settles)

Client business:
  Buys from clients:  GBP 150,000,000 nominal (various gilts)
  Sells to clients:   GBP 210,000,000 nominal (various gilts)

Interdealer business (with other GEMMs):
  Buys:  GBP  40,000,000 nominal
  Sells: GBP  60,000,000 nominal

GROSS DAILY TURNOVER REPORTED
  = 150,000,000 + 210,000,000 + 40,000,000 + 60,000,000
  = GBP 460,000,000

(reported as GROSS two-way turnover -- a GBP 150,000,000 buy and
a GBP 210,000,000 sell are NOT netted into a GBP 60,000,000 net
figure; the DMO wants to see the level of ACTIVITY, not just the
net position change)
Takeaway. "Gross, not net" is the single most important convention in this report — the same design choice Module 3 flags for settlement-level activity shows up again here for a different reason: a market-maker who buys GBP 150,000,000 and sells GBP 210,000,000 in one day did meaningfully more market-making work than one who simply held a static GBP 60,000,000 short position all day, even though the net effect on their book looks similar.

06 · Smoothing out auction-day spikes

Weekly Trade Reporting — Worked Example

The Weekly Trade Report is Section 5's daily figures, summed across the trading week — a smoothed view that dampens single-day noise (like an auction day's elevated volume) before feeding the rolling market-share calculation (Section 7).

Weekly Trade ReportBank Of Toronto · Mon 17 – Fri 21-Aug-2026
Mon 17-Aug:                    GBP   320,000,000
Tue 18-Aug:                    GBP   410,000,000
Wed 19-Aug (auction day --
  Section 4's auction settlement
  flow plus ordinary secondary
  trading):                    GBP   900,000,000
Thu 20-Aug (Section 5's figure): GBP  460,000,000
Fri 21-Aug:                    GBP   350,000,000
---------------------------------------------------
TOTAL WEEKLY TURNOVER:         GBP 2,440,000,000
Takeaway. Notice Wednesday's turnover is more than double an ordinary day — auction days are structurally higher-volume, since allocation flows, PAOF exercises, and ordinary secondary-market activity all land on the same reporting day. A weekly aggregate absorbs that spike into a fuller picture; a daily-only view would make Wednesday look anomalous rather than routine.

07 · The league table

Aggregated / Periodic Reporting — Market Share & the GEMM "League Table"

The DMO calculates each GEMM's market share on a rolling basis (illustrative: a trailing 6-month window), combining secondary-market turnover against the total gilt market turnover across all GEMMs combined. This figure determines whether a GEMM keeps its status.

Rolling 6-Month Market ShareBank Of Toronto
Total secondary gilt turnover, ALL GEMMs combined,
rolling 6-month window ending 21-Aug-2026:    GBP 900,000,000,000

Bank Of Toronto's own turnover, same window:  GBP  40,500,000,000

Market share = 40,500,000,000 / 900,000,000,000 = 4.5%

Illustrative minimum required share to retain GEMM status: 2.0%
-> Bank Of Toronto is comfortably ABOVE the minimum

Illustrative GEMM league table (by rolling market share):
  1. [Other GEMM]        7.8%
  2. [Other GEMM]        6.9%
  ...
  6. Bank Of Toronto     4.5%
  ...
 16. [Other GEMM]        1.6%  <- BELOW the 2.0% minimum -> under review

Consequences of falling below the minimum: the DMO places the underperforming GEMM under review; a persistent shortfall can lead to removal from the GEMM panel — losing auction access, NCA/PAOF privileges (Section 4), and access to the DMO's secondary-market dealing facilities all at once, not gradually.

Takeaway. The periodic/aggregated report is the only one of the four report types that produces a RELATIVE figure (share of a market total) rather than an ABSOLUTE one (pounds of turnover) — which means Bank Of Toronto's own reported numbers alone can't tell it whether it's compliant; it depends equally on how much every other GEMM reported over the same window, a genuinely different kind of dependency than anything in Sections 4–6.

08 · What a GEMM's systems must hold

What Data Needs to Be Captured — Field Reference by Report Type

The fields below are what a GEMM's own trade-capture and reporting systems need to hold to actually produce Sections 4–7's reports, grouped the same way SFTR §6 grouped its four report tables.

Report TypeCore Fields
Auction ReportGEMM ID · Auction date · Gilt identifier (name/ISIN) · Bid type (Competitive / NCA / PAOF) · Bid price · Bid yield · Bid nominal amount · Clearing price · Average accepted price · Allocation nominal amount · Allocation price · Consideration amount · Settlement date (T+1)
Daily Trade ReportGEMM ID · Trade date · Gilt identifier · Gilt type (conventional / index-linked / green) · Direction (buy/sell) · Nominal amount · Price · Yield · Counterparty type (client / other GEMM / interdealer broker) · Venue · Execution time · Settlement date · CREST settlement reference
Weekly Trade ReportGEMM ID · Week-ending date · Aggregated buy nominal · Aggregated sell nominal · Gross weekly turnover · Breakdown by gilt type
Aggregated / Periodic ReportGEMM ID · Reporting period (rolling window) · Total auction bid nominal · Total auction allocation nominal · Total secondary turnover · Market-wide total turnover · Computed market share % · League-table rank · Compliance flag
Takeaway. Every field in the Weekly and Aggregated rows is a DERIVED field — computable entirely from the Daily Trade Report's raw data plus the market-wide total the DMO itself publishes — while the Auction and Daily rows contain the only genuinely PRIMARY data a GEMM's systems capture at the point of trade. A well-designed pipeline stores Auction and Daily data as the source of truth and computes Weekly/Aggregated as views over it, rather than maintaining four independently-entered datasets that can drift out of reconciliation.

09 · Commercial vs. statutory

DMO Reporting vs. Other Regimes — Same Trade, Different Destinations

A single gilt trade routinely triggers two entirely separate reports, to two entirely separate recipients, for two entirely separate reasons — exactly the pattern Module 14 §1 established for OTC derivatives, now shown for a cash government bond.

Diagram · One Gilt Trade, Two ReportsUK MiFIR × DMO
Bank Of Toronto sells GBP 10,000,000 nominal of a gilt to a client
on Thu 20-Aug-2026 (part of Section 5's GBP 210,000,000 client-sell
total):

-> UK MiFIR TRANSACTION REPORTING (statutory, to the FCA):
   every execution in an in-scope instrument -- gilts included --
   gets reported for MARKET ABUSE SURVEILLANCE, the same purpose
   Module 14 §4 describes for MiFID II Transaction Reporting, just
   under the UK's onshored MiFIR regime (systems-landscape §10)

-> DMO DAILY TRADE REPORT (commercial/contractual, to the DMO):
   the SAME GBP 10,000,000 sale is one line inside that day's
   GBP 210,000,000 client-sell total (Section 5) -- reported for
   GEMM PERFORMANCE MONITORING, an entirely different purpose,
   to an entirely different recipient, under NO statute at all
DimensionUK MiFIR Transaction ReportingDMO Reporting
Legal basisStatutory (onshored UK MiFIR)Commercial (the GEMM agreement)
RecipientFCA (via an Approved Reporting Mechanism)The DMO directly
PurposeMarket abuse detectionGEMM performance / market-making monitoring
Consequence of failureRegulatory enforcement, fines (Module 14 §8)Loss of GEMM status — a commercial, not regulatory, consequence
ScopeEvery in-scope instrument execution, all firmsGilts only, GEMMs only
Takeaway. Treat these as two independent pipelines even though they consume nearly identical trade data — a firm that gets its UK MiFIR transaction reporting perfectly right can still lose its GEMM status entirely on a DMO reporting failure, and vice versa, because the two obligations answer to different masters with no shared enforcement mechanism between them.

10 · Don't reuse the offset

Settlement Tie-In — CREST & T+1

Every gilt trade in this module — auction allocations (Section 4) and secondary-market trades alike (Sections 5–6) — settles through CREST, the UK's Central Securities Depository operated by Euroclear UK & International, on a T+1 cycle (systems-landscape §3's settlement-cycle table: "United Kingdom | Gilts | T+1").

Takeaway. Gilts have settled T+1 for a long time already — unlike UK equities, which still run T+2 with a targeted move to T+1 only in October 2027 (systems-landscape §3, §6) — so a reporting system correctly computing "Trade Date + 1" for a gilt auction allocation must NOT reuse that same offset for a UK equity trade executing on the very same desk, the same per-market-per-asset-class trap systems-landscape §3's takeaway already flags.

Self-check · Solutions

Recap Quiz — Answers & Explanations

Check your work against these.

Q1 — DMO reporting arises from a private commercial agreement, not a statute — the consequence is losing GEMM status, not a regulatory fine

Section 1's "Important distinction up front" states this directly: there's no Trade Repository, no regulation number, and no regulator-imposed fine for a late DMO report — it's closer to a supplier scorecard than a regulatory filing, even though losing GEMM status is a serious commercial outcome.

Q2 — Bid 3's price (98.60) is BELOW the clearing price (98.62); Bids 1 and 2 are filled, and all filled bids settle AT the clearing price (98.62), not at what they bid

Section 4 states this is a single-price (uniform-price) auction: every successful competitive bidder pays the same clearing price regardless of what price they actually bid, as long as their bid was at or above it. Bank Of Toronto's higher bid prices only mattered for determining that its bids would clear.

Q3 — NCA and PAOF are, by design, for participants who did NOT specify a competitive price — priced at the average so they don't earn the potentially more favorable clearing price competitive bidders get for taking on price risk
Section 4's definition states NCA is "a fixed nominal amount with no price specified, allotted at the average accepted price instead," and PAOF is applied for at the average price too — both are priced worse than the clearing price precisely because neither required committing to a specific price ahead of the result.
Q4 — The DMO is measuring genuine market-making ACTIVITY, not just end-of-day position change

A GEMM that bought GBP 150,000,000 and sold GBP 210,000,000 (Section 5) did meaningfully more two-way price-making work than one that simply held a static net short position all day, even if the net effect on their book looks similar. Section 5's takeaway draws the parallel to Module 3's gross-vs-net settlement distinction.

Q5 — Market share is a RELATIVE figure — Bank Of Toronto's turnover divided by ALL GEMMs' combined turnover

Section 7's takeaway states this explicitly: the periodic/aggregated report "produces a relative figure... rather than an absolute one," so Bank Of Toronto's compliance depends equally on figures it doesn't control or see until the DMO publishes the aggregate.

Q6 — Auction and Daily Trade Reports are PRIMARY; Weekly and Aggregated/Periodic are entirely DERIVED
Section 8's takeaway recommends storing Auction/Daily data as the single source of truth and computing Weekly/Aggregated as views over it — avoiding the risk of four independently-entered datasets drifting out of reconciliation with each other.
Q7 — Yes — the two obligations have entirely different legal bases and no shared enforcement mechanism

Section 9's takeaway states this directly: "a firm that gets its UK MiFIR transaction reporting perfectly right can still lose its GEMM status entirely on a DMO reporting failure, and vice versa, because the two obligations answer to different masters... with no shared enforcement mechanism between them."

Q8 — Gilts settle T+1 while UK equities still settle T+2 (until October 2027) — hard-coding "+1" for every UK trade would compute the wrong settlement date for equities

Section 10's takeaway cites the exact same underlying warning from systems-landscape §3 that a global settlement-offset assumption breaks the moment it's applied across different asset classes within the very same market.
