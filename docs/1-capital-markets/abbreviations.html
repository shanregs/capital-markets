01 · Which exact thing is this?

Instrument & Trade Identifiers

The codes that answer "which exact instrument, trade, or entity is this record about."

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
ISINInternational Securities Identification NumberGlobal, 12-character identifier for a security (2-letter country code + 9-char body + check digit). See Module 2 §2.
CUSIPCommittee on Uniform Securities Identification Procedures9-character identifier for US/Canada securities; embedded inside the ISIN for North American securities.
SEDOLStock Exchange Daily Official List7-character UK-originated identifier, still used for some London-listed securities.
UPIUnique Product IdentifierGlobal identifier for OTC derivative products (not individual trades) — increasingly replaces ad-hoc internal codes in regulatory reporting. See Module 2 §11.
UTIUnique Trade IdentifierGlobally unique identifier for one specific derivative transaction, generated at execution — the derivatives world's answer to a "Trade ID."
CFIClassification of Financial Instruments (ISO 10962)6-character code on a security master describing instrument type. The cash-instrument counterpart to ISDA taxonomy for derivatives. See Module 2 §6.
LEILegal Entity Identifier20-character global identifier for a legal entity (a bank, fund, corporate) — not an instrument. Mandatory on most regulatory trade reports. Covered in Module 13. On a Credit Default Swap specifically, LEI also identifies the Reference Entity — a second, distinct role beyond identifying counterparties (Module 9 §6).
MICMarket Identifier Code (ISO 10383)4-character code identifying a specific exchange or trading venue (e.g. XNYS for NYSE).

02 · Where the trade happens

Market Structure & Venues

Who or what sits between two counterparties, and where trading actually takes place.

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
OTCOver-the-CounterA trade negotiated bilaterally between two parties, with no central exchange. See Module 1 §5.
CCPCentral CounterpartyA clearing house that steps in between two counterparties post-trade, becoming buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer. Novation mechanics covered in Module 3 §6; risk-reduction effect covered in Module 11. For a Listed Derivative, novation happens instantaneously at execution rather than as a later step (Module 7 §5, Module 15 §1).
LCH / SwapClearLondon Clearing House / SwapClearLCH is a CCP group headquartered in London, part of the London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG) — one of the world's largest clearing houses. SwapClear is LCH's specific clearing service for interest rate swaps, novating the majority of the world's centrally-cleared IRS volume; "LCH" names the legal entity/group, "SwapClear" the product line within it. Used throughout this roadmap as the concrete, named CCP example. See Module 3 §6, Module 8 §7-9, Systems Landscape §6, §11.
CSDCentral Securities DepositoryThe single central ledger for a market recording ultimate legal ownership of securities (e.g. CDS in Canada, DTC in the US). See Module 1 §7.
ICSDInternational Central Securities DepositoryA CSD that settles cross-border/international securities (Euroclear, Clearstream).
MTFMultilateral Trading FacilityA EU-regulated trading venue that isn't a formal "exchange" but still matches multiple buyers/sellers under common rules.
SEFSwap Execution FacilityA US-regulated electronic venue for trading certain standardized OTC swaps, mandated post-2008 crisis.
SISystematic InternalizerA firm dealing on its own account when executing client orders outside a regulated exchange/MTF, above certain volume thresholds (a MiFID II concept).

03 · What kind of thing is being traded

Products & Derivatives

The instrument families that recur across Module 2's asset-class tables.

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
IRSInterest Rate SwapTwo parties exchange fixed vs floating interest cash flows on a notional. See Module 1 §8, Module 2 §8.
FRAForward Rate AgreementA contract locking in an interest rate for a single future period — essentially a one-period IRS.
CDSCredit Default SwapProtection against a reference entity's default; buyer pays premium, seller pays out on a credit event. See Module 2 §9.
TRSTotal Return SwapOne party pays the total return (price change + income) of an asset in exchange for a funding-rate payment.
NDFNon-Deliverable ForwardAn FX Forward where only the net cash difference settles, used when a currency isn't freely convertible. See Module 2 §10.
ETDExchange-Traded DerivativeA derivative (future, listed option) traded on a regulated exchange, contrasted with OTC.
ETFExchange-Traded FundA fund whose units trade on an exchange like a share, typically tracking an index.
MBSMortgage-Backed SecurityA security backed by a pool of mortgage loans; principal amortizes over time. See Module 2 §6.
ABSAsset-Backed SecurityA security backed by a pool of non-mortgage assets (auto loans, credit card receivables, etc.).
CLOCollateralized Loan ObligationA securitization backed by a pool of leveraged corporate loans.
CDOCollateralized Debt ObligationA broader securitization structure backed by a pool of debt instruments, tranched by risk/seniority.
ADR / GDRAmerican / Global Depositary ReceiptA certificate representing shares of a foreign company, traded on a domestic exchange.
CTDCheapest to DeliverOn a physically-settled bond future, the specific eligible bond (from a defined delivery basket) that's most economical for the short to source and deliver, chosen using a published conversion factor. See Module 15 §5.

04 · What the floating leg references

Rates & Benchmarks

The reference rates that drive floating legs, discounting, and spread quoting.

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
CORRACanadian Overnight Repo Rate AverageCanada's risk-free overnight benchmark rate, calculated daily by the Bank of Canada from actual overnight repo transactions collateralized by Government of Canada securities — not from bank estimates the way CDOR/LIBOR were. Used as the floating leg on CAD swaps (Module 1 §8's Underlier explainer and worked example; full CDOR-to-CORRA transition story in Module 8 §1).
CDORCanadian Dollar Offered RateThe legacy, survey-based CAD interbank benchmark CORRA replaced — banks self-reported what they believed they could borrow at, rather than CORRA's actual-transaction basis. Discontinued after June 2024. See Module 8 §1.
SOFRSecured Overnight Financing RateThe US's risk-free overnight benchmark rate, successor to USD LIBOR.
SONIASterling Overnight Index AverageThe UK's risk-free overnight benchmark rate, successor to GBP LIBOR.
LIBORLondon Interbank Offered RateThe legacy interbank benchmark rate, phased out by 2023 in favor of SOFR/SONIA/CORRA-style rates. Still seen in legacy trade data.
OISOvernight Index SwapA swap referencing a compounded overnight rate (like CORRA/SOFR) as its floating leg — now the standard IRS structure.
bpsBasis Points1 bps = 0.01%. Used to quote spreads, rates, and small price moves precisely (e.g. CDS spread "120 bps" in Module 2 §9). Full theory, cross-asset-class worked examples, and how bps drive Valuation/MTM/YTM: Module 24 — Basis Points Deep Dive.

05 · What a position is worth

Pricing & Valuation

The measures that turn a trade record into a dollar value on a report.

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
YTMYield to MaturityThe total annualized return on a bond if held to maturity, factoring in price paid, coupons, and the pull-to-par gain/loss. See Module 1 §1.
MTMMark-to-MarketRevaluing a position at current market prices/rates rather than its original trade price — what Middle Office computes daily. See Module 1 §4.
Position(not an acronym)The net, cumulative holding in an instrument at a point in time, built from every trade executed so far — a state that persists and revalues daily, distinct from a Trade (a one-time event). Full theory, cross-asset-class worked examples, and daily regulatory reporting impact: Module 25 — Position Deep Dive.
NPVNet Present ValueThe value today of a stream of future cash flows, discounted back at an appropriate rate. Full treatment in Module 10 (Valuation); Module 8 (Rates) already uses NPV-style discounting for the FRA worked example's upfront settlement (Module 8 §4b).
PV01 / DV01Price/Dollar Value of 1 Basis PointHow much a position's value changes for a 1 bps move in interest rates — a core interest rate risk sensitivity measure, first previewed in Module 5 §1 (bond Duration) and Module 8 §1 (Rates), fully covered in Module 10. The Credit-market equivalent for a spread move is "Spread DV01" / "CS01" — see Module 24 — Basis Points Deep Dive §5.
P&LProfit and LossThe gain or loss on a position or trade over a period.
NAVNet Asset ValueThe per-unit value of a fund's holdings (assets minus liabilities), used to price fund units/ETF shares.
IPVIndependent Price VerificationThe control process (typically Product Control, inside Middle Office) that independently re-derives a position's valuation to check it against the Front Office's own mark — scrutiny intensity scales with Fair Value Level (Module 10 §6-7).

06 · When and how cash/securities actually move

Settlement & Clearing

The mechanics that turn an agreed trade into securities and cash actually changing hands.

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
T, T+1, T+2Trade Date, Trade Date + n business daysStandard settlement-timing shorthand. See Module 1 §6 for full worked examples with real dates.
DvPDelivery versus PaymentA settlement mechanism ensuring securities and cash move simultaneously, so neither party is left exposed. See Module 1 §7.
SSIStandard Settlement InstructionsPre-agreed, static instructions (custodian, account number, etc.) for where to deliver securities/cash — reference data, not per-trade data.
FOPFree of PaymentA securities transfer with no corresponding cash payment (the opposite of DvP) — e.g. moving collateral.

07 · How exposure is measured and covered

Risk, Collateral & Margin

The measures and mechanisms protecting each side of a trade against the other's default.

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
IMInitial MarginCollateral posted upfront to cover potential future losses on a derivative position before it's even marked-to-market. Covered in Module 12.
VMVariation MarginCollateral exchanged periodically (daily, for exchange-traded and many centrally-cleared products) to cover the current mark-to-market gain/loss on a position. Covered in Module 12; concrete daily examples already worked in Module 4 §4c, Module 7 §5, Module 8 §5, and Module 15 §4c.
CSACredit Support AnnexThe legal document (an annex to the ISDA Master Agreement) that sets out collateral terms between two OTC derivative counterparties. See Module 2 §7, Module 12.
VaRValue at RiskA statistical estimate of the maximum expected loss on a portfolio over a given time horizon at a given confidence level. Covered in Module 11.
PFEPotential Future ExposureAn estimate of how large counterparty credit exposure on a derivative could grow over its remaining life. Covered in Module 11.
CVACredit Valuation AdjustmentAn adjustment to a derivative's value reflecting the risk that the counterparty might default before the contract matures — a direct application of Credit's default-risk mechanics (Module 9) to Risk & Exposure (Module 11).
CECurrent ExposureThe loss a firm would suffer TODAY if its counterparty defaulted right now — equal to MAX(MTM, 0), never negative. See Module 11 §1.
SA-CCRStandardized Approach for Counterparty Credit RiskThe current Basel methodology banks use to calculate regulatory capital against counterparty credit risk on derivatives, formalizing Current Exposure/PFE/netting into a regulator-mandated formula. See Module 11 §8.
SIMMISDA Standard Initial Margin ModelISDA's standardized model calculating Initial Margin for non-centrally-cleared derivatives directly from a position's risk sensitivities. See Module 11 §7, Module 12.
UMRUncleared Margin RulesThe joint IOSCO/BCBS framework requiring Initial and Variation Margin on non-centrally-cleared derivatives above certain thresholds. See Module 11 §8, Module 12.
CCARComprehensive Capital Analysis and ReviewThe US Federal Reserve's annual stress-testing regime for large banks, extending VaR-style portfolio risk logic to extreme hypothetical macroeconomic scenarios. See Module 11 §8.
Haircut— (not an acronym, but standard shorthand)A percentage reduction applied to a security's Market Value when used as collateral, converting it into Collateral Value. See Module 12 §3.
MTAMinimum Transfer AmountThe smallest collateral movement a CSA allows, alongside Threshold, to prevent constant tiny transfers as MTM wobbles day to day. See Module 12 §5.
AANAAverage Aggregate Notional AmountThe metric that determines which phase of UMR's six-wave rollout a firm falls into. See Module 12 §8.
HQLAHigh-Quality Liquid AssetsThe pool of assets a bank must hold enough of, under Basel's LCR, to survive a 30-day stress scenario — posting collateral ties up a bank's own HQLA. See Module 12 §8.
LCRLiquidity Coverage RatioThe Basel III/IV ratio requiring a bank to hold enough HQLA to survive a 30-day liquidity stress scenario — directly connected to collateral management. See Module 12 §8.
CSDRCentral Securities Depositories RegulationThe EU regime setting settlement-discipline rules (mandatory buy-ins, cash penalties for fails) applying to securities-collateral movements settling through EU CSDs. See Module 12 §8.

08 · Who sets and enforces the rules, globally

Global Regulatory & Industry Bodies

Bodies and standards that operate above/across individual jurisdictions — every regional regime in Section 09 ultimately traces back to one of these.

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
ISDAInternational Swaps and Derivatives AssociationThe trade body standardizing OTC derivative documentation (Master Agreement) and product classification (ISDA Taxonomy). See Module 2 §7. Its Determinations Committees — industry panels, not a government regulator — rule on whether a CDS Credit Event has occurred, the single most consequential decision in a CDS's lifecycle (Module 9 §5, §8).
BCBSBasel Committee on Banking SupervisionThe global standard-setter for bank capital/liquidity rules (the "Basel" accords) — drives IM/VM and capital requirements.
IOSCOInternational Organization of Securities CommissionsThe global standard-setter coordinating securities regulators across jurisdictions — its principles underpin most national trade-reporting regimes.
FSBFinancial Stability BoardThe G20-mandated body monitoring global financial system risk; it mandated OTC derivative reforms (clearing, reporting, margining) globally post-2008, which each region then implemented locally.
CPMICommittee on Payments and Market InfrastructuresThe global standard-setter for payment, clearing, and settlement systems (works jointly with IOSCO on CCP/CSD standards).
GLEIFGlobal Legal Entity Identifier FoundationThe body overseeing the global LEI system — accredits the Local Operating Units (LOUs) that issue LEIs to firms in each country.
LOULocal Operating UnitThe national entity actually accredited by GLEIF to issue and renew LEIs — an LEI's ACTIVE/LAPSED status (Module 13 §7) is maintained at the LOU level.
IASBInternational Accounting Standards BoardThe global accounting standard-setter — issues IFRS 13, the standard defining Fair Value and its Level 1/2/3 hierarchy (Module 10 §6). The first standard-setter in this glossary that isn't a markets/derivatives regulator at all, but an accounting one.
FASBFinancial Accounting Standards BoardThe US accounting standard-setter — issues ASC 820, the US GAAP equivalent of IFRS 13, with the same Level 1/2/3 Fair Value concept. See Module 10 §8.
FRTBFundamental Review of the Trading BookThe BCBS's post-2008 overhaul of how banks calculate regulatory capital against trading-book market risk — directly penalizes banks for holding positions with less-observable, harder-to-verify (Level 2/3) valuations. See Module 10 §8.

09 · Who sets and enforces the rules, by jurisdiction

Regional Reporting Regimes & Regulators

Every OTC derivative trade Bank Of Toronto books has to be reported somewhere under local law — but "somewhere" means a different regulator, a different rulebook, and often a different Trade Repository depending on which jurisdiction the trade (or the counterparty) touches.

United States

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
CFTCCommodity Futures Trading CommissionThe primary US regulator for OTC derivatives (swaps) — sets swap data reporting, clearing, and SEF trading rules under Dodd-Frank.
SECSecurities and Exchange CommissionThe US regulator for securities markets (equities, bonds, exchanges) and for security-based swaps (e.g. single-name equity/credit swaps) under Reg SBSR.
Dodd-FrankDodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection ActThe 2010 US law mandating OTC derivative clearing, reporting, and SEF trading — the US's version of what EMIR does in the EU.
Reg SBSRRegulation Security-Based Swap ReportingThe SEC's reporting rulebook for security-based swaps, as distinct from the CFTC's rules for other swaps — a US-specific split not seen in most other jurisdictions.
SDRSwap Data RepositoryThe US term for a Trade Repository — where CFTC-regulated swap data must be reported.
FINRAFinancial Industry Regulatory AuthorityThe US self-regulatory body overseeing broker-dealers.
TRACETrade Reporting and Compliance EngineFINRA's post-trade price transparency system for corporate/agency bonds — a cash-bond reporting regime, separate from derivatives reporting.
CATConsolidated Audit TrailThe SEC-mandated system capturing order and trade lifecycle data across all US equity and options markets, for market surveillance.

United Kingdom

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
FCAFinancial Conduct AuthorityThe UK's primary markets/conduct regulator — supervises trade reporting, market conduct, and most firms' day-to-day compliance.
PRAPrudential Regulation AuthorityThe Bank of England unit responsible for the prudential (capital/solvency) supervision of banks and large investment firms — the UK's Basel-implementer.
BoEBank of EnglandThe UK's central bank; oversees CCPs and systemic financial infrastructure alongside the PRA.
UK EMIRUK European Market Infrastructure RegulationEU EMIR as "onshored" into UK law after Brexit (1 Jan 2021) — same core reporting/clearing obligations, now supervised by the FCA/BoE instead of ESMA.
UK MiFIRUK Markets in Financial Instruments RegulationThe onshored UK version of EU MiFIR, governing UK transaction reporting and market transparency post-Brexit.

European Union

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
EMIREuropean Market Infrastructure RegulationThe EU regulation requiring OTC derivative trades to be reported to Trade Repositories and, for standardized products, centrally cleared.
EMIR REFITEMIR Regulatory Fitness and Performance ProgrammeThe 2024 overhaul standardizing EMIR's reporting fields onto the ISO 20022 XML message format — a major IT migration event for derivatives reporting pipelines. See Module 14 §8.
ISO 20022(ISO messaging standard, not an initialism)The structured XML financial-messaging standard EMIR REFIT (and increasingly other regimes) reports are formatted in, replacing older, less structured formats. See Module 14 §8.
MiFID II / MiFIRMarkets in Financial Instruments Directive / RegulationThe EU's core rulebook for market structure, transparency, and transaction reporting across most financial instruments.
RTS 22Regulatory Technical Standard 22The specific MiFIR technical standard governing Transaction Reporting field content (~65 fields) — distinct from, and narrower in destination than, EMIR's own field set. See Module 14 §4.
ESMAEuropean Securities and Markets AuthorityThe EU-level body that writes the technical standards under EMIR/MiFIR and coordinates the national regulators (NCAs) below.
NCANational Competent AuthorityThe generic term for each EU member state's own regulator (e.g. Germany's BaFin, France's AMF) — day-to-day supervision happens at NCA level, with ESMA setting the common rulebook.
SFTRSecurities Financing Transactions RegulationThe EU regime requiring reporting of repo, securities lending, and other securities financing transactions — a sibling regime to EMIR, covering a different product set.
TRTrade RepositoryThe generic EU/global term for the central registry OTC derivative trades must be reported to (Module 1 §5) — the EU equivalent of a US "SDR."

Canada

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
CSA (regulatory)Canadian Securities AdministratorsThe umbrella council of Canada's provincial/territorial securities regulators (OSC, AMF, BCSC, etc.) that jointly writes harmonized rules, including OTC derivatives trade-reporting rules. Not the same CSA as Credit Support Annex (Section 07) — the abbreviation collides, so always read it from context.
OSCOntario Securities CommissionCanada's largest provincial securities regulator (Canada has no single national SEC-equivalent — regulation is provincial, coordinated via the CSA above).
CIROCanadian Investment Regulatory OrganizationCanada's self-regulatory body for investment dealers and trading activity, formed in 2023 from the merger of IIROC and the MFDA.
TR RulesTrade Reporting Rules (e.g. Multilateral Instrument 96-101)The CSA's harmonized OTC derivatives trade-reporting rules, requiring reporting to a designated Trade Repository — Canada's equivalent of EMIR/Dodd-Frank reporting.
OSFIOffice of the Superintendent of Financial InstitutionsCanada's federal prudential regulator for banks and insurers — the Basel-implementer for Canadian banks like Bank Of Toronto.
BoCBank of CanadaCanada's central bank; publishes CORRA (Section 04) and oversees systemically important clearing/settlement systems.

Hong Kong

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
SFCSecurities and Futures CommissionHong Kong's primary securities and derivatives markets regulator.
HKMAHong Kong Monetary AuthorityHong Kong's central banking institution — jointly administers the OTC derivatives reporting regime with the SFC (for banks and licensed corporations respectively) and oversees systemic infrastructure.
SFOSecurities and Futures OrdinanceThe primary Hong Kong statute under which the OTC derivatives reporting, clearing, and licensing regime is established.
HKTRHong Kong Trade RepositoryThe trade repository operated by the HKMA where OTC derivative transactions booked in/through Hong Kong must be reported.
Takeaway. The pattern repeats everywhere even though the acronyms don't — every region has (1) a markets/conduct regulator, (2) a prudential/central-bank-linked supervisor, (3) a specific law or rulebook mandating OTC derivative reporting, and (4) a Trade Repository the data actually lands in. Once you can name those four things for one jurisdiction, spotting the equivalent in another is mostly a vocabulary exercise — the underlying reporting obligation (Module 14) is structurally the same everywhere.

10 · Internal shorthand

Organizational Shorthand

Common shorthand for internal functions referenced across every module.

Abbr.Full FormMeaning
FO / MO / BOFront Office / Middle Office / Back OfficeThe three-stage internal pipeline a trade passes through — execution, risk/valuation/confirmation, then settlement/reporting. See Module 1 §4.
KYCKnow Your CustomerThe due-diligence process for verifying a counterparty's identity before trading with them — feeds into LEI/reference-data setup.
AMLAnti-Money LaunderingRegulatory controls preventing the financial system from being used to launder illicit funds.
