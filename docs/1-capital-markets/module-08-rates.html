01 · Notional never settles, only the net difference does

What Is Rates? — Theory & Terminology

Rates is the asset class of contracts whose cash flows depend on an interest rate. Module 1 §8 already introduced the core building block — the Interest Rate Swap — as a bilateral agreement to exchange a fixed-rate cash flow stream for a floating-rate one, referencing a Notional that never itself settles. Rates is where that rule applies to almost every product, not the exception.

TermMeaning
Fixed LegThe side of a swap paying a rate agreed at trade date, unchanged for the life of the trade
Floating LegThe side paying a rate that resets periodically against a published reference rate
Reference RateThe published benchmark a floating leg resets against — e.g. CORRA (Canada), SOFR (US), €STR (EU), SONIA (UK)
Payment DatesThe scheduled dates cash flows are calculated and exchanged — quarterly is the most common convention
DV01"Dollar Value of 01" — how much a Rates position's value changes for a 1bp move in rates; the Rates cousin of Module 5 §1's Duration, covered fully in Module 10 (Valuation). For the basis point unit itself, across every asset class, see Module 24 — Basis Points Deep Dive.

The LIBOR-to-RFR transition — why "CORRA" and not "CDOR" or "LIBOR"

IT/reporting angle: until the early 2020s, almost every floating leg in this asset class referenced LIBOR (London Interbank Offered Rate) or a local equivalent — in Canada, that local equivalent was CDOR (Canadian Dollar Offered Rate), a survey-based rate submitted daily by a panel of banks. Both LIBOR and CDOR shared the same fatal flaw: they were based on interbank estimates of what a bank believed it could borrow at, not actual observed transactions — and both proved manipulable (the LIBOR-rigging scandals of the early 2010s). CDOR itself was discontinued after June 2024, following LIBOR's own phase-out a couple of years earlier.

What CORRA actually measures. CORRA (Canadian Overnight Repo Rate Average) replaced CDOR as Canada's benchmark. Unlike CDOR, CORRA isn't a survey of opinions — it's calculated by the Bank of Canada, each business day, directly from the prior day's actual overnight repo transactions (Module 5 §3's repo mechanic) collateralized by Government of Canada securities. Because every input is a real, executed trade at an observable rate rather than a bank's self-reported estimate, CORRA is structurally far harder to manipulate — the same reasoning behind SOFR (US), €STR (EU), and SONIA (UK) all replacing their own local IBOR-style predecessors around the same time. This module uses CORRA throughout, consistent with Module 1 §8's original worked example (which also introduces the general Underlier concept CORRA is one instance of) — but any reporting system built before ~2022 that still has a hardcoded "LIBOR" or "CDOR" reference-rate field is carrying legacy debt from this exact transition, one of the largest coordinated data-migration events the industry has ever run.

Takeaway. Every product in this module is a variation on one theme — fixed vs. floating, or the option to enter into fixed vs. floating — with Notional as a pure calculation input. Keeping that one sentence in mind is enough to correctly reason about every worked example in Section 04.

02 · Four built on IRS, one that isn't

What Are The Products? — Cap/Floor, IR Forward Debt, FRA, IRS & Swaption

IRS is the base case; FRA is one slice of it settled differently; Cap/Floor is a strip of options on the same rate; Swaption is an option on the IRS itself; IR Forward Debt steps outside the pattern entirely.

Diagram · Interest Rate Product TreeFive products, one Avro schema node
INTEREST RATE
│
├── Interest Rate Swap (IRS)   (exchange fixed vs floating cash flows, periodically, for a term)
├── FRA (Forward Rate Agreement) (a single-period IRS, cash-settled once, upfront)
├── Cap / Floor                (a strip of options on a floating rate, one per period)
├── Swaption                   (an option to ENTER an IRS at a future date)
└── IR Forward Debt            (a forward purchase/sale of a fixed income security)
ProductWhat it is
Interest Rate Swap (IRS)Two counterparties exchange a fixed-rate cash flow stream for a floating-rate one, on the same Notional, over a multi-year term — Module 1 §8's worked example
FRAEconomically a single period of an IRS, isolated: one fixed rate locked in today against one future floating rate reset, cash-settled once — but settled upfront, discounted, at the START of the reference period rather than the end (Section 04b)
Cap / FloorA strip of individual options (caplets/floorlets), each paying out only if the floating reference rate is above (Cap) or below (Floor) a strike at that reset — buyer's downside capped at premium, same asymmetric payoff as every prior Option product
SwaptionAn option to enter an IRS at a future date, as either the fixed-rate payer ("Payer Swaption") or receiver ("Receiver Swaption") — an option on a swap, not a swap itself
IR Forward DebtA forward contract to buy or sell a fixed income security (Module 5) at a future date, at a price/yield fixed today — the Rates version of Module 6 §2's FX Forward, referencing a bond instead of a currency pair
Takeaway. Four of five products are built directly on top of the IRS mental model from Module 1 §8 — only IR Forward Debt steps outside it, referencing a bond's price rather than a rate directly.

03 · One product bottoms out in a real security

What Exactly Is Being Traded? — Contracts Referencing a Rate, Not a Rate Itself

Like FX (Module 6 §3), Rates has no security equivalent for four of its five products — pure contracts, created at execution and settled without delivering anything except net cash. IR Forward Debt is the exception.

Diagram · Pure Contracts vs Contract-on-a-SecurityIR Forward Debt is the exception
Interest Rate Swap / FRA / Cap-Floor / Swaption (PURE CONTRACTS):
  None of these reference an existing security. An IRS's Notional
  (Module 1 §8) is a calculation input, never delivered. A Cap's
  strike is compared against a published rate, not a traded
  instrument. Nothing here has an ISIN (Section 06).

IR Forward Debt (CONTRACT ON A SECURITY):
  Bank Of Toronto agrees TODAY to buy CAD 10,000,000 face value
  of a Government of Canada bond in 3 months, at a forward price
  fixed today. Unless it's cash-settled, in 3 months an ACTUAL
  bond (Module 5 §3's real, transferable security) moves via DvP
  -- the contract's underlying is real and has its own ISIN, even
  though the FORWARD itself is a contract, not the security.

-> This is the Rates asset class's version of Module 6 §3's
   "contracts all the way down" finding -- except one of the five
   products isn't quite all the way down; it bottoms out in an
   actual bond.
Takeaway. IR Forward Debt is the reason Section 06's identifier table needs an extra row that none of Modules 5, 6, or 7 required — a single trade record can carry a UPI/UTI for the forward contract itself and an ISIN for the bond it may eventually deliver, and both are correct at the same time.

04 · Buyer → Seller → Trade

Real-World Trade Examples

Five trades — one for each product, with the FRA's upfront discounting and the Forward Debt's physical delivery as the two genuine exceptions to the vanilla-IRS pattern.

Complete worked examples available. This module's products form a connected lesson chain, each with its own full standalone example: Interest Rate Swap (IRS) → FRA → Cap → Floor → Collar → Swaption → Cross-Currency Swap.

4a. Interest Rate Swap

Interest Rate Swap · 5Y CADReused from Module 1 §8
Bank Of Toronto:  pays FIXED, receives FLOATING (CORRA + spread)
Goldman Sachs:     pays FLOATING, receives FIXED

Notional (reference only, NEVER exchanged): CAD 100,000,000
Term: 5 years, quarterly payments
Fixed Rate: 4.00%

ONE QUARTER'S SETTLEMENT — 91-day quarter, actual/365, CORRA
averages 3.20% over the quarter + 0.05% spread = 3.25% floating

Bank Of Toronto's FIXED leg = 100,000,000 x 4.00% x 91/365 = CAD 997,260.27
Goldman Sachs' FLOATING leg = 100,000,000 x 3.25% x 91/365 = CAD 810,273.97

Net cash flow: Bank Of Toronto PAYS Goldman Sachs
  997,260.27 - 810,273.97 = CAD 186,986.30
-> only this NET amount moves; the CAD 100,000,000 notional never
   settles at all, exactly Module 1 §8's original point.

4b. FRA (Forward Rate Agreement)

FRA · 3x6 on CORRAUpfront discounted settlement
Bank Of Toronto: BUYS the FRA — pays FIXED (the "FRA Rate"),
                  receives FLOATING (3-month CORRA)
Counterparty: Goldman Sachs

Notional: CAD 20,000,000
FRA Rate (locked today): 4.85%
Reference period: 91 days, actual/365

AT FIXING (3 months from trade date) — 3-month CORRA settles at 4.50%
(rates fell since the FRA was agreed)

Undiscounted difference = 20,000,000 x (4.85% - 4.50%) x 91/365
                         = 20,000,000 x 0.0035 x 0.249315
                         = CAD 17,452.05

Discounted settlement (paid NOW, at the START of the reference
period, not its end):
  = 17,452.05 / (1 + 4.50% x 91/365)
  = 17,452.05 / 1.011219
  = CAD 17,259.79

Bank Of Toronto PAYS Goldman Sachs CAD 17,259.79 -- because Bank
Of Toronto locked in 4.85% fixed but the market floating rate
settled lower (4.50%), Bank Of Toronto's FRA turned out to be a
worse deal than just floating, so it compensates the counterparty.
IT/reporting angle: this discounting step is the single field most likely to be coded wrong by someone who's only built IRS logic before — an IRS's periodic cash flow settles at the END of its period; an FRA's single cash flow settles at the START of its period (the rate is already known by then), which means it must be present-valued back to that start date using the very rate that was just fixed.

4c. Cap/Floor

Interest Rate Cap · 2Y on 3M CORRA8 caplets
Buyer: Bank Of Toronto
Seller (writer): Goldman Sachs

Notional: CAD 15,000,000, Strike (Cap Rate): 5.00%
8 quarterly caplets over 2 years
Premium: 0.85% of notional, paid upfront = CAD 127,500

CAPLET #3 RESET — 3-month CORRA settles at 5.40% (above strike)
  Payout = 15,000,000 x (5.40% - 5.00%) x 91/365
         = 15,000,000 x 0.004 x 0.249315
         = CAD 1,495.89 -- paid to Bank Of Toronto

CAPLET #4 RESET — 3-month CORRA settles at 4.70% (below strike)
  Payout = CAD 0 -- this caplet simply doesn't pay; Bank Of
  Toronto's downside was already capped at the premium paid,
  exactly the asymmetric-payoff pattern from every Option product
  in this roadmap so far (Module 4 §4d, Module 6 §4d, Module 7 §4d)

4d. Swaption

Swaption · 1Y-into-5Y PayerOption on an IRS
Buyer: Bank Of Toronto (buys the RIGHT to enter a 5Y IRS, as
       fixed-rate PAYER, in 1 year)
Seller (writer): Goldman Sachs

Notional: CAD 50,000,000, Strike: 4.25% fixed
Expiry: 1 year, Underlying Swap Term: 5 years from expiry
Premium: 0.60% of notional, paid upfront = CAD 300,000

AT EXPIRY (1 year) -- Scenario A: prevailing 5Y swap rate = 4.75%
  Exercise: Bank Of Toronto enters the 5Y IRS paying only 4.25%
  fixed, when the market rate is 4.75% -- a favourable swap it
  could not otherwise get. In-the-money by construction.

AT EXPIRY -- Scenario B: prevailing 5Y swap rate = 3.90%
  Don't exercise: entering a FRESH swap in the market at 3.90%
  fixed beats exercising the swaption at 4.25%. Swaption expires
  WORTHLESS; Bank Of Toronto's loss is capped at the CAD 300,000
  premium -- never more, same maximum-loss pattern as every other
  option product in this roadmap.

4e. IR Forward Debt

IR Forward Debt · Government of Canada BondForward purchase
Buyer: Bank Of Toronto (agrees TODAY to buy in 3 months)
Seller: Goldman Sachs

Underlying: Canada 5% Bond 2035 (ISIN CA1358XXXXX — Module 5 §4a)
Face Value: CAD 10,000,000
Forward Price (fixed today): 97.80 (reflecting today's spot price
  of 98.50 minus 3 months of carry -- coupon income the SELLER
  keeps for holding the bond those 3 months, a mechanic closer to
  Module 6 §1's forward points than to Module 7 §1's contango)

IN 3 MONTHS, if physically settled:
  Bank Of Toronto pays 10,000,000 x 97.80 / 100 = CAD 9,780,000
  Bank Of Toronto receives CAD 10,000,000 face value of the bond
  via DvP (Module 1 §7) -- an ACTUAL security moves, unlike 4a-4d
Takeaway. Five products, one recurring mechanic — a fixed rate (or fixed price, for 4e) locked in today against something that resets or is observed later, with only the difference (or, for 4e, the full principal if physically settled) actually moving. The FRA's upfront discounting (4b) and the Forward Debt's physical bond delivery (4e) are the two genuine exceptions, and both are exactly where a reporting system is most likely to get the mechanics wrong.

          UTI (this specific trade) — Section 06
Counterparty after clearing. Once novated, Section 04a's CAD 186,986.30 net quarterly flow doesn't move directly between Bank Of Toronto and Goldman Sachs — it moves Bank Of Toronto ↔ LCH SwapClear and, on a separate leg, LCH SwapClear ↔ Goldman Sachs. Section 04a's diagram is still economically correct about the amount and direction; it's just describing the trade's original bilateral terms, before the CLEARING stage above redirects who that amount is actually owed to.
Takeaway. A single trade generates 20 separate quarterly settlement events over its life, each needing its own net cash flow calculation off a then-current CORRA reset — nothing else in this roadmap has a lifecycle that repeats its own Settlement stage that many times from one Trade Capture event.
Deep dive available. An IRS position is pure notional referenced inside a live contract, not a held quantity — one row per contract, valued off its own terms. For the full theory (and the same pattern for CDS), see Module 25 — Position Deep Dive §6.

06 · UPI, UTI, LEI — and one ISIN exception

Identifiers — UPI, UTI, LEI & the IR Forward Debt Exception

Like FX (Module 6 §6), four of the five Rates products have no security to identify — but IR Forward Debt genuinely does, because of Section 03's finding.

IdentifierApplies to Rates?Example
ISIN / CUSIP / Ticker / FIGINo for IRS, FRA, Cap/Floor, Swaption — pure contracts (Section 03)—
ISINYes, but only for the underlying bond on IR Forward DebtCA1358XXXXX — identifies the Canada 5% Bond 2035 the forward may deliver, not the forward itself
UPIYes, for all five products, as OTC derivative product templates (Module 2 §12)One UPI for "5Y CAD Fixed-Float IRS referencing CORRA" (Module 2 §12), reused across every trade sharing that shape
UTIYes — every individual trade across all five products gets its ownA fresh UTI per trade, same logic as every prior module

Takeaway. An IR Forward Debt trade record carrying both a UPI/UTI (for the forward contract) and an ISIN (for the underlying bond) is the healthy, expected pattern — not the red flag Module 5 §6 raised about a UPI appearing on a plain bond record. Two real things genuinely coexist on one trade: a contract and (potentially) the security it delivers.

07 · OTC-negotiated, but mandatorily cleared

Exchange vs OTC vs Bilateral vs Centrally Cleared

Rates is where "OTC but centrally cleared" — a growing, optional trend for Commodity Swaps (Module 7 §7) — becomes an outright regulatory mandate for standardized IRS.

ProductExchange or OTC?Bilateral or Centrally Cleared?
Interest Rate Swap (standardized)OTC-negotiated (often via a SEF)Mandatorily centrally cleared (LCH SwapClear, CME) under EMIR/Dodd-Frank — Section 08
FRAOTCMostly bilateral; less standardized than IRS, clearing less commonly mandated

Takeaway. This table has the same shape as Module 7 §7's Commodities table — one product (IRS here, Futures there) carries the asset class's central-clearing volume, while the rest stays bilateral OTC. The mechanism differs (Commodities' Futures clear because they're exchange-traded by design; Rates' IRS clears because regulation mandates it) but the resulting pattern repeats.

08 · EMIR's mandatory clearing obligation, headline fact

Regulatory Perspective — MiFID, EMIR, SFTR, FCA, CIRO, HKMA, DTCC, DMO

EMIR's central clearing mandate for standardized IRS is the defining regulatory fact of this module.

Regulation / BodyHow it touches Rates specifically
MiFID II / MiFIR (EU)Trading-venue rules for standardized IRS (mandating on-venue execution for liquid swaps), plus transaction reporting
EMIRThe defining regulation for this module — sets the mandatory central clearing obligation for standardized IRS (Section 07), on top of the general OTC derivative reporting obligation that also covers FRA, Cap/Floor, Swaption, and IR Forward Debt
SFTRNot applicable — none of these five products are securities financing transactions (contrast Module 5 §08's repo)
FCA (UK)Regulates UK-cleared IRS activity; London/LCH SwapClear is the single largest global venue for IRS clearing, giving the FCA/Bank of England outsized relevance here, similar in spirit to Module 6 §08's FX-volume point
CIRO (Canada)SRO overseeing Canadian rates derivatives dealer conduct
HKMA (Hong Kong)Runs Hong Kong's own mandatory clearing and reporting regime for OTC derivatives including IRS — a genuine regulator role here, distinct from its currency-peg role in FX (Module 6 §08) or bond-issuer role in Fixed Income (Module 5 §08)
DTCCIts GTR is one of the largest global trade repositories for IRS/FRA/Swaption reporting, alongside REGIS-TR and UnaVista in the EU/UK
DMO (UK Debt Management Office)Relevant here specifically via IR Forward Debt on Gilts — the DMO's issuance calendar and outstanding Gilt stock is exactly what a Gilt forward references, the first module where DMO is genuinely on-topic for a derivative, not just the underlying cash bond (Module 5 §08)
Takeaway. EMIR's mandatory clearing obligation is this module's headline regulatory fact — it turns Section 07's "OTC but centrally cleared" pattern from a market convention (as with Commodity Swaps, Module 7 §07) into a hard legal requirement for standardized IRS, explaining why an IRS trade record shows a CCP counterparty while a Swaption record on the same desk shows a bilateral one.

Self-check · Solutions

Recap Quiz — Answers & Explanations

Check your work against these before moving on.

Q1 — Goldman Sachs pays Bank Of Toronto CAD 87,260.28 — a reversal of direction from the worked example
Math · Floating Above Fixed5Y CAD IRS
Bank Of Toronto's FIXED leg = 100,000,000 x 4.00% x 91/365 = CAD 997,260.27
Goldman Sachs' FLOATING leg = 100,000,000 x 4.35% x 91/365 = CAD 1,084,520.55
Net: Goldman Sachs PAYS Bank Of Toronto = 1,084,520.55 - 997,260.27 = CAD 87,260.28

Whenever the floating rate settles above the fixed rate, the fixed-rate payer (Bank Of Toronto) becomes the net receiver — the same fixed-vs-floating netting mechanic Module 1 §8 established, just running in the opposite direction because CORRA came in higher this quarter.

Q2 — An FRA's cash flow is paid at the START of its reference period, so it must be present-valued back to that earlier date; an IRS's cash flow is paid at the END of each period, which is already the natural date

Section 04b's IT/reporting angle states this directly: this is the single field most likely to be coded wrong by someone porting IRS valuation logic to FRAs without accounting for the different settlement-timing convention.

Q3 — The payout is CAD 0 — at exactly the strike, there's no positive difference to pay on

Payout = Notional x (Rate - Strike) x Days/365 — when Rate equals Strike, (Rate - Strike) = 0, so the payout is zero regardless of notional or day count. This mirrors Module 6 §4d's Q5 finding for the at-the-money FX option: hitting the strike exactly produces the boundary case of the payoff, not a partial one.

Q4 — A Payer Swaption profits when it can pay a strike BELOW the market rate; a Receiver Swaption needs the opposite move to be favourable

Section 04d's Scenario A exercises because entering the underlying swap at the old, lower strike beats entering a fresh swap at the new, higher market rate — but that logic only holds for the side that wants to lock in the LOWER of the two rates (the fixed-rate payer). A Receiver Swaption holder wants to lock in the HIGHER rate as a receiver, so a market rate below strike would be what favours it instead — the same 4.75% outcome would make a Receiver Swaption worthless.

Q5 — More likely an IR Forward Debt trade — Section 06 established ISIN only ever appears on Rates products for the underlying bond a forward may deliver

This does NOT contradict Module 5 §6's warning, because that warning was specifically about a UPI appearing on a Fixed Income module's BOND record (ISIN + UPI on the same schema/record as a red flag) — here, the ISIN and UPI/UTI describe two conceptually different things on one trade (the forward contract and its potential underlying delivery), which Section 03 and Section 06 both establish as the expected, healthy pattern for this specific product. The distinguishing test: is the ISIN on the SAME schema entity as the derivative fields (suspicious), or clearly scoped to a distinct underlying-security reference within an IR Forward Debt record (expected)?

Q6 — Mechanically different (venue design vs. regulatory mandate), structurally identical (one standardized product carries the whole asset class's CCP-cleared volume)
Commodities Futures clear via a CCP purely because they're exchange-traded BY DESIGN from the moment of execution (Module 7 §05 — a structural/venue fact); Rates IRS clear via a CCP because EMIR/Dodd-Frank LEGALLY REQUIRE it for standardized OTC-negotiated swaps (Section 08 — a regulatory fact). Section 07's takeaway draws this comparison explicitly: same resulting table shape, reached by two completely different causal routes.
Q7 — EMIR's mandatory central clearing obligation is responsible; EMIR does NOT require the same mandatory clearing for a Swaption on the same desk

Section 08's takeaway calls this out as the module's headline regulatory fact: EMIR's mandate is scoped to specific, standardized product types (vanilla IRS being the clearest example), not to "Rates derivatives" as a blanket category — Section 07's table shows Swaptions remain predominantly bilateral, since they're less standardized than a vanilla IRS and generally sit outside the specific product scope EMIR's clearing mandate targets.

Q8 — LCH SwapClear is the dominant global CCP for centrally-cleared IRS across many currencies including CAD; CDCC clears MX-listed CAD rate futures domestically because those trade on a Canadian venue
Section 09's takeaway states this directly: currency denomination and clearing location are independent facts — a CAD-denominated OTC swap's clearing follows the CCP the product family uses globally (LCH SwapClear), while an exchange-listed CAD future's clearing follows the CCP attached to its listing venue (CDCC, attached to MX). A system that assumes "CAD-denominated therefore Canadian CCP" gets the OTC case backwards.
