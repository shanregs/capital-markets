01 · The business scenario

Purpose & Business Scenario

This module walks one complete OTC derivative trade — a USD/CAD FX Forward — from Trade Capture through Confirmation, Enrichment, Valuation, MTM, Collateral, Settlement, and Regulatory Reporting, covering both the business lifecycle and the data/fields each stage generates or consumes.

RoleParty
BankBank Of Toronto
Client / CounterpartyABC Canada Inc.
TradeUSD/CAD FX Forward
ProductFX Forward
PurposeHedge future USD payment

ABC Canada expects to pay a US supplier USD 10,000,000 on 30-Nov-2026. ABC is concerned that USD/CAD may rise, making the future USD payment more expensive in CAD terms, and therefore enters an OTC FX Forward with Bank Of Toronto, executed on 30-Aug-2026.

Trade EconomicsExecuted 30-Aug-2026
Trade Date              : 30-Aug-2026
Value Date / Maturity   : 30-Nov-2026
Currency Pair           : USD/CAD
Buy Currency            : USD          Sell Currency: CAD
USD Notional            : USD 10,000,000
Forward Rate            : CAD 1.3545 per USD
CAD Notional            : CAD 13,545,000
Settlement              : Physical (Deliverable Forward)
Business Purpose        : Hedge
Direction (ABC)         : Buy USD / Sell CAD
Governing Agreement     : ISDA Master Agreement + CSA (Module 12)
ISDA Taxonomy Path      : Foreign Exchange / Forward / Deliverable / Forward
Hedge Designation       : Yes -- hedges a known future USD payable (Module 22 §9)

ABC has locked in: USD 10,000,000 x CAD 1.3545 = CAD 13,545,000
Callout — USD/CAD is a G10, fully convertible pair, so Deliverable is the default. Not every FX Forward needs a Deliverable-vs-NDF decision the way an emerging-market currency does. CAD, like every other G10 currency, has no capital-account restrictions, so a USD/CAD forward — like this trade — almost always settles physically. Contrast this with the INR example this module was originally built around: because INR is not fully convertible, USD/INR forwards split into an onshore Deliverable Forward (via an RBI-authorized dealer bank) versus an offshore NDF, which settles only a USD cash difference. The NDF Flag field kept on this trade's data model (Section 04) is still worth carrying on every FX Forward record for exactly that reason — it reads N on almost every G10 trade like this one, but the same schema needs to be ready for the day it carries an EM currency pair where the answer genuinely varies.
Takeaway. Every field this module tracks downstream — enrichment, valuation, collateral, settlement, regulatory reporting — traces back to this one trade record. Nothing about the scenario is exotic: it's the single most common reason a corporate treasury enters an FX Forward at all.

05 · What is it worth today?

Stage 4 — Valuation

The trade was agreed at a forward rate of 1.3545 CAD/USD, but the market forward rate changes every day. Suppose shortly afterward the current market 3-month USD/CAD forward rate is 1.3650 — the original trade has become valuable to the party that buys USD at 1.3545.

Simplified FX Forward Valuation
MTM ~ Notional x (Current Forward Rate - Contract Forward Rate)
      (for a USD buyer, ignoring discounting and other conventions)

Notional = USD 10,000,000
Contract Rate = CAD 1.3545
Current Forward Rate = CAD 1.3650

Difference = 1.3650 - 1.3545 = CAD 0.0105/USD

Approximate value = 10,000,000 x 0.0105 = CAD 105,000

-> ABC's position is economically favorable.

A production valuation engine does not simply use Notional × rate difference — it considers the current spot FX rate, forward FX points/curve, remaining maturity, interest-rate curves, discount factors, settlement convention, market data timestamp, and CVA/FVA where applicable:

Conceptual PV Formula
PV = Expected future cash-flow difference x Discount Factor
FieldExample
Valuation Date2026-09-01
Spot / Forward USD/CAD1.3490 / 1.3650
Contract Rate1.3545
Remaining Days90
Discount Factor0.985
Present ValueCAD 103,425
Pricing Source / ModelMarket Data Service / FX Forward PV
Takeaway. The exact FX-forward pricing methodology depends on the bank's own valuation framework, but every version answers the same question — what would this trade cost to replace, at today's market rates, discounted back to today.

06 · Who's gaining, who's losing

Stage 5 — MTM

MTM = Mark-to-Market. It answers: "if we valued this trade using today's market prices, how much would we gain or lose?" The signs reverse between counterparties because they hold opposite positions.

Simplified MTM
Trade Rate = 1.3545 | Market Forward Rate = 1.3650 | Notional = USD 10M

(1.3650 - 1.3545) x 10M = CAD 105,000

ABC perspective:              +CAD 105,000
Bank Of Toronto perspective:  -CAD 105,000
DateMarket Forward RateApprox. MTM to USD Buyer
30-Aug1.35450
01-Sep1.3650+105,000 CAD
30-Sep1.3400-145,000 CAD
31-Oct1.3750+205,000 CAD
30-Nov1.3900+355,000 CAD
Takeaway. The trade can move from positive to negative and back during its life — this is exactly why daily valuation matters, and exactly the swing Section 07's collateral mechanism exists to manage.

07 · Managing counterparty credit risk

Stage 6 — Collateral

OTC derivatives create counterparty credit risk — if Bank Of Toronto owes ABC a large amount and Bank Of Toronto defaults, ABC loses that value. To reduce this risk, parties exchange collateral under a CSA (Credit Support Annex), as cash, government securities, or other eligible collateral.

ConceptWhat it protects against
Variation Margin (VM)Reflects changes in the current value of positions — MTM increases, collateral requirement may increase
Initial Margin (IM)Protection against potential future exposure during the period needed to close/replace positions after a default; applicability depends on counterparties, regulation, and documentation
Example Collateral Calculation
Portfolio MTM = +CAD 105,000 to ABC
Threshold = CAD 50,000
Minimum Transfer Amount (MTA) = CAD 10,000

Simplified collateral requirement = 105,000 - 50,000 = CAD 55,000

-> collateral processing is generally portfolio/CSA based, not a
   standalone per-trade calculation
FieldExample
CSA IDCSA-BOT-ABC-001
Portfolio Value / Threshold / MTACAD 105,000 / CAD 50,000 / CAD 10,000
Collateral RequirementCAD 55,000
Collateral Type / CurrencyCash / CAD
Margin TypeVM
Call / Settlement Date2026-09-01 / 2026-09-02
Collateral / Dispute StatusCALLED / NONE
Takeaway. Collateral exists to protect against exactly the MTM swings Section 06 showed — a trade with zero MTM at inception still needs a CSA in place, because the risk it protects against is a future swing, not today's snapshot.

09 · What must be reported

11 · Trade, product, counterparty

Identifiers & Data Modeling

Identifiers are extremely important when designing a regulatory data platform (RDP) — Trade ID, UTI, UPI, and LEI each answer a genuinely different question.

Four Identifiers, Four Questions
LEI  -> identifies Counterparty / Reporting Entity
UTI  -> identifies the Transaction (BOT-FX-20260830-00012345 internally;
        5493001KJTIIGC8Y1R1220260830FX00012345 for regulatory pairing)
UPI  -> identifies the Product (FX9M3RXQ2K18)
QuestionAnswer for this trade
Product — "What is being traded?"FX Forward
Counterparty — "Who is involved?"Bank Of Toronto, ABC Canada Inc.
Trade — "What specific transaction occurred?"Trade ID BOT-FX-20260830-00012345
Position — "What exposure remains?"Depends on all transactions netted together, not this trade alone

Takeaway. Trade, Product, Counterparty, and Position are four genuinely different questions a data model must answer separately — collapsing any two of them into one field is a common design mistake in an RDP.

12 · Microservices & Kafka

Event-Driven Architecture

For an RDP architecture, an event-oriented model is often useful — each stage of the lifecycle becomes an event other services can react to independently.

Event-Based Model
TradeCreated -> TradeConfirmed -> TradeEnriched -> TradeValued
    -> MTMUpdated -> CollateralUpdated -> SettlementInitiated
    -> SettlementCompleted -> RegulatoryReported

Each event can carry: Event ID, Trade ID, UTI, Event Type,
Event Timestamp, Source System, Previous/New State, Version,
Correlation ID, Payload
ServiceRole
trade-serviceRoot of the decomposition
confirmation / enrichment / valuation / risk / collateral / settlement / regulatory-serviceOne microservice per lifecycle stage
reference-data / counterparty / market-data / identifier / audit / notification-serviceSupporting services consumed by the above
Example Kafka Event Flow
trade.created       -> confirmation-service, enrichment-service,
                       risk-service, regulatory-service

valuation.updated   -> risk-service, collateral-service,
                       regulatory-service

settlement.completed -> accounting-service, regulatory-service,
                        audit-service
Takeaway. An event bus lets each downstream consumer react to the same underlying change independently and at its own pace — a valuation.updated event fans out to three services simultaneously, rather than forcing one blocking synchronous chain.

13 · Static vs. dynamic data

What Changes During the Lifecycle, and the Full Timeline

The trade's core economics generally remain the same — but dynamic fields (market spot, forward curve, PV, MTM, exposure, collateral, settlement/reporting status) change constantly, which is why an RDP should distinguish static/contractual data from dynamic data.

Static / contractual dataDynamic data
Trade ID, Product, Notional, Currency, Contract Rate, Trade Date, Maturity Date, CounterpartiesValuation, MTM, Market Data, Exposure, Collateral, Settlement Status, Reporting Status
Full Example Timeline
30-Aug-2026
  10:32 Trade executed -> 10:33 Captured -> 10:34 Confirmed
  10:35 Enrichment completed -> 10:36 Regulatory IDs generated
  End of day: Trade valued, MTM calculated

01-Sep-2026
  Market moves -> Forward curve changes -> Trade revalued
  -> MTM changes -> Collateral requirement recalculated
  -> Collateral call generated if required

30-Nov-2026
  Maturity -> Settlement instructions validated
  -> USD payment + CAD payment -> Settlement completed
  -> Trade closed -> Final regulatory lifecycle event
Takeaway. Most of what makes an OTC derivative operationally interesting happens AFTER trade date — the contractual terms are frozen at execution, but the dynamic fields keep every downstream system busy for the trade's entire life.

Self-check · Solutions

Recap Quiz — Answers & Explanations

Check your work against these.

Q1 — CAD, like every other G10 currency, is fully convertible with no capital-account restrictions, so both currencies can and do physically exchange at maturity without special authorization; the NDF Flag field (N) captures this directly, and would flip to Y for a currency like INR that isn't fully convertible

Section 01's callout contrasts the G10 default (CAD) against the INR example the module was originally built around.

Q2 — Any two of: LEIs (which entities), UTI (which transaction, for regulatory pairing), UPI (which product template), ISDA Taxonomy Path (how the product is classified), Hedge Designation (is this formally linked to an offsetting exposure) — none exist on the raw capture record

Section 04's enriched-trade example shows these fields appearing only after enrichment.

Q3 — MTM measures value against today's market forward rate, not the fixed contract rate; the contract rate never moves, but the market's own forward rate moves every day
Section 05's formula treats Contract Forward Rate as a constant and Current Forward Rate as the only variable.
Q4 — Collateral reduces counterparty credit risk — that whichever party owes money on MTM doesn't pay if it defaults; this exists even at zero initial MTM because value can swing significantly by maturity

Section 06's timeline shows the same trade ranging from -145,000 to +355,000 CAD over its life.

Q5 — Only a single net USD cash payment would move, calculated against a reference fixing rate — no CAD would ever be exchanged (even though a USD/CAD trade essentially never is booked this way in practice)

Section 01's callout and Section 08's physical settlement diagram, read together, describe exactly this contrast.

Q6 — Different downstream consumers (risk, collateral, regulatory, accounting) each need to react to the same change independently and at their own pace, without a single blocking chain

Section 12's Kafka flow shows one valuation.updated event fanning out to three services simultaneously.

Q7 — The UPI identifies WHICH issued product-reference-data record matches; the ISDA Taxonomy Path is the human-readable classification the UPI's reference data was built from, telling a downstream system the asset class/base product/sub-product/transaction type without a lookup

Module 21 §5 covers this exact lineage — the taxonomy is the "grammar," the UPI reference data is the "dictionary" built from it.
