## 1. The Trade — Economic Terms

```text
5-YEAR CAD INTEREST RATE SWAP — ECONOMIC TERMS
========================================================================
  Trade ID:            BOT-IRS-2026-034410
  Trade Date:           2026-08-25  (Tuesday)
  Effective Date:       2026-08-27  (Trade Date + 2 business days — "spot start")
  Maturity Date:        2031-08-27  (Effective Date + 5 years)
  Notional:              CAD 100,000,000  (reference only — NEVER exchanged, Module 1 §8)
  Currency:              CAD

  Bank Of Toronto:       pays FIXED, receives FLOATING (CORRA + spread)
  Goldman Sachs:         pays FLOATING (CORRA + spread), receives FIXED

  Fixed Rate:            4.00% per annum
  Fixed Leg Frequency:   Quarterly
  Fixed Leg Day Count:   Actual/365 (fixed) — standard for CAD swaps

  Floating Index:        CORRA (Canadian Overnight Repo Rate Average — Module 8 §1)
  Floating Spread:       +0.05% (5 basis points), applied to Bank Of Toronto's
                          receive leg
  Floating Compounding:  Daily, compounded IN ARREARS over each quarter
  Floating Leg Frequency: Quarterly (same payment dates as the fixed leg)
  Floating Leg Day Count: Actual/365

  Business Day Convention: Modified Following
  Payment Lag:            2 business days after each Accrual End Date
                           (Section 4 explains why the floating leg needs this lag)

  Clearing:                Centrally cleared via LCH SwapClear (CCP) — mandatory
                           for a standardized CAD IRS under EMIR/Dodd-Frank
                           (Section 4 covers the novation mechanics and how it
                           changes every money flow described in this document)
```

**A note on precision in this document.** To keep the arithmetic auditable by hand, every quarter below is treated as a uniform 91-day accrual period (the same simplification [Module 8](./module-08-rates.md) §4a uses) and calendar dates are generated by adding 3 calendar months per period without walking every date through a real holiday calendar. In production, both the day-count fraction (which genuinely varies 90–92 days per real quarter) and the business-day-adjusted dates are generated by the trade system's schedule engine, not computed by hand — treat the dates and day counts here as illustrative, and the rate/formula mechanics as exact.

## 2. How the Fixed Leg Is Calculated

The fixed leg never changes for the life of the trade — the only inputs are Notional, the agreed Fixed Rate, and the day-count fraction of each period:

```text
Fixed Leg Accrual = Notional x Fixed Rate x (Accrual Days / 365)

  = 100,000,000 x 4.00% x (91 / 365)
  = 100,000,000 x 0.04 x 0.249315...
  = CAD 997,260.27

-> This SAME amount recurs every one of the 20 quarters, because the
   fixed rate and notional never change. It is the one number in this
   entire worked example that doesn't need to be recalculated per period.
```

## 3. How the Floating Leg Is Calculated — CORRA Compounded in Arrears + Spread

**Why "compounded in arrears" and not "reset in advance."** Under the old CDOR/LIBOR-style regime, a floating leg's rate was *set once, at the start* of each period, and stayed fixed for the whole quarter — the rate was known in advance. CORRA (like SOFR, €STR, SONIA — [Module 8](./module-08-rates.md) §1) is an **overnight** rate, so a quarterly floating leg instead **compounds the daily overnight CORRA prints** observed across the whole period, day by day, and the compounded rate for the period is only fully known once the period has *ended*. This is the single biggest mechanical difference IT systems had to absorb in the LIBOR-to-RFR transition.

```text
COMPOUNDED-IN-ARREARS FORMULA

  Compounded Factor = PRODUCT over every calendar day i in the period
                       of  [ 1 + r_i x (d_i / 365) ]

    where r_i  = that day's published overnight CORRA rate
          d_i  = the number of calendar days that rate applies to
                 (1 for a weekday; 3 for a Friday print, which also
                 covers Saturday and Sunday under the standard
                 "non-business day carries the prior day's rate" rule)

  Compounded Rate (annualized) = (Compounded Factor - 1) x (365 / D)

    where D = total calendar days in the period (91, in this example)
```

This produces a single annualized rate for the whole quarter — economically very close to a simple daily average at these rate levels, but not identical to one, which is why "CORRA averaged 3.20% this quarter" (the shorthand this document and [Module 8](./module-08-rates.md) §4a both use) really means "the daily-compounded rate over the period came out to 3.20%," not a plain arithmetic mean.

**Why the +0.05% spread exists.** A swap is normally priced so its value at inception is zero (a "par swap") — the fixed rate that makes that true is rarely a clean round number. Rather than quote an awkward fixed rate like 3.9713%, a desk commonly quotes a clean round Fixed Rate (4.00% here) and absorbs the remaining basis as a small spread added to the floating leg instead. The spread is agreed once, at trade date, and — unlike CORRA itself — never changes for the life of the trade.

**Worked example — Period 1's floating leg:**

```text
Floating Leg Accrual = Notional x (Compounded CORRA + Spread) x (Accrual Days / 365)

  Compounded CORRA over Period 1:  3.20%
  + Spread:                        0.05%
  = Floating Rate Applied:         3.25%

  = 100,000,000 x 3.25% x (91 / 365)
  = 100,000,000 x 0.0325 x 0.249315...
  = CAD 810,273.97
```

**IT/reporting angle:** because the compounded rate isn't fully known until the accrual period itself ends, a floating-leg accrual amount literally *cannot* be calculated — let alone invoiced or settled — before the last CORRA print of the period is published. That is exactly why Section 1's terms include a **2-business-day payment lag** after every Accrual End Date: it's the operational buffer a back-office system needs to pull the final daily CORRA prints, run the compounding formula above, generate the settlement amount, and release payment instructions — not an arbitrary convention.

## 4. Netting &amp; Settlement Mechanics — What Actually Moves, and When

### Clearing — Novation to LCH SwapClear

As Section 1 states, this trade is centrally cleared — not a hypothetical, this is the actual status carried on the Section 7 trade record (`clearingStatus: Cleared`, `ccpId: LCH SwapClear`). Shortly after execution, Bank Of Toronto and Goldman Sachs each submit the trade to **LCH SwapClear** — LCH's dedicated clearing service for interest rate swaps ([Module 3](./module-03-trade-lifecycle.md) §6 covers the general novation mechanism; this is that same mechanism applied to this specific trade). Novation **extinguishes** the original bilateral contract and **replaces** it with two new, economically mirrored contracts:

```text
BEFORE NOVATION  —  one bilateral trade
========================================================================
  Bank Of Toronto  <====  5Y CAD IRS, Notional CAD 100,000,000  ====>  Goldman Sachs
       (pays fixed 4.00% / receives floating CORRA + 0.05%)

AFTER NOVATION  —  LCH SwapClear steps in; TWO trades now exist
========================================================================
  Bank Of Toronto      <====  IRS Leg #1, CAD 100,000,000  ====>  LCH SwapClear (CCP)
       (pays fixed 4.00% / receives floating CORRA + 0.05%)

  LCH SwapClear (CCP)  <====  IRS Leg #2, CAD 100,000,000  ====>  Goldman Sachs
       (pays fixed 4.00% / receives floating CORRA + 0.05% — mirror of Leg #1)

  -> The original Bank Of Toronto <-> Goldman Sachs contract no longer
     exists; it was legally extinguished by novation. Every Fixed/
     Floating accrual and Net Amount calculated in Sections 2, 3, and
     5 below now settles TWICE — once per leg — never once directly
     between the two banks. Bank Of Toronto's only counterparty for
     the remaining life of the trade is LCH SwapClear; if Goldman
     Sachs defaulted, LCH SwapClear absorbs the loss and Bank Of
     Toronto's own leg is unaffected.
```

Both legs mirror each other exactly in rate, notional, and dates, so LCH SwapClear takes on no net *market* risk from this trade — it collects on one leg exactly what it pays on the other. What it does take on is **counterparty credit risk** from each bank individually, which is why it requires Initial Margin from both sides at novation, plus daily Variation Margin thereafter (full mechanics in [Module 12](./module-12-collateral-margin.md); the novation mechanism itself in [Module 3](./module-03-trade-lifecycle.md) §6).

### The Netting Calculation — Applied Identically to Each Leg

```text
NETTING, EVERY QUARTER  (same math on each of the two novated legs)
========================================================================
  Fixed Leg Accrual (fixed-rate payer owes the other side)   CAD 997,260.27
  Floating Leg Accrual (floating-rate payer owes the other)  CAD 810,273.97  (Period 1)
                                                              --------------
  Net Settlement = Fixed - Floating = 997,260.27 - 810,273.97 = CAD 186,986.30

  -> Only this ONE net amount moves, in ONE direction, per leg, on the
     Payment Date. Neither side of either leg ever transfers its own
     gross accrual — that is purely an intermediate calculation. The
     CAD 100,000,000 notional itself never moves at all, on this date
     or any other, on either leg (Module 1 §8).
```

```text
MONEY FLOW — PERIOD 1  (post-novation, via LCH SwapClear)
========================================================================
   Bank Of Toronto  --CAD 186,986.30 (net)-->  LCH SwapClear (CCP)  --CAD 186,986.30 (net)-->  Goldman Sachs
   (fixed-rate payer, Leg #1)                                       (floating-rate payer, Leg #2)

   Settlement Date: 2026-11-29  (Accrual End 2026-11-27 + 2 business days)

   -> Two separate settlement instructions fire on the same date, for
      the same amount, in the same direction. LCH SwapClear passes the
      amount straight through and is never left holding a net position
      on this trade — the same "collects on one leg what it pays on
      the other" property noted above.
```

**Sign convention used throughout Section 5's schedule:** a **positive** Net Amount means the Fixed leg is larger, so **Bank Of Toronto pays LCH SwapClear (Leg #1), and LCH SwapClear pays Goldman Sachs (Leg #2)**. A **negative** Net Amount means the Floating leg is larger, so the direction reverses on both legs — **Goldman Sachs pays LCH SwapClear, and LCH SwapClear pays Bank Of Toronto**. Section 5's table names the original bilateral direction ("Bank Of Toronto → Goldman Sachs") for economic clarity; operationally it is always two legs through the CCP, never one direct transfer — the same fixed-vs-floating reversal mechanic [Module 8](./module-08-rates.md) §4a's Recap Quiz Answer 1 walks through for a single quarter.

This is precisely the economic reason a treasury desk pays fixed on an IRS in the first place: it is a hedge that costs money while rates stay low, and pays off once rates rise past the locked-in fixed rate.

## 6. Final Settlement at Maturity

The Period 20 row above **is** the final settlement — there is no separate "maturity payment" beyond it:

```text
FINAL SETTLEMENT — 2031-08-29 (Period 20's Payment Date)
========================================================================
  Goldman Sachs --CAD 49,863.02--> LCH SwapClear --CAD 49,863.02--> Bank Of Toronto
  (the same two-leg routing Section 4 established, applied one last time)

  On the SAME date, the trade reaches its Maturity Date (2031-08-27,
  two business days earlier) and simply EXPIRES:

  - The CAD 100,000,000 Notional is NEVER exchanged -- not on this
    date, not on any of the prior 19 Payment Dates, on either leg. A
    single-currency IRS has nothing left to settle beyond each
    period's net cash flow (contrast a Cross-Currency Swap, which
    DOES exchange notional at both start and maturity -- out of
    scope for this module).
  - LCH SwapClear closes out BOTH legs simultaneously — there is no
    separate unwind step specific to the CCP relationship; maturity
    just means Period 20 was the last Payment Date on the schedule.
  - Bank Of Toronto's Rates book removes the position; the trade's
    UTI (Section 7) transitions from ACTIVE to MATURED in the
    position-keeping system.
  - No further regulatory reporting event is required beyond the
    routine lifecycle-event report closing the trade out at the
    Trade Repository (Module 14 §-scoped lifecycle reporting).
```

## 8. Where This Fits in the Trade Lifecycle

This module is a drill-down on the **Clearing**, **Settlement**, and **Position** stages of [Module 8](./module-08-rates.md) §5's full lifecycle walkthrough for the same trade (`BOT-IRS-2026-034410`) — Order, Execution, Trade Capture, and Confirmation all happen exactly once, at trade inception; **Clearing happens once, shortly after execution — this is the moment LCH SwapClear novates the trade into the two mirrored legs Section 4 diagrams**; but **Settlement repeats 20 times**, once per row in Section 5's table, each one independently calculated off that quarter's own CORRA compounding and settled twice (once per leg) via LCH SwapClear. Nothing else in the [Module 8](./module-08-rates.md) roadmap has a lifecycle that revisits its own Settlement stage this many times from a single Trade Capture event.

## Answers — Recap Quiz, with Explanations

**1. Floating Accrual = 100,000,000 x 2.95% x (91/365) = CAD 735,479.45 — confirmed. It's the largest net payment because it's the period with the WIDEST gap below the 4.00% fixed rate (2.95% floating vs. 4.00% fixed = a 1.05-point spread, the widest in the whole schedule).**

Net Amount = Fixed Accrual - Floating Accrual, so the largest net payment corresponds to the largest gap between the Fixed Rate (constant at 4.00%) and that period's Floating Rate — Period 6/7's 2.95% floating rate is the lowest in the entire 20-quarter schedule, producing the widest gap and therefore the largest single net payment (CAD 261,780.82).

**2. Period 16 settles at CAD 0.00 because compounded CORRA (3.95%) plus the 0.05% spread exactly equals the 4.00% Fixed Rate — the two legs are numerically identical, so there is nothing left over to net. Operationally, the Payment Date still occurs and still generates a settlement instruction of CAD 0.00 — most systems still book a zero-value settlement event for audit-trail completeness, rather than silently skipping the period.**

This is the same "hitting the rate exactly produces the boundary case" pattern [Module 8](./module-08-rates.md) §4c's Recap Quiz Answer 3 established for an at-the-money Cap caplet.

**3. Goldman Sachs would pay Bank Of Toronto. Floating Rate = 4.30% + 0.05% spread = 4.35%; Floating Accrual = 100,000,000 x 4.35% x (91/365) = CAD 1,084,520.55; Net = 997,260.27 - 1,084,520.55 = -CAD 87,260.28, i.e. Goldman Sachs pays Bank Of Toronto CAD 87,260.28.**

This continues the same trend Periods 17–20 already establish: the further compounded CORRA rises above the 4.00% fixed rate, the larger Bank Of Toronto's net receipt becomes.

**4. A Cross-Currency Swap is the Rates-family product where notional genuinely IS exchanged — both at the start AND at maturity, in two different currencies, because a cross-currency swap's whole purpose is to swap a principal amount in one currency for the equivalent principal in another, with each side then paying interest on the currency it now holds. A plain single-currency IRS never needs to exchange notional at all, because both legs' cash flows are already denominated in the same currency (CAD here) — netting the interest is sufficient, and there's no currency-conversion purpose that would require exchanging principal in the first place.**

Cross-Currency Swap sits outside [Module 8](./module-08-rates.md)'s five-product Interest Rate taxonomy (it's typically modelled as its own FX/Rates-hybrid product) — flagged here specifically because "notional never settles" (Section 1; [Module 1](./module-01-fundamentals.md) §8) is a rule about *this* module's five products, not an absolute rule about every derivative that has a Notional field.
