01 · One theory, three shapes

What Are Forward, Swap & Option? — Theory: The Three OTC Building Blocks

Almost every OTC derivative in this curriculum — across Rates, Credit, FX, Equity, and Commodity — is one of exactly three underlying structures, which is also literally the ISDA Product Taxonomy's Transaction Type controlled vocabulary.

ForwardSwapOption
One-line theoryObligation to transact ONCE, fixed price, one future dateObligation to transact REPEATEDLY, on a scheduleRIGHT (not obligation) to transact once, by/at expiry
Number of exchangesOneMany (periodic)Zero or one
Premium at inceptionNoneNoneYes — paid upfront
Payoff shapeLinear, symmetricLinear, symmetric, repeatedAsymmetric — capped for buyer
The Structural RelationshipHow the Three Fit Together
A SWAP is literally a STRIP OF FORWARDS -- one forward-style
exchange per payment date, bundled into a single legal contract
instead of negotiated separately. Section 3's IRS worked example
makes this exact -- each quarterly settlement is its own tiny
forward-rate calculation.

An OPTION is the RIGHT to enter a Forward, not the obligation.
Strip away the "right, not obligation" and pay no premium, and an
Option degenerates into a Forward -- sometimes described as "an
Option with a 100% guaranteed exercise decision, and therefore no
premium charged for that certainty."

Forward  ---- repeat many times ---->  Swap
   |
   +---- make it optional, charge a premium ---->  Option
Takeaway. This isn't a curriculum simplification — it's the actual regulatory classification. The ISDA Product Taxonomy's Transaction Type level uses exactly these values, which is why the same three words appear on trade records regardless of asset class (Section 7 returns to this directly).

02 · One exchange, no premium

FORWARD — Theory & Worked Examples Across Asset Classes

A bilateral agreement to transact a fixed amount at a fixed price on one future date — no premium, linear payoff, dollar-for-dollar in either direction.

Forward PayoffLinear, Both Directions
Buyer's P&L
    +  |                    /
       |                  /
       |                /
  -----+--------------+------------------  Market Price at maturity
       |            / Agreed
       |          /  (Forward) Price
    -  |        /

-> unlike Section 4's Option, there is NO flat/capped region on
   either side -- the line runs straight through, in both directions

FX — reusing Module 2 §10 in full

3-Month USD/CAD Forward
Forward Rate locked today: 1.3545 (Spot 1.3500 + 0.0045 forward points)
In 3 months: Bank Of Toronto pays CAD 13,545,000, receives USD 10,000,000
-> no cash moves today; only the RATE is locked in now

Rates — the FRA, and its cousin IR Forward Debt

FRA & IR Forward DebtModule 8 §4b, §4e
FRA (Forward Rate Agreement) -- a "forward" written on a RATE
------------------------------------------------------------
3x6 FRA: Bank Of Toronto BUYS (pays fixed, receives floating)
Notional CAD 20,000,000 | FRA Rate: 4.85% | 91-day reference period

At fixing, 3-month CORRA settles at 4.50% (lower than 4.85%)
Bank Of Toronto PAYS Goldman Sachs CAD 17,259.79 -- the discounted
difference between the FRA Rate and the settled floating rate

-> ONE isolated forward-rate exchange, settled ONCE, upfront
   (discounted) -- Section 3 shows this is what an IRS repeats
   every quarter, undiscounted

IR FORWARD DEBT -- a "forward" written on a BOND'S PRICE
------------------------------------------------------------
Bank Of Toronto agrees TODAY to buy Canada 5% Bond 2035 in 3 months
Forward Price: 97.80 (today's spot 98.50, minus 3 months of carry)
In 3 months, if physically settled: pays CAD 9,780,000, receives
CAD 10,000,000 face value of the bond via DvP

-> structurally identical to the FX Forward above, just
   referencing a BOND'S PRICE instead of a currency pair's rate

Equity — new worked example

Equity ForwardIllustrative — fills a gap
Built on Module 4 §4a's real KO position: 10,000 shares @ 68.40

Bank Of Toronto (holder) agrees TODAY to sell all 10,000 shares
to a client in 6 months, at a forward price fixed now.

Forward Price = Spot + Cost of Carry - Expected Dividends
              = 68.40 + (68.40 x 4.5% x 0.5) - (68.40 x 1.5% x 0.5)
              = 68.40 + 1.539 - 0.513 = 69.43 (illustrative)

IN 6 MONTHS: client pays 10,000 x 69.43 = USD 694,300, receives
10,000 KO shares -- regardless of where KO is trading then

-> same cost-of-carry logic as FX forward points, with an extra
   term for the dividends the SELLER forgoes

Commodity — new worked example

Commodity ForwardOTC, uncleared version of a Future
Bank Of Toronto agrees TODAY, bilaterally with an oil producer
client, to buy 50,000 bbl WTI crude in 2 months at USD 77.00/bbl
fixed -- negotiated directly, NOT executed on CME's order book,
NOT novated to a CCP at execution (contrast Module 7 §4b's Future)

IN 2 MONTHS, if WTI spot is USD 82.00/bbl:
  Bank Of Toronto still only pays 77.00/bbl -- gain of
  (82.00 - 77.00) x 50,000 = USD 250,000 vs. buying at spot then

-> ECONOMICALLY near-identical to Module 7 §4b's exchange Future
   -- the difference is entirely VENUE and CLEARING, not payoff
   mechanics (Module 15's "standardization is a venue property")
Callout — Credit doesn't have a clean "Forward" building block. Credit's core OTC product (the CDS, Section 3) is already priced as a periodic-premium contract with a contingent payout, not a one-off fixed-price exchange. A "forward-starting CDS" exists, but it's a variant of the Swap-family CDS, not a distinct Forward product — a genuine asset-class-specific exception.
Takeaway. Every Forward here shares the exact same shape — one fixed price, agreed today, for one exchange on one future date, no premium — whatever the underlying. What changes is only what the fixed price applies to, and whether an actual asset can ever be delivered.

03 · A strip of forwards

SWAP — Theory & Worked Examples Across Asset Classes

An agreement to exchange a series of cash flows over time, calculated off a formula, on the same Notional, repeated on a schedule — structurally a strip of Forwards bundled into one contract.

5Y CAD IRSModule 8 §4a
Bank Of Toronto pays fixed, receives floating
Notional CAD 100,000,000 | Fixed Rate 4.00% | Floating: CORRA + spread
Quarterly settlements, netted, for 5 years (20 total settlements)

-> EACH quarterly settlement is its own isolated fixed-vs-floating
   comparison -- the SAME calculation as the FRA above, repeated
   20 times inside one contract instead of 20 separate forwards.
   This IS Section 1's "Swap = strip of Forwards" claim, made
   concrete: an IRS really is 20 FRAs, wrapped in one document,
   undiscounted.

Full 20-settlement walkthrough: see the Interest Rate Swap (IRS)
Deep Dive.

Credit — the CDS, named a "Swap" but structurally different

5Y CDS on Northern Rail CorpModule 2 §9
Notional CAD 20,000,000 | Spread 120bps | Quarterly premium

Bank Of Toronto pays Goldman Sachs CAD 59,836 EVERY QUARTER (the
periodic, swap-like leg) -- UNLESS Northern Rail Corp defaults, in
which case Goldman Sachs pays Bank Of Toronto CAD 12,000,000 ONCE
(a CONTINGENT leg, not a periodic one)

-> ONE leg (the premium) behaves exactly like a swap's periodic
   exchange. The OTHER leg (the default payout) behaves like
   Section 4's OPTION -- contingent, capped payout. A CDS is
   documented AS a swap, but sits on the boundary between the
   Swap family and the Option family.

FX — the FX Swap (a short-dated exception), and Cross-Currency Swap

FX Swap vs. Cross-Currency SwapA Naming Trap — Module 6 §2
FX SWAP (Module 6 §4c): commonly overnight to 1 year, just a Spot
and a Forward bolted together -- TWO principal exchanges, NO
periodic interest payments. Closer to Section 2's Forward family
than a true multi-period Swap.

CROSS-CURRENCY SWAP: longer-dated (years), WITH periodic interest
payments exchanged in two currencies -- genuinely behaves like the
IRS, just with two different-currency legs. ISDA classifies it
under INTEREST RATE, not FX -- its periodic-exchange mechanic is a
Rates-family Swap, despite involving two currencies.

Equity — Total Return Swap on Apple Inc. shares

Equity TRSModule 2 §8
Periodic net cash flows tracking the share's price movement PLUS
dividends, exchanged against a financing-rate leg (typically a
floating short-term rate) -- same fixed/floating-style exchange
shape as the IRS, just with "price + dividend return" standing in
for "fixed rate" as one of the two legs being swapped.

Commodity — Brent Crude Oil Swap

1Y Brent Crude Oil SwapModule 7 §4c
Fixed Price: USD 78.00/bbl | Monthly notional: 8,333 bbl

MONTH 1 -- ICE Brent monthly average settles at USD 81.20/bbl
  Floating leg owed to Bank Of Toronto = 8,333 x 81.20 = USD 676,640
  Fixed leg owed by Bank Of Toronto    = 8,333 x 78.00 = USD 649,974
  Net cash flow: Bank Of Toronto RECEIVES USD 26,666
  -> only the NET amount moves, repeating monthly for 12 months --
     another Forward-style netted exchange, once per month
Takeaway. Four of five asset classes' Swaps follow the identical fixed-vs-floating periodic netting mechanic — the outlier is Credit's CDS, whose contingent default leg borrows the Option-family payoff shape. That dual identity isn't a modeling error — it's a real structural fact requiring genuinely different pricing models for its two legs.

04 · A right, not an obligation

OPTION — Theory & Worked Examples Across Asset Classes

Grants the buyer the right, not the obligation, to transact at a fixed strike by/at expiry, for an upfront premium — capped loss for the buyer, uncapped for the seller.

Option PayoffAsymmetric, Capped on One Side
Buyer's P&L
    +  |                         /
       |                       /
       |                     /
  -----+-------------------+------------------  Market Price at expiry
       |. . . . . . . . . /  Strike Price
    -  |________________/
       (flat -- capped at premium paid, no matter how far the
        market moves against the position)

FX — reusing Module 6 §4d in full

FX Option
USD Call / CAD Put, Notional USD 5,000,000, Strike 1.3600
Premium: 0.0180 CAD per USD notional = CAD 90,000

Spot to 1.3800: exercise, net benefit CAD 10,000 (after premium)
Spot to 1.3400: expires worthless, max loss = CAD 90,000 premium

Commodity — reusing Module 7 §4d in full

Commodity Option
Call on CME WTI Crude Oil Future
Strike USD 80.00/bbl, Notional 100,000 bbl, Premium USD 3.20/bbl
Premium Paid = USD 320,000

WTI to USD 88.00/bbl: exercise, net benefit USD 480,000
WTI to USD 74.00/bbl: expires worthless, max loss = USD 320,000 premium

Rates — the Swaption

Swaption1Y-into-5Y Payer — Module 8 §4d
Bank Of Toronto buys the RIGHT to enter a 5Y IRS, as fixed-rate
PAYER, in 1 year -- literally "the option to enter Section 3's
IRS," Section 1's Forward-vs-Option relationship one layer up

Notional CAD 50,000,000, Strike 4.25%, Premium 0.60% = CAD 300,000

Prevailing 5Y rate at expiry = 4.75%: EXERCISE -- enters the swap
paying only 4.25% fixed when the market rate is 4.75%
Prevailing 5Y rate at expiry = 3.90%: DON'T exercise -- a fresh
market swap beats the 4.25% strike; loss capped at CAD 300,000

Equity — an OTC put, contrasted directly with a listed one

OTC Equity OptionReusing the Hedging Deep Dive's KO Put
Maple Leaf Pension Fund holds 10,000 KO shares (Module 4 §4a). It
buys a PROTECTIVE PUT directly from Bank Of Toronto (its dealer)
rather than through a listed options exchange:

KO Put, Strike USD 65.00, Notional 10,000 shares, Expiry 1 month
Premium USD 1.20/share = USD 12,000

KO falls to 58.00: exercise, avoided loss = USD 70,000
KO rises to 75.00: expires worthless, max loss = USD 12,000 premium

-> the PAYOFF SHAPE is identical to Module 4 §4d's LISTED AAPL
   call -- the only real difference is VENUE: negotiated
   bilaterally, never touches the OCC
Callout — Credit's option-like behavior already lives inside the CDS. Rather than a separate standalone "Credit Option," Section 3 already showed the CDS's default-contingent leg behaves exactly like an Option's payoff. CDS Index Swaptions exist as a true standalone product, but this curriculum doesn't build a separate worked example for that niche instrument.
Takeaway. The asymmetric shape — premium paid, capped downside, uncapped upside — recurs identically across every underlying. The Swaption is conceptually the most important entry here: it proves Section 1's Forward → Swap, Option-on-Forward relationship generalizes one level up.

06 · Front-loaded vs. back-loaded risk

Why the Structural Difference Matters — Premium Timing & Counterparty Credit Exposure

Forward/Swap vs. Option produce genuinely different counterparty credit exposure profiles over a trade's life, directly relevant to Module 11 §2's Current Exposure vs. PFE distinction.

Exposure Profile Over Time
FORWARD / SWAP (no premium at inception):
  Day 1 MTM = CAD 0 (fairly priced at trade date)
  Current Exposure = CAD 0 on day 1 -- but PFE is NOT zero (Module
  11 §2: PFE estimated at roughly CAD 4,500,000, 2 years into a
  5Y CAD 100,000,000 IRS, at 95% confidence)
  -> RISK BUILDS UP GRADUALLY as rates/prices move, either direction

OPTION (premium paid upfront):
  Day 1: BUYER has ALREADY PAID the premium (e.g. CAD 90,000 FX
  Option premium) -- maximum possible loss is fully known and
  realized from day one; can only shrink toward zero at expiry
  -> for the SELLER (writer), exposure runs the OPPOSITE way --
     having COLLECTED the premium, risk is entirely in the FUTURE,
     potentially large and one-directional
Takeaway. This is why credit-limit and Initial Margin calculations treat a Forward/Swap position and an Option position differently even at identical notional — symmetric and back-loaded vs. asymmetric, front-loaded for the buyer but back-loaded and one-directional for the seller.

07 · Closing the loop

ISDA Taxonomy Tie-In — Forward, Swap & Option as Transaction Type Values

The ISDA Product Taxonomy's four-level hierarchy uses these same three words as controlled values at its final level, Transaction Type.

Where Forward/Swap/Option SitIn the ISDA Taxonomy
Interest Rate  -> IRSwap     -> FixedFloat   -> SWAP
Credit         -> SingleName -> Corporate    -> CREDITDEFAULTSWAP
                                                 (named "Swap," Section 3's
                                                 dual-natured exception)
Foreign Exch.  -> Forward    -> Deliverable  -> FORWARD
Equity         -> Swap       -> PriceReturn  -> SWAP
Commodity      -> Energy     -> Oil          -> SWAP

-> Notice OPTION doesn't appear as a Transaction Type value in any
   of these five paths, because none of Sections 2-3's examples
   were Options -- Section 4's Swaption, FX Option, Commodity
   Option, and Equity Option trades would each carry their OWN
   taxonomy path with Transaction Type = OPTION, distinct from
   their underlying Forward/Swap cousins.
Takeaway. This is the concrete answer to "where does the Forward/Swap/Option distinction actually show up in a trade record" — a literal, regulator-facing field value on every OTC derivative's ISDA taxonomy path, which feeds directly into the trade's UPI reference data.

Self-check · Solutions

Recap Quiz — Answers & Explanations

Check your work against these.

Q1 — A Swap repeats a Forward-style exchange on a schedule instead of once; an Option is the right, not obligation, to enter that same exchange — strip away optionality and premium, and it collapses back into a Forward

Section 1's diagram states both halves directly; Section 3's IRS (20 quarterly settlements, each its own fixed-vs-floating comparison) makes it concrete.

Q2 — The FRA settles early because the rate is already known by then; IR Forward Debt only ever has one settlement date to begin with. Not a structural difference — just a timing convention specific to a rate-referencing forward vs. a price-referencing one

Section 2's FRA and IR Forward Debt examples state their settlement timing explicitly.

Q3 — Credit's core OTC product (the CDS) is already a periodic-premium, contingent-payout contract, not a one-off fixed-price exchange; a forward-starting CDS is a Swap-family variant, not a distinct Forward product
Section 2's callout states this as a genuine asset-class-specific exception.
Q4 — The premium leg is paid periodically regardless of outcome (a Swap's repeated exchange shape); the default leg pays out once, only if triggered, capped by notional and recovery (an Option's payoff shape)

Section 3's CDS worked example and takeaway state this dual-nature explicitly.

Q5 — What makes it a "Swap" — periodic interest exchange over years — is an Interest-Rate-family mechanic; FX's own products never have periodic interest payments, only principal exchanges

Section 3's naming-trap callout cross-references Module 6 §2's own explicit warning.

Q6 — It proves the Forward-to-Option relationship isn't limited to a single exchange — an Option can be written on an entire Swap, one layer of abstraction up

Section 4's Swaption example calls it "Section 1's relationship applied one layer up, to an entire Swap instead of a single exchange."

Q7 — A Forward/Swap starts at zero MTM with no premium capping cost either way, so exposure grows as prices move; an Option buyer pays the full premium upfront — that IS the max loss, already realized, shrinking toward zero at expiry

Section 6's exposure-profile diagram reuses Module 11 §2's PFE example for the Forward/Swap side.

Q8 — No — every asset class has its own Option variant, so Transaction Type = OPTION alone is ambiguous. Asset Class, read with Base Product and Sub-Product, is what actually identifies it

Section 7 cross-references the ISDA Product Taxonomy Deep Dive's point that no single taxonomy level is sufficient alone.
