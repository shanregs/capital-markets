01 · A state, not an event

What Is a Position? — Definition & the Three Things Every Position Has

A Position is the net, cumulative holding in an instrument at a point in time, built from every trade executed so far. A Trade is an event — it happens once. A Position is a state — it persists and revalues daily even with no new trade.

Trade (an Event) vs. Position (a State)
TRADE:     "Bank Of Toronto bought 10,000,000 face value of the
           Canada 5% Bond 2035 on 15-Jan-2026" -- happened once, at
           one price, never changes after Confirmation

POSITION:  "Bank Of Toronto HOLDS 10,000,000 face value of the
           Canada 5% Bond 2035" -- true every day for as long as
           held, REVALUED daily even though no new trade occurs,
           and only changes when a NEW trade actually happens

-> A system that treats "position" as just "the most recent
   trade" breaks the moment a SECOND trade in the same instrument
   occurs.
FactQuestion it answersExample (Module 5 §4a)
Quantity / NotionalHow much do we hold?CAD 10,000,000 face value
DirectionAre we long or short?Long (we hold the bond)
Cost BasisWhat did we pay, on average?Clean Price 98.50 (CAD 9,850,000)
Takeaway. Every worked example in this module is a position built from exactly these three facts, just with a different unit for "Quantity" — shares, face value, currency amount, swap notional, protection notional, or contract lots. Section 2 maps exactly where each asset class's position fact lives.

02 · Two fundamentally different shapes

Position Across Asset Classes — Where the "Quantity" Lives

Every asset class already showed its own POSITION stage in Module 3's nine-stage lifecycle. This section collects them side by side.

Asset classWhat "Quantity" meansLong vs. Short
EquityNumber of shares heldLong = own shares; Short = borrowed and sold
Fixed IncomeFace Value of bonds heldLong = hold the bond (creditor)
FXA currency BALANCE — both sides of the pairLong base / short quote, or vice versa
Rates (IRS)Notional referenced by the swap"Pay fixed" vs "receive fixed"
Credit (CDS)Notional of protection bought/soldLong protection vs. short protection
CommoditiesNumber of futures/forward contracts (lots)Long = bought; Short = sold
Takeaway. A reporting system's Position table needs an asset-class-aware schema — Equity/Fixed Income/Commodities positions are genuinely held quantities of something, Rates/Credit positions are notional references to a contract's terms, and FX positions are two linked currency balances, not one number.

03 · The simplest position

Worked Example — Equity: A Share Position, Marked Daily

Reusing Module 4 §5's full lifecycle for Maple Leaf Pension Fund's KO trade — a share position is just a running count, revalued daily.

Equity Position — Maple Leaf Pension Fund, KOModule 4 §5
TRADE: buy 10,000 shares KO @ USD 68.40

POSITION (after Settlement, T+1): holding increases by 10,000
shares in the position-keeping system

VALUATION / P&L -- marked to the current market price EVERY DAY:
  Day of purchase:  10,000 x 68.40 = USD 684,000 (cost basis)
  One week later:   10,000 x 71.10 = USD 711,000 (market value)
  Unrealized P&L =  711,000 - 684,000 = +USD 27,000

-> The position itself didn't change all week (still 10,000
   shares) -- only its VALUATION changed. A partial sale would
   drop the position from that day forward.
Takeaway. An equity position is the cleanest illustration of the Trade-vs-Position distinction — the Trade happened once; the Position persists and gets revalued every trading day until a further trade changes the quantity.

04 · Two moving valuation parts

Worked Example — Fixed Income: A Bond Position, Marked Daily With Accrual

Reusing Module 5 §5's Canada 5% Bond 2035 position — one layer more complex than Equity, because a bond position accrues interest daily on top of price moves.

Fixed Income Position — Canada 5% Bond 2035Module 5 §5
TRADE: buy CAD 10,000,000 face value @ Clean Price 98.50

POSITION (after Settlement, T+2): book increases by CAD
10,000,000 face value of this bond

VALUATION / P&L -- marked daily to Clean Price, PLUS accrual:
  Settlement day:  Clean 98.50, Accrued 82,418
  One week later:  Clean 98.65, Accrued 106,164
    Price P&L   = (98.65 - 98.50)/100 x 10,000,000 = +CAD 15,000
    Accrual P&L = 106,164 - 82,418                = +CAD 23,746

Why position SIZE turns a basis-point move into a dollar figure. Module 25 §3's DV01 (CAD 7,092/bp) is computed directly off this position's Market Value (CAD 9,850,000). Doubling the face value to CAD 20,000,000 would double DV01 to ~CAD 14,184/bp, for the identical 25bp yield move. Position size is the multiplier that turns a per-unit sensitivity into an actual dollar P&L.

Takeaway. A position's valuation depends on both a per-unit market observation (Clean Price/YTM, set by the whole market) and the position's own size (which only changes when Bank Of Toronto itself trades) — two inputs that change on entirely different logic.

05 · Not a single number

Worked Example — FX: A Position Is Two Linked Currency Balances

Reusing Module 6 §5's USD/CAD Spot trade — the asset class where "position" genuinely isn't a single number.

FX Position — USD/CADModule 6 §5
TRADE: buy USD 10,000,000 @ Spot Rate 1.3500 (pay CAD 13,500,000)

POSITION (after Settlement, T+1): USD position INCREASES by
USD 10,000,000; CAD position DECREASES by CAD 13,500,000 -- ONE
trade, TWO position changes, in two currencies

VALUATION / P&L -- marked to the current spot rate daily:
  Trade day:       USD 10,000,000 @ 1.3500 = CAD 13,500,000
  One week later:  USD 10,000,000 @ 1.3550 = CAD 13,550,000
  Unrealized P&L = 13,550,000 - 13,500,000 = +CAD 50,000
Takeaway. Every FX position is really a currency pair's worth of two simultaneous, opposite-signed balances — a system showing FX positions per-currency, in isolation, loses the fact that they're economically one trade's two halves.

06 · Notional, not a held quantity

Worked Example — Rates & Credit: A Position That's Pure Notional

Reusing Module 8 §5's 5Y CAD IRS and Module 9 §5's CDS — the two asset classes where a position isn't a quantity of something owned, just a notional reference inside a live contract.

Rates Position — 5Y CAD IRSModule 8 §5
TRADE: pay FIXED 4.00%, receive FLOATING (CORRA),
       Notional CAD 100,000,000

POSITION: ONE 5Y IRS, CAD 100,000,000 notional -- the position
IS the contract's remaining terms, not a "quantity of swaps"

VALUATION / P&L -- DCF off the current CORRA curve:
  Rate moves 4.00% -> 4.30% (30bp rise)
  Approx. MTM Gain = Swap DV01 x bps moved = 41,000 x 30
                    = CAD 1,230,000
Credit Position — CDS on Northstar Resources Inc.Module 9 §5
TRADE: BUY 5Y protection, USD 10,000,000 notional,
       Running Coupon 100bps, vs Goldman Sachs

POSITION: ONE CDS, USD 10,000,000 notional of protection BOUGHT

VALUATION / P&L -- market spread widens 250bps -> 400bps over
one year:
  Unrealized Gain = USD 525,000 to Bank Of Toronto
Takeaway. An IRS or CDS position is one row per live contract, carrying that contract's own notional and remaining schedule — closing it means terminating or offsetting the specific contract, not "selling some quantity" the way a share position works.

07 · Cash-settled every day

Worked Example — Commodities: A Position Cash-Settled Every Single Day

Reusing Module 7 §5's CME WTI Crude Future — the sharpest illustration of a position whose valuation change is realized in cash daily, not just tracked on paper.

Commodity Futures Position — 50 WTI ContractsModule 7 §5
TRADE: buy 50 front-month WTI contracts @ USD 75.50/bbl,
       novated to CME Clearing immediately at execution

POSITION: 50 WTI contracts, front-month, until rolled or closed

DAILY VARIATION MARGIN -- marked to daily settlement price, CASH
moves every day:
  Day 1 settle USD 76.00: Bank Of Toronto RECEIVES
    50 x 1,000 x (76.00 - 75.50) = USD 25,000
  Day 2 settle USD 75.20: Bank Of Toronto PAYS
    50 x 1,000 x (76.00 - 75.20) = USD 40,000
Takeaway. Exchange-traded futures collapse the distinction between "the position's valuation changed" and "cash actually moved" into a single daily event — a genuinely different operational pattern from every other worked example in this module.

08 · Three different clocks

How Often Does a Position Actually Change? — Three Different Clocks

"Position" changes on three genuinely different clocks, and conflating them is a common source of confusion for a system trying to answer "is this position's data stale?"

What changesTriggerFrequency
Position quantity/notionalA new tradeOnly when a trade happens
Position valuation (MTM)The market movingContinuously; formally snapshotted at End of Day
Corporate/contractual eventsIssuer/contract terms, not a tradeIrregular, event-driven (split, coupon, reset, roll)

Intraday (real-time) vs. End-of-Day (official) position. A front-office system typically shows a real-time running position, updating with every fill. But the position that feeds risk, P&L sign-off, and regulatory reporting is almost always the End-of-Day (EOD) snapshot — a single, reconciled, official number captured once per business day (Module 3 §8's reconciliation certifies it). The two can legitimately disagree mid-day without either being "wrong."

Takeaway. A position's quantity changes only on trade events; its valuation changes continuously but is officially captured once a day; corporate/contractual events change it on their own schedule, unrelated to trading activity. A system's "as-of" timestamp needs to say which clock it's reporting against.

09 · The multiplier that produces MTM

Position's Impact on Valuation, MTM & YTM

Position is one of the two direct multiplicative inputs — alongside a market-observed price/yield/rate/spread — that produces Valuation and MTM, and it interacts with YTM in a specific, easy-to-get-backwards way.

ConceptWhat it answersHow Position drives it
ValuationWhat is this holding worth now?Position Quantity/Notional × current market Price/Rate/Spread
MTMHow much did that worth change?Position size × (bps moved × per-unit sensitivity)
YTMWhat am I earning if held to maturity?A per-unit figure — does NOT change with position size at all
YTM Doesn't Care About Size — the Dollar Impact Does
Bank Of Toronto: CAD 10,000,000 face, YTM 5.21%
Another desk:     CAD 100,000,000 face, SAME bond, YTM 5.21%
(identical YTM -- a property of the bond, not the holder)

A 25bp YTM rise costs:
  Bank Of Toronto's position:  -CAD 177,300
  The other desk's position:   -CAD 1,773,000  (10x size, 10x $)

-> YTM is a RATE (unaffected by size); DV01 in dollars is a
   POSITION-SIZED figure (directly proportional to size).
Takeaway. Every valuation and MTM figure reduces to "position size × a market-observed rate or price" — getting position size wrong (a missed trade, an unapplied partial close) produces a valuation error that scales linearly with the mistake, exactly why Module 3 §8's reconciliation is a hard control, not optional.

10 · A shared, position-sized input

Position in Risk Systems — VaR, Exposure & the Reconciliation Control

Module 11 §6 already computes VaR directly off a position — this section makes explicit that VaR, like MTM, is fundamentally a position-sized calculation.

1-Day 99% VaR — Canada 5% Bond 2035 PositionModule 11 §6
Position: CAD 10,000,000 face value
DV01: CAD 7,092 per basis point (position-sized)
Daily yield volatility: 8 basis points | 99% multiplier: ~2.33

1-Day 99% VaR = 7,092 x 2.33 x 8 = CAD 132,235

-> Double the position, double the DV01, double the VaR, for
   the identical bond and volatility assumption.

Why position reconciliation is a risk control. If the position feeding this VaR were wrong — say, understated by a missed trade — every downstream number (VaR, MTM, regulatory reporting) would be wrong in the same proportion, silently. Module 3 §8's three-way reconciliation exists specifically to catch this before it propagates.

Takeaway. A position isn't just a database row — it's the shared input every downstream valuation, MTM, VaR, and regulatory calculation depends on, which is why getting it reconciled and correct is a first-class control.

11 · Two genuinely different clocks, formalized

Position Reporting to Regulators — Daily, Periodic & Real-Time

Module 14 §1 establishes five reporting obligations, not one. This section focuses on the ones that report a Position (a standing state) rather than a Trade (a one-time event), and how often each runs.

RegimeWhat gets reportedFrequency
EMIR / Dodd-FrankDaily valuation updates for every open OTC derivative positionDaily, for the life of the position
SFTRDaily valuation/collateral updates for every open repo/SL positionDaily, for the life of the position
MiFID II Position ReportingStanding positions in position-limit-subject commodity derivativesPeriodic (weekly to venue)
CFTC Large Trader ReportingLarge concentrated futures/options positions above thresholdDaily, once threshold crossed
Exchange futures marginingDaily settlement-price revaluation, driving VMDaily, automatically
SFTR's Daily Valuation Update — Day 1 → Day 2Module 16 §6b
Day 1: Collateral market value = GBP 20,250,000
Overnight: Gilt price falls 0.5%
Day 2: Collateral market value = 20,250,000 x 0.995
                                = GBP 20,148,750 (down GBP 101,250)

-> A NEW valuation-update report is submitted for THIS SAME
   POSITION on Day 2, even though NO NEW TRADE happened -- SFTR
   requires that moved value reported, daily, for as long as the
   position stays open.
Trade-Level vs. Position-Level ReportingModule 14 §1
Bank Of Toronto's 5Y CAD IRS with Goldman Sachs:

TRADE-LEVEL (EMIR/Dodd-Frank): ONE initial report at execution,
  THEN a fresh valuation-update report EVERY DAY the position
  stays open.

POSITION-LEVEL (MiFID Position Reporting): NOT triggered for a
  plain IRS -- only relevant if a CUMULATIVE position in a
  position-limit-subject commodity derivative crosses a
  threshold.
Takeaway. EMIR/SFTR require a daily valuation report for as long as a derivative or SFT position stays open, turning the continuous MTM clock into a formal daily obligation; MiFID's Position Reporting is a periodic, concentration-focused check on a completely different cadence and question. A rules engine needs both clocks wired in separately.

Recap Quiz — Answers & Explanations

Check your work against these.

Q1 — A Trade is a one-time event, never changing once confirmed; a Position persists and gets revalued continuously even without a new trade

A system treating "position" as "the latest trade" loses the cumulative running total the moment a second trade needs to be added or netted.

Q2 — A Rates or Credit position is one row per live contract, valued off its own notional and terms, not a running total of a fungible identical unit

Section 6's data-model discussion contrasts quantity-held positions with contract-referencing positions.

Q3 — DV01 = Modified Duration × Market Value × 0.0001; Duration and YTM are properties of the bond itself, but Market Value (and DV01 in dollars) scales directly with position size

Section 4's worked CAD 10,000,000 vs. CAD 20,000,000 comparison.

Q4 — A single FX trade always changes two currency balances simultaneously and oppositely (buying USD means selling CAD)

Section 5's worked example: buying USD 10,000,000 increases USD and decreases CAD by CAD 13,500,000, from one trade.

Q5 — The position is novated to a CCP that marks it to the daily settlement price and moves Variation Margin cash every day, so paper valuation and actual cash flow are identical

Section 7's WTI futures example, contrasted with Sections 4 and 6's paper-only P&L between cash events.

Q6 — Position quantity/notional (trade-driven), position valuation/MTM (continuous, EOD-snapshotted), and corporate/contractual events (irregular, event-driven); a stock split falls under the third

Section 8's three-clock table names corporate actions explicitly as the third category.

Q7 — YTM is a per-unit rate, identical regardless of size; the dollar impact is Position Size × DV01-per-unit, which scales with how much face value is held
Section 9's CAD 10,000,000 vs. CAD 100,000,000 comparison states this as "the key nuance."
Q8 — EMIR's Derivatives Reporting applies to essentially every OTC derivative, requiring daily valuation for as long as it's open; MiFID Position Reporting only applies to position-limit-subject commodity derivatives once a cumulative position crosses a threshold — a plain IRS is never in scope there
