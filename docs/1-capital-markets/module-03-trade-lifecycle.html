01 · The whole journey, top to bottom

The Trade Lifecycle — Nine Stages, One Picture

Every trade you'll ever see in a reporting system passes through the same nine stages, in the same order, regardless of asset class. What differs per asset class is how each stage is implemented — the sequence itself never changes.

02 · Where "the trade" actually begins

Order vs Execution

An Order is an instruction to buy or sell — no counterparty commitment, can be amended or cancelled freely. An Execution is the moment an order meets a matching counterparty at an agreed price — from that instant, a legally binding trade exists, even before any system has recorded it.

Worked example — Maple Leaf Pension Fund routes an order through Bank Of Toronto

Timeline · Order to Execution09:02 – 09:03 UTC
09:02:00  ORDER placed
  Maple Leaf Pension Fund instructs Bank Of Toronto:
    "BUY CAD 50,000,000 face value, Canada 5% Bond 2035,
     LIMIT price 98.60 or better"
  -> No trade exists yet. This is pure intent.

09:03:12  EXECUTION
  Bank Of Toronto's trading desk finds a seller in the interbank
  market willing to sell at 98.55 (better than the fund's 98.60 limit)
  -> A TRADE now exists, legally, at 09:03:12 — before any
     system has captured it as a data record.

  Trade economics agreed at execution:
    Instrument : Canada 5% Bond 2035 (ISIN CA1358XXXXX)
    Quantity   : CAD 50,000,000 face value
    Price      : 98.55
    Counterparty: Maple Leaf Pension Fund
    Side       : Bank Of Toronto sells / Maple Leaf Pension Fund buys

An order can also be partially filled — e.g. the desk might only find CAD 30,000,000 face value available at or better than 98.60 in the first pass, execute that slice immediately, and leave the remaining CAD 20,000,000 as a working order until more supply appears. Each fill that occurs is its own Execution and, downstream, its own Trade record — one Order can produce many Trades.

Takeaway. "Order" and "Trade" are not the same object, and a reporting system that conflates them will show phantom trades for cancelled/unfilled orders, or miss that one order became three separate trade records. The Execution timestamp — not the Order timestamp, and not the Trade Capture timestamp — is the legally binding moment the deal was struck.

03 · The trade becomes data

The moment the Execution becomes an electronic record — a Trade ID is assigned, and every field needed downstream (settlement, valuation, regulatory reporting) is populated for the first time.

Trade Date vs Execution Time

Trade Date is a calendar day; Execution Time is a precise timestamp, needed for best-execution analysis and for regulatory reporting deadlines that are measured in minutes, not days (Section 09).

Status = NEW

Every trade record starts life with a status, and that status field is what the rest of this module (Sections 04–09) advances through. Section 12 lays out the full state machine.

Takeaway. If a Trade record and its parent Order have different quantities, that is not automatically a bug — it may simply mean the order was partially filled across several separate Trade Capture events, each with its own Trade ID.

04 · Putting it in writing

Confirmation & Affirmation

Confirmation is the message one party sends stating what it believes was agreed. Affirmation is the counterparty's formal agreement that those terms match. Only after both steps complete is a trade considered legally settled-in-principle.

Diagram · Confirmation & Affirmation09:15 – 10:31 UTC
Bank Of Toronto                                Maple Leaf Pension Fund
     |                                                    |
     | 09:15  Sends CONFIRMATION                            |
     |   Trade ID BOT-2026-000901                            |
     |   Canada 5% Bond 2035, CAD 50,000,000 @ 98.55         |
     |   Settlement Date 19-Aug-2026                        |
     |------------------------------------------------------>|
     |                                                    |
     |                                    10:30  Reviews terms
     |                                    against its own order
     |                                    record -> MATCHES
     |                                                    |
     |                              10:31  Sends AFFIRMATION|
     |<------------------------------------------------------|
     |                                                    |
Status: CONFIRMED -> AFFIRMED

For cash securities (bonds, equities), this typically happens through platforms such as CTM (Central Trade Manager, DTCC) or Omgeo/Alert. For OTC derivatives, the equivalent step usually runs through MarkitWire or direct FpML message exchange between the two counterparties' systems, feeding the ISDA Master Agreement's legal framework referenced in Module 2, Section 07.

Takeaway. A trade sitting in "CONFIRMED" but not yet "AFFIRMED" status for an unusually long time is a strong signal something's wrong — either the counterparty disagrees with a term (a break, Section 05) or an operational delay is quietly eating into the T+2 settlement runway.

05 · Do both sides actually agree?

Matching — Bilateral Matching and Trade Breaks

The automated, field-by-field comparison of both counterparties' trade records. Every field lines up exactly -> MATCHED. Even one field disagrees -> a BREAK, held until a human resolves it.

FIELD                 BANK OF TORONTO SIDE       PENSION FUND SIDE

Trade ID (internal)   BOT-2026-000901            MLPF-77213
Instrument (ISIN)     CA1358XXXXX                CA1358XXXXX          OK
Quantity               50,000,000                 50,000,000           OK
Price                  98.55                       98.55                OK
Settlement Date        2026-08-19                  2026-08-19           OK
Currency               CAD                          CAD                  OK
-------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT: MATCHED  ->  proceeds straight to Settlement (Section 07)
Comparison · A Broken Match15bp price mismatch
FIELD                 BANK OF TORONTO SIDE       COUNTERPARTY SIDE
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Instrument (ISIN)     CA1358XXXXX                CA1358XXXXX          OK
Quantity               10,000,000                  10,000,000          OK
Price                  98.50                        98.65               MISMATCH (15bp)
Settlement Date        2026-08-19                   2026-08-19          OK
-------------------------------------------------------------------
RESULT: BROKEN  ->  held, routed to an Ops queue for investigation

  Root cause found: the counterparty's trader fat-fingered 98.65
  instead of 98.50 when keying the trade into their own system.
  Corrected, re-submitted, re-matched -> MATCHED at 98.50, one
  business hour later than a clean trade would have taken.
A 15-basis-point price break on CAD 10,000,000 face value is a difference of 10,000,000 x 0.15 / 100 = CAD 15,000 in consideration — small enough to look trivial in isolation, but exactly the kind of discrepancy that, left unresolved, cascades into a failed settlement (Section 13) two days later.
Takeaway. "Matched" and "Confirmed" are not synonyms — Confirmation is one party stating terms; Matching is the automated proof that both parties' independently-keyed records agree. A trade can be Confirmed and still fail to Match if the counterparty's own capture had an error.

06 · The CCP steps into the middle

Clearing — Novation Through a CCP

Clearing only applies to products routed through a CCP (Central Counterparty) — mandatory for most standardized OTC derivatives. The CCP performs novation: a specific legal mechanism (governed by the CCP's own rulebook, not just market convention) that extinguishes the original bilateral contract and replaces it with two brand-new contracts — one between each original party and the CCP. This is not the same as an assignment (where the original contract just changes hands); novation kills the old contract outright and creates two new ones in its place.

What is LCH / LCH SwapClear? LCH (London Clearing House) is a CCP group headquartered in London, part of the London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG) — one of the world's largest clearing houses. SwapClear is LCH's specific clearing service for interest rate swaps: the actual named CCP that novates the vast majority of the world's centrally-cleared IRS volume, across many currencies (CAD included, as this worked example uses). "LCH" is the legal entity/group; "SwapClear" is the product line within it. Full market-infrastructure detail in Systems Landscape §6 and §11.
Diagram · Novation5Y CAD IRS, CAD 100,000,000
BEFORE CLEARING  —  one bilateral trade
========================================================================

  Bank Of Toronto  <====  5Y CAD IRS, Notional CAD 100,000,000  ====>  Goldman Sachs
       (pays fixed 4.00% / receives floating CORRA)

AFTER NOVATION  —  LCH SwapClear steps in; TWO trades now exist
========================================================================

  Bank Of Toronto  <====  IRS #1, CAD 100,000,000  ====>   LCH SwapClear (CCP)
       (pays fixed 4.00% / receives floating)

  LCH SwapClear (CCP)  <====  IRS #2, CAD 100,000,000  ====>   Goldman Sachs
       (pays fixed 4.00% / receives floating — mirror of IRS #1)

  -> The original Bank Of Toronto <-> Goldman Sachs contract no longer
     exists; it was legally extinguished by novation. Bank Of Toronto's
     counterparty risk is now LCH SwapClear, not Goldman Sachs. If
     Goldman Sachs defaulted, LCH SwapClear absorbs the loss and Bank
     Of Toronto's trade with LCH SwapClear is unaffected.

Novation is not free — both sides must post Initial Margin (IM) to the CCP to cover potential future losses, plus daily Variation Margin (VM) as the trade's value moves. Full mechanics belong to Module 12 (Collateral & Margin); here's the shape of the numbers just to make "clearing has a cost" concrete:

Math · Illustrative MarginCAD 100,000,000 notional
Initial Margin (IM) — illustrative rate ~2% of notional for a 5Y IRS:
  100,000,000 x 2.00% = CAD 2,000,000
  Bank Of Toronto posts CAD 2,000,000 to the CCP at trade inception.
  Goldman Sachs separately posts its own CAD 2,000,000 to the CCP.

Variation Margin (VM) — next business day, market rates move,
the swap's mark-to-market value shifts by CAD 180,000 in
Bank Of Toronto's favor:
  CCP collects CAD 180,000 from Goldman Sachs' VM account
  CCP pays CAD 180,000 to Bank Of Toronto's VM account
  (VM is exchanged DAILY for the life of the trade — Module 12)
Takeaway. The reason a cleared trade's record shows a CCP as counterparty, not the original bank, is novation — it's not a data error or a proxy field, it's what legally happened at the Clearing stage. Bilateral (uncleared) products, like the plain bond trade in Sections 02–05, simply skip this stage entirely.

07 · The promise becomes reality

Settlement — DvP Mechanics, Revisited With Real Numbers

Securities move from seller to buyer, cash moves from buyer to seller, simultaneously via DvP (Delivery versus Payment) so neither side is ever left holding one leg without the other.

Settlement · BOT-2026-000901T+2 · 19-Aug-2026
Bank Of Toronto (Seller)                Maple Leaf Pension Fund (Buyer)
Custodian: BNY Mellon                    Custodian: State Street
     |                                                    |
     | Instructs BNY Mellon to deliver                     |
     | CAD 50,000,000 face value                          |
     | Canada 5% Bond 2035 (ISIN CA1358XXXXX)               |
     |                                                    |
     |         Both custodians instruct the CSD (CDS)      |
     |         to move bond + cash SIMULTANEOUSLY (DvP)    |
     v                                                    v
--------------------------------------------------------------------
|                    CDS — Central Securities Depository            |
|  09:00:14 (T+2)  Bond: CAD 50,000,000 face value moves from        |
|                   Bank Of Toronto's account to Maple Leaf's        |
|  09:00:14 (T+2)  Cash: CAD 49,275,000 moves from Maple Leaf's      |
|                   cash account to Bank Of Toronto's cash account   |
|                   (50,000,000 x 98.55 / 100)                       |
--------------------------------------------------------------------

RESULT: both legs move in the same instant. Neither party is ever
exposed to "I delivered/paid and the other side didn't."
Status: MATCHED -> SETTLED
Takeaway. DvP is the whole reason "settlement risk" (Herstatt risk) is manageable for securities in modern markets — the two legs are mechanically tied together at the CSD so a partial failure simply cannot happen for products settling through this rail. Section 13 covers what happens when the whole trade fails to settle.

Takeaway. Reconciliation breaks are not automatically "the custodian is wrong" or "we made a trading mistake" — the most common root cause in practice is an internal system simply failing to process a downstream event. This is precisely the class of bug an IT/reporting engineer gets pulled into: the money is fine, the data disagrees.

09 · The clock the regulator watches

Regulatory Reporting — The T+1 Deadline

Once a trade is executed, most jurisdictions require it to be reported to a regulator or Trade Repository within a strict deadline — commonly T+1, though some in-scope products/venues require same-day or near-real-time reporting.

Deadline Check · Two TradesT+1 = next business day EOD
TRADE 1 — Bond, BOT-2026-000901
  Executed          : Mon 17-Aug-2026, 09:03:12 UTC
  Reporting Deadline : Tue 18-Aug-2026, 23:59:59 UTC   (T+1)
  Reported            : Mon 17-Aug-2026, 18:40:00 UTC   -> WELL WITHIN DEADLINE
  Report includes     : Trade ID, ISIN, Quantity, Price, Counterparty LEI,
                        Trade Date/Time, Venue (OTC)

TRADE 2 — Cleared 5Y CAD IRS (Section 06)
  Executed            : Mon 17-Aug-2026, 14:12:05 UTC
  Reporting Deadline   : Tue 18-Aug-2026, 23:59:59 UTC   (T+1)
  Reported              : Tue 18-Aug-2026, 08:15:00 UTC   -> WITHIN DEADLINE
  Report includes       : UTI (Section 12, Module 2), UPI (Module 2 §12),
                          Notional, Fixed Rate, Counterparty LEI,
                          CCP LEI (post-novation), ISDA Taxonomy fields

MISSED DEADLINE EXAMPLE
  If Trade 2 wasn't reported until Wed 19-Aug-2026, 09:00:00 UTC,
  that's a LATE report — a regulatory breach in itself, independent
  of whether the trade settled/cleared correctly.
Takeaway. Regulatory reporting deadlines are measured from Execution Time (Section 02), not Trade Capture time or Settlement Date — a trade that settles perfectly two days later can still represent a reporting breach if the report itself missed its T+1 window on day one.

11 · Clearing adds a whole extra stage

End-to-End Timeline — One Cleared Interest Rate Swap

Same exercise, for the Section 06 IRS — notice Clearing inserts real extra steps (and an ongoing relationship) that the plain bond trade above never needed.

Full Lifecycle · 5Y CAD IRSCAD 100,000,000 notional
Mon 17-Aug-2026 (T)
  14:10:00   EXECUTION — Bank Of Toronto & Goldman Sachs agree      [Sec 02]
             terms bilaterally (fixed 4.00% vs floating CORRA)
  14:12:05   TRADE CAPTURE in both firms' systems                   [Sec 03]
  14:20:00   CONFIRMATION exchanged via FpML/MarkitWire              [Sec 04]
  14:45:00   AFFIRMATION received, MATCHED                          [Sec 05]
  15:00:00   Submitted to CCP (e.g. LCH) for clearing                [Sec 06]
  16:30:00   NOVATION completes — CCP is now legal counterparty
             to both sides. Two new trades exist (IRS #1, IRS #2).
  17:00:00   Initial Margin call: CAD 2,000,000 due from each side   [Sec 06]

Tue 18-Aug-2026 (T+1)
  09:00:00   Bank Of Toronto posts CAD 2,000,000 IM to the CCP
  08:15:00   Reported to Trade Repository — UTI + UPI included       [Sec 09]
             (within T+1 deadline)
  17:00:00   First Variation Margin call: CAD 180,000 moves from
             Goldman Sachs' account to Bank Of Toronto's account
             via the CCP (Section 06)

Every business day thereafter, for 5 years:
  - Daily VM exchanged based on the swap's mark-to-market movement
  - Quarterly net cash flow paid per Module 1 §8's mechanics
    (~CAD 187,192 per quarter in that worked example)

Total elapsed time, Execution to fully cleared + margined:
~27 hours — one extra full stage (Clearing) versus the bond trade,
plus an ongoing daily margin-exchange relationship that a plain
bond trade never has.
Takeaway. "Settlement" for a cleared derivative doesn't mean one T+2 event like a bond — it means novation, an initial margin call, and then a recurring daily/quarterly cash-flow relationship with the CCP for the life of the trade. Any report showing a derivative trade as "settled" on a single date is almost certainly using the word loosely; the more accurate status is "cleared," with ongoing margin activity continuing underneath it.

13 · The unhappy path, with real cost

When Things Go Wrong — A Settlement Fail, With Numbers

A settlement fail occurs when a trade's Settlement Date arrives and one leg cannot complete — most commonly because the seller doesn't actually hold enough of the security in custody. Because DvP mechanically prevents a partial failure, the whole settlement simply doesn't happen at all.

Settlement Fail · BOT-2026-000955CAD 1,000,000 short
Instrument  : Bank Of Toronto 4.5% Bond 2030
Quantity     : CAD 10,000,000 face value
Price        : 99.20
Counterparty : Goldman Sachs (buyer)
Settlement Date (T+2): Wed 19-Aug-2026

19-Aug-2026, 09:00: DvP instruction sent to the CDS.
19-Aug-2026, 09:00: FAILS — Bank Of Toronto's custody account only
                     holds CAD 9,000,000 face value of this bond,
                     CAD 1,000,000 short (a separate internal
                     position error, exactly the flavor of bug
                     described in Section 08).

Status: SETTLED -> FAILED

Trade re-attempted daily; actually settles 2 business days late,
on Fri 21-Aug-2026.

Under settlement-discipline regimes (e.g. CSDR in the EU), a late settlement generates a daily cash penalty, charged to the failing party, for every business day the fail persists:

Math · Illustrative Cash Penalty0.10 bps / day
Daily Penalty = Reference Price x Quantity x (Penalty Rate / 10,000)

Reference Price   = 99.20 (per 100 face value)
Quantity           = CAD 10,000,000 face value
Penalty Rate        = 0.10 bps = 0.0010%

Market Value used for the calc = 10,000,000 x 99.20 / 100
                                = CAD 9,920,000

Daily Penalty = 9,920,000 x 0.0010 / 100 = CAD 99.20 per day late

2 business days late (settled 21-Aug instead of 19-Aug):
  Total penalty = 99.20 x 2 = CAD 198.40

-> Charged to Bank Of Toronto, the failing (delivering) party.

The cash penalty here looks small, but the real cost of a fail is rarely the penalty itself — it's the downstream risk: if Goldman Sachs needed those bonds to deliver into a different trade of its own, Bank Of Toronto's fail can cascade into a second fail elsewhere in the market (a "fails chain"), and after a defined grace period, the buyer can exercise a buy-in — sourcing the securities elsewhere and charging the original seller any price difference plus costs.

Takeaway. A FAILED status in a settlement system is a direct, traceable consequence of a break upstream — very often the exact same category of internal position-keeping error described in Section 08's reconciliation break. Chasing "why did this trade fail?" usually leads straight back to "why did our books show more than we actually held?" — precisely why Reconciliation and Settlement are adjacent stages, not distant ones, in the lifecycle.

Self-check · Solutions

Recap Quiz — Answers & Explanations

Check your work against these before moving on.

Q1 — Landed in Maple Leaf Pension Fund's favor, by 0.05 (5 basis points) per CAD 100 face value

The fund's limit was "98.60 or better" (i.e., 98.60 or lower, since it's a buyer — lower price is better for a buyer). Bank Of Toronto executed at 98.55, which is 0.05 below the limit — the fund paid less than its worst-acceptable price, i.e., a better outcome for the buyer.

Q2 — Trade ID, Execution Time, Price, and Status (as NEW)

At Order time (09:02:00), only intent existed — "buy CAD 50,000,000 at 98.60 or better" — with no confirmed counterparty, no agreed price, and certainly no Trade ID yet, since a Trade ID is only assigned once an actual Execution has occurred and Trade Capture runs (Section 03).

Q3 — Most likely means the counterparty hasn't reviewed/responded yet — and Confirmation is the earlier stage

Looking at the sequence in Section 01: Confirmation → Matching, with Affirmation happening as part of the Confirmation exchange (Section 04) before the trade can even be submitted for the automated Matching step (Section 05). A six-hour gap between Confirmed and Affirmed means the counterparty simply hasn't formally agreed yet — it hasn't even reached the point where an automated field-by-field comparison (Matching) could run.

Q4 — No, it will break, not match

Matching (Section 05) is an exact field-by-field comparison. An ISIN mismatch — even if every other field (quantity, price, settlement date) is identical — is exactly the kind of single-field disagreement the "broken match" example in Section 05 showed with price. The trade would be held in BROKEN status until someone identifies that one side is using a stale/reissued identifier and corrects it.

Q5 — IRS #1: Bank Of Toronto vs the CCP. IRS #2 is CCP vs Goldman Sachs (no direct BOT exposure)

From Bank Of Toronto's perspective specifically, its only remaining counterparty after novation is the CCP — the original Goldman Sachs relationship on this trade is fully replaced (Section 06). Bank Of Toronto is not a party to IRS #2 at all; that trade exists purely between the CCP and Goldman Sachs.

Q6 — Reaches CLEARED, not SETTLED — followed by ongoing daily VM and periodic net cash flows

Unlike a bond, where one DvP event on T+2 fully discharges the trade (Section 07), a cleared swap has no notional to deliver (Module 1 §8) — there's nothing that constitutes a single "settlement" event. Instead, Section 11 shows the trade moving into an ongoing relationship with the CCP: margin calls the next day, and then a recurring cash-flow schedule for the full 5-year tenor.

Q7 — Most likely something else entirely — an internal processing/messaging error

This mirrors the Section 08 worked example exactly: when the custodian and the CSD (two independent, external sources) agree with each other but disagree with internal books, the far more likely explanation is that the firm's own internal position-keeping system failed to process a downstream event (e.g., a dropped settlement confirmation message) — not that money actually went missing, and not that the custodian's own records are wrong.

Q8 — Yes, within the T+1 deadline

Executed Monday 14:12:05 UTC means the T+1 deadline runs through the end of the next business day — Tuesday 23:59:59 UTC (Section 09). The actual report at Tuesday 08:15:00 UTC comfortably beat that cutoff. A UTC timestamp any time on or before Tuesday 18-Aug-2026, 23:59:59 UTC would still meet it; anything from Wednesday 19-Aug-2026, 00:00:00 UTC onward would be a missed deadline.

Q9 — CAD 99.20
Math · One Day LateCAD 10,000,000 face
Market Value = 10,000,000 x 99.20 / 100 = CAD 9,920,000
Daily Penalty = 9,920,000 x 0.0010 / 100 = CAD 99.20 per day
1 day late    = CAD 99.20 x 1 = CAD 99.20

The daily penalty rate itself doesn't change with how many days a trade is late — only the total scales with days late. One day late is exactly one day's penalty, CAD 99.20 — precisely half the CAD 198.40 total shown in Section 13 for two days late.

Q10 — NEW → AFFIRMED → MATCHED → SETTLED → RECONCILED
This follows the Section 01 stage order directly: Trade Capture sets NEW (Sec 03); Confirmation produces AFFIRMED once the counterparty agrees (Sec 04); Matching then confirms both independently-keyed records agree, MATCHED (Sec 05); Settlement moves it to SETTLED on T+2 (Sec 07); and the end-of-day tie-out finally lands it at RECONCILED (Sec 08) — exactly the sequence walked through in Section 10's end-to-end timeline.
