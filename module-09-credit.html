01 · Trading default risk itself

What Is Credit? — Theory & Terminology

Credit is about trading default risk itself — the risk that a specific borrower fails to pay what it owes — separately from owning that borrower's actual debt. The core product is the Credit Default Swap (CDS) — economically closest to insurance: one party pays a periodic premium, the other pays out if a defined bad event happens.

TermMeaning
Reference EntityThe specific company or sovereign whose default risk the CDS references
Reference ObligationA specific bond issued by the Reference Entity, used to define what counts as "the debt" for settlement purposes
Credit EventA defined trigger (default, bankruptcy, or restructuring) that causes the CDS to pay out
Running CouponThe periodic premium the protection buyer pays — standardized since 2009's ISDA "Big Bang" Protocol to fixed levels (typically 100bps or 500bps)
Upfront PaymentA one-time payment, either direction, reconciling the market's actual credit spread vs. the standardized Running Coupon (Section 04)
Recovery RateThe fraction of face value creditors are expected to recover after a default — directly determines how much a CDS pays out
Diagram · CDS as InsuranceCore mental model
Protection BUYER (Bank Of Toronto):
  Pays a periodic Running Coupon (like an insurance premium)
  -> in exchange for protection against the Reference Entity
     defaulting

Protection SELLER (Goldman Sachs):
  Receives the Running Coupon
  -> pays out a large amount ONLY IF a Credit Event happens
     (like an insurer paying a claim)

-> Critically: Bank Of Toronto does NOT need to own any bond
   issued by the Reference Entity to buy this protection -- a
   "naked" CDS position is possible, unlike home insurance,
   where you generally must own the home to insure it.
Takeaway. The insurance analogy has one famous, important limit — you can buy CDS protection on a company's debt without owning any of that debt, a structural difference from real insurance that made CDS central to the 2008 financial crisis (large protection sellers, like AIG, owed far more in potential payouts than they held in reserves) and is exactly why Section 08's regulatory response was so significant.

Unlike every other module in the roadmap, the taxonomy doesn't split these into separate schema nodes.

ProductWhat it is

03 · The second product referencing a real security without being one

What Exactly Is Being Traded? — A Pure Contract, With One Familiar Exception

A CDS is a contract, not a security — the same "contracts all the way down" pattern Module 6 §3 established for FX. But exactly like Module 8 §3's IR Forward Debt, a CDS's Reference Obligation is a real bond, with a real ISIN, that the contract's settlement mechanics point back to.

Diagram · CDS vs Reference ObligationContract vs the security it points to
Credit Default Swap (CONTRACT):
  Bank Of Toronto and Goldman Sachs create this contract at trade
  date. It has no existence before the trade, and (barring a
  credit event) ceases to exist at its maturity date. Nothing
  about the Reference Entity's actual debt changes hands.

Reference Obligation (SECURITY):
  A specific bond issued by the Reference Entity -- e.g. a
  Northstar Resources Inc. 6% Bond 2031 -- named in the CDS
  contract purely to define what "the debt" means for settlement
  (Section 05's credit-event auction references THIS obligation's
  market price, post-default, to determine the payout).

-> The CDS itself never delivers this bond in modern practice
   (Section 05's cash-settlement-via-auction convention replaced
   the older physical-settlement norm after the 2009 Big Bang
   Protocol) -- but the bond's ISIN still appears on the CDS
   trade record as a reference field (Section 06).
Takeaway. This is the second product family in the roadmap (after Module 8's IR Forward Debt) where a genuinely "contract, not security" product legitimately carries a real ISIN on its trade record — for the same reason in both cases: the contract's settlement mechanics are defined in terms of a specific, real security, even though the contract itself is what's actually traded.

04 · Buyer → Seller → Trade

Real-World Trade Examples

One trade, two moments — inception, and the credit event that ends it early.

4a. Single-Name CDS — Trade Inception

5Y Protection on Northstar Resources Inc.Single-Name CDS
Protection Buyer:  Bank Of Toronto
Protection Seller: Goldman Sachs

Reference Entity: Northstar Resources Inc. (fictional corporate)
Reference Obligation: Northstar Resources Inc. 6% Bond 2031
Notional: USD 10,000,000
Term: 5 years
Standardized Running Coupon: 100bps (1.00%), paid quarterly
Market-quoted credit spread today: 250bps (2.50%)

Because the market spread (2.50%) is well above the standardized
coupon (1.00%), Bank Of Toronto must pay an UPFRONT amount to
Goldman Sachs to compensate for the below-market running coupon
it'll actually receive going forward (a full ISDA CDS Standard
Model calculation is beyond this module's scope; the commonly
used approximation is shown for intuition):

Approx. Upfront = (Market Spread - Running Coupon) x Risky Duration x Notional
                 = (2.50% - 1.00%) x ~4.5 x 10,000,000
                 = 1.50% x 4.5 x 10,000,000
                 = USD 675,000 (paid by Bank Of Toronto at inception)

Quarterly Running Coupon payment thereafter:
  10,000,000 x 1.00% x 91/360 (CDS convention is act/360, unlike
  the act/365 convention Module 8 used for CORRA) = USD 2,527.78
Schema: CreditDefaultSwapTrade.avsc / CreditDefaultSwapValuation.avsc.

4b. Single-Name CDS — Credit Event, Two Years Later

Credit Event · Northstar Resources Inc. DefaultsCash settlement via auction
Trigger: Northstar Resources Inc. misses a bond coupon payment
and files for bankruptcy protection

ISDA Determinations Committee (Section 08) formally rules a
"Credit Event" has occurred -- this is a genuinely different
kind of body from any regulator in this roadmap so far: an
INDUSTRY body, not a government regulator, that decides the
single most consequential fact in this whole trade's lifecycle

CREDIT EVENT AUCTION determines the Reference Obligation's
post-default market price: settles at 35 (i.e. 35% of face value
-- bondholders expected to recover roughly 35 cents on the dollar)

Cash Settlement (the modern, standard mechanic post-2009 Big
Bang Protocol -- NOT physical delivery of the defaulted bond):
  Payout = Notional x (100% - Recovery Rate)
         = 10,000,000 x (100% - 35%)
         = 10,000,000 x 65%
         = USD 6,500,000

Goldman Sachs (protection seller) PAYS Bank Of Toronto (protection
buyer) USD 6,500,000 -- the CDS's entire purpose realized: Bank Of
Toronto is made economically whole for a USD 10,000,000 exposure
to Northstar Resources Inc. it may never have even held as a bond,
receiving 65% of face value in cash rather than a defaulted bond
worth only 35 cents on the dollar.
Takeaway. The credit-event auction mechanic is Credit's single most distinctive lifecycle event — nothing in Equity, Fixed Income, FX, Commodities, or Rates has an equivalent "an industry committee formally declares a defined bad thing happened, then an auction sets the payout price" sequence. A Credit Event is where Credit genuinely diverges from every other asset class in this roadmap.

05 · The first product an external event can end early

Trade Lifecycle — Credit-Specific Walkthrough

Order → Execution → Trade Capture → Confirmation → Clearing → Settlement → Position → Valuation/P&L → Reporting, applied to the CDS from Section 04a, through to the Credit Event in Section 04b.

Full Lifecycle · 5Y CDS on Northstar Resources Inc.BOT-CDS-2026-041127
YEAR 0
10:40:00  ORDER — Bank Of Toronto's credit desk instructed to buy
          5Y protection, USD 10,000,000, Northstar Resources Inc.

10:40:15  EXECUTION @ 250bps market spread vs Goldman Sachs

10:40:15  TRADE CAPTURE — Trade ID BOT-CDS-2026-041127
          Upfront payment calculated: USD 675,000 (Section 04a)

10:55:00  CONFIRMATION/MATCHING -> MATCHED

CLEARING — for a standardized single-name or index CDS, novated
          to a CCP (ICE Clear Credit or LCH CDSClear) under a
          clearing mandate that parallels EMIR/Dodd-Frank's
          Rates mandate (Module 8 §07-08) but scoped specifically
          to CDS index products and the most liquid single names

QUARTERLY RUNNING COUPON — USD 2,527.78 paid by Bank Of Toronto
          to Goldman Sachs each quarter, for as long as the trade
          survives without a Credit Event (Section 04a)

YEAR 2
          CREDIT EVENT — Northstar Resources Inc. defaults
          (Section 04b)

          ISDA DETERMINATIONS COMMITTEE rules the event is a
          valid Credit Event under the contract's terms

          CREDIT EVENT AUCTION sets the Reference Obligation's
          settlement price at 35

SETTLEMENT — Goldman Sachs pays Bank Of Toronto USD 6,500,000
          cash (Section 04b); the CDS contract TERMINATES --
          unlike an IRS (Module 8 §5), which runs its full term
          regardless of what happens to interest rates, a CDS
          can end early, permanently, the moment its one defined
          trigger event occurs

REPORTING — trade reported to a Trade Repository (DTCC GTR)
          throughout its life, with a final settlement/
          termination event reported once the Credit Event
          auction concludes
Takeaway. Every other product in this roadmap either runs to its stated maturity or is closed out/rolled voluntarily (Module 7 §4b's futures roll). A CDS is the first product where an external event outside either counterparty's control can end the trade early — the Reference Entity's own solvency, not a market price move, is what terminates it.

06 · LEI plays two roles on the same record

Identifiers — UPI, UTI, LEI (for TWO different roles), and a Reference Obligation ISIN

CDS reuses the familiar OTC derivative identifier set, but with a genuine twist: LEI shows up in two structurally different roles on the same trade record.

IdentifierApplies to CDS?Example
ISINNo, for the CDS contract itself — but yes, for the Reference Obligation it points to (Section 03)— for the CDS; a real ISIN for the Northstar Resources Inc. 6% Bond 2031
UPIYes, as an OTC derivative product template (Module 2 §12)One UPI for "5Y USD Single-Name CDS, Northstar Resources Inc."
UTIYes — every individual CDS trade gets its ownA fresh UTI per trade
LEI — Counterparty roleYes — identifies Bank Of Toronto and Goldman Sachs as the two trading partiesSame role LEI plays in every prior module (Module 4 §6)
LEI — Reference Entity roleYes, and this is new: Northstar Resources Inc. also carries its own LEI, a completely separate field from the counterparty LEIsIdentifies WHAT the contract references, not WHO is trading it
Diagram · LEI's Two RolesOne CDS trade record
Counterparty LEI (Buyer)  : identifies Bank Of Toronto
Counterparty LEI (Seller) : identifies Goldman Sachs
Reference Entity LEI      : identifies Northstar Resources Inc.

-> every prior module's LEI only ever answered "WHO is trading
   this?" (Module 4 §6). Credit is the first product where LEI
   ALSO answers "WHAT is the contract about?" -- a role every
   other module gave to an instrument identifier (ISIN, Ticker,
   currency pair) instead.
Takeaway. A CDS trade record with three populated LEI fields and one populated ISIN field (for the Reference Obligation) isn't over-populated or wrong — it's the fullest expression yet in this roadmap of how many distinct "who" and "what" questions one OTC derivative trade record needs to answer at once.

07 · The same pattern, a third time

Exchange vs OTC vs Bilateral vs Centrally Cleared

Credit follows the exact structural pattern Module 8 §7 established for Rates — OTC-negotiated by default, with the most standardized instruments pulled into mandatory central clearing.

ProductExchange or OTC?Bilateral or Centrally Cleared?
CDS Index (CDX, iTraxx)OTC-negotiatedMandatorily centrally cleared (ICE Clear Credit, LCH CDSClear) for the most liquid indices, under an EMIR/Dodd-Frank-style clearing mandate
Single-Name CDS (liquid, standardized names)OTC-negotiatedIncreasingly centrally cleared
Single-Name CDS (bespoke/illiquid Reference Entity)OTC-negotiatedBilateral (the norm) — the Northstar Resources Inc. trade in Section 04 is written as this case
Takeaway. This is the third module in a row (after Rates, Module 8 §7, and echoing Commodities, Module 7 §7) where the same underlying pattern repeats — one standardized slice of the asset class carries the central-clearing volume, while a long tail of bespoke, illiquid instruments stays bilateral OTC. By this point, that pattern itself is worth recognizing as a general truth about OTC derivatives markets, not a coincidence specific to any one asset class.

08 · A split US jurisdiction and an industry body

Regulatory Perspective — MiFID, EMIR, SFTR, FCA, CIRO, HKMA, DTCC, DMO

Two genuinely new regulatory concepts appear for the first time in this roadmap's final module.

Regulation / BodyHow it touches Credit specifically
MiFID II / MiFIR (EU)Trading-venue and transparency rules for standardized CDS, similar in shape to its Rates treatment (Module 8 §08)
EMIRMandates central clearing for the most liquid CDS Indices specifically (e.g. iTraxx Europe Main and Crossover) — a narrower clearing mandate than Rates' broader IRS mandate (Module 8 §08), reflecting how much less standardized most single-name CDS remain
SFTRNot applicable — a CDS is a derivative, not a securities financing transaction
FCA (UK)UK conduct regulator for CDS trading and clearing activity
CIRO (Canada)SRO overseeing Canadian credit derivatives dealer conduct
HKMA (Hong Kong)Runs Hong Kong's OTC derivatives reporting regime for CDS alongside Rates (Module 8 §08), under the same Securities and Futures Ordinance framework
CFTC / SEC (US) — new to this moduleThe first split jurisdiction in this roadmap: broad-based CDS Indices are regulated by the CFTC as "swaps," while single-name CDS and narrow-based indices are "security-based swaps," regulated instead by the SEC — a genuine US jurisdictional fork this roadmap hasn't seen in any other asset class
ISDA Determinations Committees — new to this moduleNot a government regulator at all, but an industry self-governance body (Section 04b, Section 05) whose ruling on whether a Credit Event occurred is the single most consequential decision in a CDS's entire lifecycle
DTCCIts GTR is a major trade repository for CDS reporting, same infrastructure role as Module 8 §08
DMO (UK Debt Management Office)Not Credit-relevant in the way it was for Rates (IR Forward Debt on Gilts, Module 8 §08) — sovereign CDS exists but isn't this module's worked example; included here for completeness
Takeaway. A split CFTC/SEC jurisdiction and an industry body (ISDA's Determinations Committees) making the single most consequential call in the entire trade lifecycle both exist specifically because of what makes Credit different from every prior asset class: a contract whose entire purpose is to trigger on a discrete, disputable event rather than settling against a continuously observable market price.

Self-check · Solutions

Recap Quiz — Answers & Explanations

Check your work against these before moving on.

Q1 — Bank Of Toronto would RECEIVE the upfront amount, roughly USD 180,000
Math · Spread Below CouponCDS Upfront
Approx. Upfront = (Market Spread - Running Coupon) x Risky Duration x Notional
                 = (0.60% - 1.00%) x 4.5 x 10,000,000
                 = -0.40% x 4.5 x 10,000,000
                 = -USD 180,000 (negative = Bank Of Toronto RECEIVES)

When the market spread is BELOW the standardized Running Coupon, Bank Of Toronto would be overpaying relative to the market's actual pricing of Northstar Resources Inc.'s credit risk every quarter — so Goldman Sachs compensates Bank Of Toronto upfront for locking into that above-market coupon, the mirror image of Section 04a's worked example.

Q2 — A Credit Event Auction determines the Reference Obligation's actual post-default market price; bondholders typically still recover something, so the payout is calibrated to the ACTUAL loss, not an assumed total loss

Section 04b's payout formula, Notional x (100% - Recovery Rate), only reduces to a full notional payout in the extreme case of a 0% Recovery Rate — the auction mechanism exists specifically to discover what recovery rate applies to this particular default, rather than assuming the worst case by default.

Q3 — No, it's not correct — the CDS contract itself has no ISIN, but the Reference Obligation it names legitimately carries its own ISIN as a reference field on the CDS trade record

This is exactly the same nuance Module 8 §6 established for IR Forward Debt: "no ISIN for the contract itself, but yes for the real security the contract's mechanics point to" is a recurring, healthy pattern for contracts that reference — without necessarily delivering — an actual security, not a universal "derivatives never have ISINs" rule.

Q4 — That would represent Goldman Sachs having sold protection on ITS OWN default risk — a real (if unusual and risky) scenario, not automatically a data error

Section 06's two-roles explanation makes clear that Counterparty LEI and Reference Entity LEI are populated independently and can, in principle, coincide — the systems don't structurally prevent it, even though a risk or compliance function would likely flag it given the concentrated risk it represents ("wrong-way risk," previewed for Module 11: Risk & Exposure): if Goldman Sachs itself defaults, it's simultaneously unable to pay out AND the very trigger event.

Q5 — Every other row names a government-affiliated regulator operating within a defined jurisdiction; the ISDA Determinations Committee is an industry body whose authority comes from the CDS contract's own documentation, not from statute
Section 08's takeaway flags this explicitly as one of two genuinely new regulatory concepts in this module — a private, contractually-designated decision-making body sitting alongside (not replacing) the actual government regulators in the same table.
Q6 — Dodd-Frank drew a legal line between "swaps" (CFTC) and "security-based swaps" (SEC); single-name/narrow CDS reference an individual company's debt securities, triggering SEC jurisdiction, while a vanilla IRS references a pure rate benchmark with no securities-law hook

Section 08's table states this split directly, contrasting it with Module 8 §08's clean CFTC-only picture for Rates — the deciding factor is whether the underlying reference is a security-linked concept (triggering SEC jurisdiction) or a pure rate/benchmark (staying with the CFTC alone).

Q7 — The same shape has now appeared independently in three asset classes, each reached via a different specific mechanism

Commodities' Futures (Module 7 §07, venue design), Rates' IRS (Module 8 §07, regulatory mandate), and Credit's CDS Indices/liquid names (this module's Section 07, a narrower regulatory mandate) all show the same structural pattern. Section 07's takeaway draws this conclusion explicitly: by the third independent occurrence across genuinely different products and causal mechanisms, it stops looking like asset-class-specific detail and starts looking like a general truth about how OTC derivatives markets evolve toward partial standardization.
