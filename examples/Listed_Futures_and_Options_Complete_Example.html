01

What Are Listed Derivatives?

A Listed Derivative is a Future or Option that trades on a regulated exchange, with standardized contract terms, and clears through that exchange's own CCP from the instant of execution. Unlike every OTC derivative in this series (IRS, FX Forward, CDS), nothing here is bilaterally negotiated — two traders executing the same contract an hour apart get identical terms, down to the tick.

Listed Derivative ↔ Standardized contract, exchange-traded, always centrally cleared

The important point is:

  • There is no UPI and no UTI — a Listed Derivative's fungibility makes per-trade bilateral identifiers meaningless. The exchange's own contract code and trade/clearing IDs serve that role instead.
  • Clearing isn't a separate lifecycle stage that happens later — it happens at execution, automatically, every single time.

Worked Example 1 — Interest Rate Future (Hedging, Not Speculating)

Suppose Bank Of Toronto owns a large book of long-dated Canadian government bonds (Fixed Income example) and wants to hedge against rates rising (bond prices falling).

Trade terms

TermValue
ProductCME 10-Year Treasury Note Future ("ZN")
Contract SizeUSD 100,000 notional 6% coupon 10-Year T-Note (a standardized fiction)
ActionSELL 100 contracts
Price112'16 (112 + 16/32 = 112.500)
Notional100 × 100,000 × 112.500/100 = USD 11,250,000
Trade Date05-Oct-2026
SellerBank Of Toronto
BuyerAnonymous counterparty on the CME order book
Bank Of Toronto SELLS Futures
        ↓
This is a HEDGE, not speculation — it already owns the underlying
bonds and wants to offset a potential price fall (rates rising)

Next business day — rates rise, bond prices fall

Price falls from 112.500 to 111.750 (111'24)

Variation Margin = (112.500 − 111.750)/100 × 100,000 × 100 contracts
                  = 0.0075 × 100,000 × 100
                  = USD 75,000

Because Bank Of Toronto is SHORT the future, a price fall is a gain — USD 75,000 is credited to its margin account, directly offsetting the loss on the actual bond portfolio the future is hedging.

Bond portfolio value  ↓  (loses money as rates rise)
Short Future position ↑  (gains money as rates rise)
------------------------------------------------
              Net P&L ≈ flat — the hedge is working

Worked Example 2 — Listed Equity Option

Suppose Maple Leaf Pension Fund wants upside exposure to Apple Inc. shares, with strictly limited downside.

Trade terms

TermValue
ProductListed Equity Call Option
UnderlierAAPL — Apple Inc. common stock
Expiry15-Jan-2027
StrikeUSD 250.00
Contracts100 (each covering 100 shares = 10,000 shares total)
PremiumUSD 185,000 (paid upfront)
BuyerMaple Leaf Pension Fund

Scenario A — AAPL rallies to USD 280 by expiry

Intrinsic Value = (280.00 − 250.00) × 10,000 shares
                = 30.00 × 10,000
                = USD 300,000

Net gain = 300,000 − 185,000 (premium paid) = USD 115,000

Scenario B — AAPL falls to USD 230 by expiry

Option expires WORTHLESS (strike above market price)
Maple Leaf Pension Fund loses the full USD 185,000 premium — and
nothing more. This is the defining feature of being long an option:
the maximum loss is fixed and known upfront; the maximum gain is
theoretically unlimited.
Buyer's P&L
    +  |                         /
       |                       /
       |                     /
  -----+-------------------+------------------  Market Price at expiry
       |. . . . . . . . . /  Strike Price (250)
    -  |________________/
       (flat — capped at the USD 185,000 premium, no matter how
        far the market moves against the position)

04

Why Both Examples Follow the Same Mechanic

Equity Index Future    -> E-mini S&P 500 Future
Commodity Future       -> CME WTI Crude Future
Interest Rate Future   -> CME 10-Year T-Note Future  (Section 2, above)
FX Future              -> CME EUR/USD Future
Listed Option          -> AAPL Call                  (Section 3, above)

Five different underlyings, but only two contract mechanics across all of them — this is why Listed Derivatives gets pulled out as its own cross-cutting node rather than being described six separate times inside each asset class module (Module 15 §2).

The Trade Lifecycle — Instantaneous Clearing

Order
  ↓
Execution  ═══════▶  Novation to CCP happens IMMEDIATELY, same instant
  ↓
Trade Capture
  ↓
Daily Variation Margin (every day, for the life of the position)
  ↓
[Expiry: cash settlement, physical delivery, or exercise]
  ↓
Regulatory Reporting (Position Reporting, not EMIR/Dodd-Frank trade reporting)

Compare this to a bilateral OTC derivative's Clearing stage (Section 6 of the IRS example), which happens as a discrete, later step, sometimes optionally — here, it isn't optional and it isn't later.

06

What Actually Delivers at Expiry — Three Different Answers

E-mini S&P 500 Future at expiry:
  → CASH-SETTLED. No shares ever move; only the final price
    difference is paid.

WTI Crude Future at expiry:
  → PHYSICAL DELIVERY of one specific thing (barrels of WTI at
    Cushing, Oklahoma), if not closed out or rolled first.

10-Year T-Note Future at expiry:
  → PHYSICAL DELIVERY, but the SHORT chooses which eligible bond,
    from a defined basket, to deliver — a genuinely different
    "physical settlement" than either of the above.

"Physically settled" doesn't mean the same thing across every Future — a reporting or settlement system that assumes it always means "one predetermined instrument moves" will handle a bond future's delivery notice incorrectly (Module 15 §5).

07

Clearing — Always, No Exceptions

ProductExchange or OTC?Bilateral or Centrally Cleared?
Futures (any underlying)Always exchange-tradedAlways centrally cleared, from execution
Listed Options (any underlying)Always exchange-tradedAlways centrally cleared (OCC for US equity options, CME Clearing for futures options)

Every prior product in this series had a genuine two-axis question ("is it OTC or exchange, bilateral or cleared?") with a real answer that varied by product. Listed Derivatives is the one node where the answer is always the same cell — by definition (Module 15 §7).

08

Collateral — CCP Margin, Not a Bilateral CSA

Product CategoryIM Required?VM Required?
Exchange-traded Futures / Listed OptionsYes (CCP's own model)Yes, daily

There is no CSA to negotiate here — the CCP (CME Clearing for the T-Note future, OCC for the AAPL option) simply sets its own Initial Margin and calls Variation Margin daily, mechanically, the same way for every participant.

09

Identifiers — The Reverse Diagnostic

IdentifierApplies to Listed Derivatives?
ISIN / CUSIP / TickerNo — not securities
Exchange Contract CodeYes — e.g. ZNZ26 for the T-Note future, an OCC option symbol for the AAPL call
UPI / UTINo — these identify bilaterally negotiated OTC derivatives; fungibility makes per-trade bilateral identifiers meaningless here
LEIYes — identifies Bank Of Toronto and Maple Leaf Pension Fund as counterparties

A Listed Derivative trade record with an exchange contract code but no UPI/UTI is exactly correct — the reverse of the diagnostic OTC records carry. Seeing a UPI/UTI populated on an otherwise clearly listed Futures/Options record would itself be the anomaly (Module 15 §6).

10

Regulatory Reporting

Listed Derivatives fall under Position Reporting regimes (exchange/regulator-driven, concentration-risk focused) rather than the EMIR/Dodd-Frank Trade Repository reporting every OTC product in this series requires:

RegulationApplies?
EMIR / Dodd-Frank (Trade Repository reporting)No — not an OTC derivative
Position Reporting (exchange/regulator)Yes — e.g. large-position reporting thresholds
MiFID II / MiFIRTransaction reporting still applies, since execution happened on a regulated venue

12

The Three Most Important Listed Derivatives Concepts

1. Standardization is a venue property, not an asset-class property

The same Future/Option mechanic repeats identically across equity, commodity, rates, and FX underliers.

2. Clearing happens at execution, not later

OTC:    Trade → (sometimes) → Clearing, as a separate stage
Listed: Trade = Clearing, the same instant

3. "Physical settlement" means different things for different Futures

An index future never delivers anything; a commodity future delivers one specific thing; a bond future delivers the short's choice from a menu.

13

Mental Model Recap

Need for standardized, fungible, exchange-cleared exposure → Listed Derivative → Instant novation, daily margin, no bilateral negotiation

Where every OTC example in this series (IRS, FX Forward, Commodity Swap, CDS, Equity TRS) is built around two counterparties negotiating bespoke terms, a Listed Derivative trades the ability to customize terms for the ability to transact instantly, anonymously, and with the CCP's credit standing behind every trade from the very first second.
