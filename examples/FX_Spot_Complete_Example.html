01

What is FX Spot?

FX Spot is an agreement to exchange two currencies almost immediately — the "cash" end of the FX product family. Unlike an FX Forward or FX Swap (see the FX Forward example), there's no locked-in future rate and no derivative economics — you agree on a rate today, and the currencies actually move within a day or two.

FX Spot ↔ Immediate exchange of two real currency amounts

The important point is:

  • FX Spot has no ISIN — currencies aren't securities.
  • FX Spot has no UPI either — it isn't a derivative.
  • Its only "identifier" is the currency pair itself (ISO 4217 codes).

Simple Example

Suppose:

  • Bank Of Toronto needs USD 10,000,000 for an upcoming payment obligation, but only holds CAD.
  • It buys USD against CAD in the spot market from Goldman Sachs.

03

A Deliberate Wrinkle: USD/CAD Is T+1, Not T+2

Most currency pairs settle T+2, but USD/CAD is a well-known exception, settling T+1 (Module 1 §6). So the actual Value Date here is:

Trade Date (T)  : Monday,   17 August 2026
Value Date (T+1): Tuesday,  18 August 2026

A reporting system that hard-codes "FX = T+2" will get this specific pair wrong — exactly the kind of assumption systems-landscape §7 warns against.

06

Settlement — PvP via CLS

Settling two different currencies simultaneously across two different countries' payment systems is genuinely hard — CLS (Continuous Linked Settlement) exists to solve exactly this:

Bank Of Toronto                                Goldman Sachs
     |                                              |
     |  pays CAD 13,400,000 into CLS                |
     |                                              |  pays USD 10,000,000 into CLS
     |                                              |
              CLS releases BOTH legs simultaneously,
              or releases NEITHER — Payment-versus-Payment (PvP)
     |                                              |
     |<---------------- USD 10,000,000 -------------|
     |----------------- CAD 13,400,000 ------------>|

PvP for FX is the direct analogue of DvP for securities (Module 6 §5) — it eliminates settlement risk (also called Herstatt risk), not counterparty credit risk in the CCP-novation sense.

Position

Notice: unlike an IRS's notional, the full USD 10,000,000 genuinely moved — this is a cash instrument, not a reference amount.

08

Daily Revaluation

Suppose one week later, the USD/CAD spot rate has moved to 1.3450.

Current CAD value of the USD 10,000,000 position:
  = 10,000,000 × 1.3450
  = CAD 13,450,000

Original CAD cost:
  = CAD 13,400,000

Unrealized gain (on the USD leg, in CAD terms) = CAD 50,000

Module 10 §2 classifies FX Spot as Quote-Based valuation — same simple family as a cash equity or bond, since the rate is directly observable in the market with no model needed.

09

Why FX Spot Falls Outside Derivative Reporting Regimes

RegulationApplies to FX Spot?Why
EMIRNoFX Spot is explicitly carved out — the Spot/Forward boundary is a settlement-date threshold, roughly the standard spot cycle for that pair (Module 6 §8)
MiFID II / MiFIRMostly NoPhysically-settled Spot used for genuine currency exchange sits outside MiFID's investment-product scope
SFTRNoNot a financing transaction

Once the settlement date pushes beyond that standard spot window, the same currency pair becomes an FX Forward instead — and suddenly is in scope for EMIR, needs a UPI, and gets a UTI. This is why the Spot/Forward boundary matters so much operationally: it isn't about the product "feeling" different, it's a pure settlement-date threshold.

The Three Most Important FX Spot Concepts

1. Notional genuinely settles

Unlike an IRS, the full USD 10,000,000 physically moves.

2. PvP, not DvP

Currency A  ────┐
                ├──── simultaneous, via CLS
Currency B  ────┘

3. It's the boundary case for derivative regulation

Settlement within the standard spot window  → FX Spot   → Out of EMIR scope
Settlement beyond that window                → FX Forward → In EMIR scope

13

Mental Model Recap

Need for a foreign currency, right now → FX Spot → Immediate exchange, no forward risk, no derivative machinery

FX Spot is the simplest possible FX trade — everything more complex in the FX product family (Forward, Swap, Option — see Module 6) is built by adding a future date, an option feature, or by combining two Spot/Forward legs together.
